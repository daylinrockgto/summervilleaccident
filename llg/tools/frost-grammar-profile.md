# Frost Law Group, County Research Pack

Built September 15, 2026. Covers the eight approved South Carolina counties on summervilleaccidentattorney.com.

Every fact below was verified against a primary source and carries its citation. Anything that could not be verified says so. Nothing here was inferred, estimated, or filled from general knowledge.

## How to Use This

The project rule is absolute. Do not invent intersections, crash statistics, hospital names, courthouse names, or population figures. Research and cite, or leave it out.

**If a fact is not in this pack (or the verified addendum), do not write it.**

**Read section 5 before writing any county page.** It lists what makes each county genuinely different from the other seven. Location pages built from a shared template will read as doorway pages and will be treated that way.

**Read section 6 before writing anything.** It lists the facts that look right and are wrong.

All collision figures are calendar year 2024 from the South Carolina Department of Public Safety Traffic Collision Fact Book, 2024 Edition, which is the most recent published. No 2025 edition exists yet. All population figures are Census Bureau Vintage 2025 estimates as of July 1, 2025.

## 5. What Makes Each County Page Different

**Dorchester.** The home county and the only one where the firm's own address sits. The courthouse is in St. George, not Summerville, and Family Court is at the Troy Knight Judicial Complex on Deming Way. SC-642, which is Dorchester Road, is the single worst road in the county at 519 collisions. Failed to Yield Right of Way is the top factor by a wide margin. This page should read as local knowledge, not as a service description.

**Charleston.** Volume and severity. First in the state for serious injury collisions with 241, and first by a distance. US-17 alone carries 1,805. Four of the five worst intersections are on I-26. Distracted or Inattention is the second highest contributing factor at 2,965, a pattern that does not appear this high anywhere else in the footprint. Two trauma centers in county.

**Berkeley.** Growth. The population went from 229,861 to 274,666 in five years, roughly 19 percent, and the crash pattern follows it. Driving Too Fast for Conditions is the top factor, ahead of failure to yield, which is the reverse of Dorchester and Charleston. Lowest death rate in the footprint at 13.9 per 100,000. Ninth Circuit with Charleston, so the court story is different from Dorchester's.

**Colleton.** The I-95 county. All five worst intersections are on I-95, 29 percent of all collisions happen there, and Ran Off Road is the top contributing factor. The death rate of 43.1 per 100,000 is the highest in the footprint and more than three times Berkeley's. Low volume, high lethality.

**Orangeburg.** Lethality out of proportion to size. 8th of 46 counties for fatal collisions on 83,177 people. Charleston has more than five times the population and only 57 fatal collisions to Orangeburg's 38. It carries both I-26 and I-95. It has its own Level III trauma center. It shares the First Judicial Circuit with Dorchester.

**Beaufort.** No interstate at all. The crash picture is US-278, US-21, US-17, SC-170, and named local corridors like Robert Smalls Parkway and William Hilton Parkway. Three hospitals and zero trauma centers, so serious injuries leave the county. Lowest VMT death rate in the footprint at 1.611. Resort and island geography, with Hilton Head, Daufuskie, Fripp, and Harbor Islands.

**Georgetown.** A single road county. US-17 accounts for 629 of 1,484 collisions, 42 percent, and there is no interstate anywhere. It has the only Level IV trauma center in the footprint at Tidelands Waccamaw. Nearest Level I is north in Myrtle Beach, not south in Charleston.

**Williamsburg.** Rural and shrinking. The only county in the footprint losing population. No interstate, no CDPs, and SC-261 rather than a US route is the worst road. Animal In Road is the third highest contributing factor at 124. The county hospital closed in January 2023 and the replacement sits in Cades, not the county seat. Fatalities are trending up against 2023.

## 6. Traps

**Roper St. Francis Berkeley Hospital has a Summerville address but is in Berkeley County.** 100 Callen Boulevard, Summerville, SC 29486. Do not list it as a Dorchester County hospital.

**Dorchester County's seat is St. George, not Summerville.** Moncks Corner is Berkeley's seat. Do not write that the Dorchester County Courthouse is in Summerville. Only Family Court sits in Summerville.

**Beaufort County has no interstate.** Neither does Georgetown or Williamsburg.

**The Georgia constraint bites in Beaufort.** Census road data returns a road literally named Savannah Hwy inside Beaufort County. It has been excluded. Do not reintroduce it. Write the South Carolina trauma answer, which is MUSC in Charleston.

**Williamsburg Regional Hospital in Kingstree is closed.** It shut in January 2023. Any copy that puts a hospital in Kingstree is wrong. The operating facility is MUSC Health Black River Medical Center in Cades.

**Berkeley County's US-17 figure is unreliable.** SCDOT's route inventory shows no plain US 17 in Berkeley County, only US 17A. Do not write that US 17 is the deadliest road in Berkeley County. The same caution applies to the US-17 entry in the Dorchester table, where TIGER records the route as US 17A.

**Murrells Inlet straddles the Georgetown and Horry county line.** Tidelands Waccamaw at 4070 Highway 17 South is in Georgetown County.

**Andrews straddles the Georgetown and Williamsburg line.** It belongs to both pages and should be written differently on each.

**Preliminary fatality counts are not annual figures.** Do not mix SCDPS year to date pages with Fact Book annual figures.

**There is no 2025 Fact Book.** The 2024 Edition is the most recent. Date every collision figure as 2024.

**Knightsville and Ladson are unincorporated.** Never call either a town or city.

# R1. South Carolina Auto, Insurance, Rideshare, DUI and Crash-Report Law

Verified 2026-09-28 against primary sources only (S.C. Code of Laws on scstatehouse.gov, S.C. Supreme Court opinions on sccourts.org, SCDMV, SC Department of Insurance). Raw copies of every page read are in `llg/research/raw/`.

Currency note: the scstatehouse.gov chapter pages already show 2025 Act No. 42 amendments with their January 1, 2026 effective versions, so they are current through at least the 2025 session. Changes from the 2026 session were not checked.

Check of items listed as verified elsewhere: while reading these chapters I confirmed 38-77-140 (25/50/25), 38-77-150 (mandatory UM), 56-5-3660 (helmets for riders under 21), 56-5-1260 (immediate notice of injury crashes) and 15-38-15 as amended by Act 42. All of them are correct. Two details on those items should be carried forward: see the **15-38-15(F) trap** in section 10, and the **eye protection trap** in section 4.

## 1. Rideshare / Transportation Network Companies (Uber, Lyft)

### TNC Act: where it lives
- FACT: South Carolina regulates Uber, Lyft and similar services under the Transportation Network Company Act, Article 16 of Chapter 23, Title 58, enacted in 2015. The Office of Regulatory Staff issues TNC permits.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 58-23-1610 through 58-23-1720. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 58-23-1620(A): an entity must "hold a valid TNC permit issued by the Office of Regulatory Staff."
- TRAPS: The statute uses the terms "TNC," "prearranged ride," "logged on the TNC's digital network" and "TNC service." Pages should say "rideshare" in plain English but must not invent new statutory categories.

### Insurance when the driver is logged into the app and available, with no ride accepted ("Period 1")
- FACT: While a rideshare driver is logged into the app and available for requests but has not accepted a ride, South Carolina requires primary liability coverage of at least $50,000 per person and $100,000 per incident for bodily injury or death, plus $50,000 for property damage. Uninsured motorist coverage at the Section 38-77-150 minimum is also required.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 58-23-1630(B). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "at least fifty thousand dollars for death and bodily injury per person, at least one hundred thousand dollars for death and bodily injury per incident, and at least fifty thousand dollars for property damage" and "uninsured motorist coverage as required by Section 38-77-150"
- TRAPS: This is 50/100/50, not the 25/50/25 personal auto minimum. The coverage must be **primary**.

### Insurance when en route to a pickup or with a passenger aboard (the "prearranged ride")
- FACT: From the moment a driver accepts a ride request until the last passenger gets out, the law requires at least $1,000,000 in primary liability coverage for death, bodily injury and property damage. It also requires uninsured motorist coverage at the Section 38-77-150 level.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 58-23-1630(C); definition of "prearranged ride" in 58-23-1610(9). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "primary automobile liability insurance that provides at least one million dollars for death, bodily injury, and property damage"; a prearranged ride begins "when a driver accepts a ride requested by a rider ... and ending when the last requesting rider departs from the personal vehicle."
- TRAPS: (1) The $1 million is a **combined single limit** for injury, death and property damage. It is not "$1 million per person." (2) The statute sets **no $1 million UM/UIM requirement**. It requires UM only "as required by Section 38-77-150," which is the basic 25/50/25 minimum. Do not write that the rideshare company carries $1 million in UM or UIM coverage. What Uber's or Lyft's actual policies carry beyond the statute was not verified. (3) The statute does not mention underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage for TNC trips at all.

### Who provides the coverage
- FACT: The driver, the rideshare company, or a combination of the two can satisfy the coverage. If the driver's insurance has lapsed or does not provide the required coverage, the rideshare company's insurance must pay from the first dollar and must defend the claim. The company's coverage cannot be conditioned on the driver's personal insurer denying the claim first.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 58-23-1630(A), (B)(3), (C)(3), (D), (E). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "may be satisfied by automobile insurance maintained by the TNC driver, automobile insurance maintained by the TNC, or both"; "insurance maintained by a TNC must provide the coverage required by this section beginning with the first dollar of a claim and has the duty to defend such claim."
- TRAPS: Personal auto insurers may exclude **all** coverage while the driver is logged on or on a ride. That includes liability, UM/UIM, med pay, comprehensive and collision (58-23-1625(A)). A rideshare driver's personal policy often will not respond.

### Driver requirements
- FACT: Before approving a driver, the rideshare company must confirm that the driver has a valid license and is at least 21. It must also obtain a ten-year driving record, run local and national criminal background checks plus a national sex offender registry search, and get proof of the driver's own auto liability insurance. These checks must be repeated at least once a year. Drivers are barred if they are registered sex offenders or have been convicted in the past ten years of DUI, fraud, using a vehicle to commit a felony, a felony involving property damage, theft, or a violent crime. A driver under the influence may not drive.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 58-23-1650(B), (C), (F). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "verification that the driver is twenty-one years of age or older"; "a certified copy of the driver's ten year driving record"; "Nothing in this section may be construed to require drug testing by a TNC of a TNC driver."
- TRAPS: No drug testing is required. Vehicles need a certified-mechanic safety inspection within 30 days of first use and at least once a year after that (58-23-1640). The company keeps inspection and driver records for three years (58-23-1670).

### Insurance disclosure after a crash
- FACT: A rideshare driver must carry proof of coverage. After a crash, on request, the driver must give that insurance information to the people directly involved, to insurers and to the investigating officers. The driver must also say whether he or she was logged into the app or on a ride at the time. During a coverage investigation, the rideshare company and the insurers must share the exact times the driver logged on and off in the 12 hours before and the 12 hours after the crash.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 58-23-1630(H) and 58-23-1625(D). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "a TNC driver shall also disclose to directly interested parties, automobile insurers, and the investigating police officers, whether he was logged on the TNC's digital network or on a prearranged ride at the time of an accident"; "the precise times that a driver logged on and off of the TNC's digital network in the twelve-hour period immediately preceding and in the twelve-hour period immediately following the accident"
- TRAPS: The 12-hour log-on data exchange runs among the rideshare company, the insurers and "directly involved parties" during a claims coverage investigation. Do not describe it as a public records right.

### Common carrier status
- FACT: The TNC Act does not call rideshare companies common carriers. It separates them from the taxi and motor-carrier regime in Articles 1 through 15 and says TNCs and their drivers are governed "exclusively" by Article 16 and ORS regulations, subject to local ordinances such as traffic and parking rules.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 58-23-1610(1) and 58-23-1710(A). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "A transportation network company does not include transportation services provided pursuant to Articles 1 through 15"; "TNC's and TNC drivers are governed exclusively by this article"
- TRAPS: Do not write that Uber or Lyft owes passengers a common carrier's "highest degree of care" under South Carolina law. That was not verified. Do not write that it does not, either.

## 2. Hit and Run, and Uninsured Motorist Claims Against an Unknown Driver

### Duty to stop when someone is hurt or killed (56-5-1210)
- FACT: A driver involved in a crash that injures or kills someone must stop immediately at or near the scene and stay until he or she has given name, address and registration and rendered reasonable aid. Leaving the scene is punished in three tiers:
  - Injury without great bodily injury: a misdemeanor, with 30 days to 1 year and/or a fine of $100 to $5,000.
  - Great bodily injury: a felony, with 30 days to 10 years and a fine of $5,000 to $10,000.
  - Death: a felony, with 1 to 25 years and a fine of $10,000 to $25,000.
  
  SCDMV must revoke the license of anyone convicted.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-1210(A), (C), (D); duty to give information and render aid in 56-5-1230. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "immediately shall stop the vehicle at the scene of the accident or as close to it as possible"; "great bodily injury" means "bodily injury which creates a substantial risk of death or which causes serious, permanent disfigurement, or protracted loss or impairment of the function of a bodily member or organ."
- TRAPS: 56-5-1210 covers injury and death crashes. A crash that damages only an attended vehicle falls under 56-5-1220, a misdemeanor with up to 1 year and/or a fine of $100 to $5,000. Under 56-5-1210(B), a vehicle in a great-bodily-injury or fatal crash may not be moved until the investigating officer authorizes it.

### UM claims when the at-fault driver is unknown (38-77-170), rewritten in 2024
- FACT: To recover uninsured motorist benefits when the at-fault driver or owner is unknown, all three of the following must be true:
  1. The crash was reported to an appropriate police authority within a reasonable time under the circumstances.
  2. At least one of the following applies:
     - there was physical contact with the unknown vehicle,
     - someone other than the insured vehicle's owner or driver witnessed the crash and signs an affidavit (or, if no affidavit can be obtained, the insured may seek a court-ordered presuit deposition of the witness under SCRCP Rule 27), or
     - the insured can provide a recording showing the unknown vehicle caused the injury or damage.
  3. The insured was not negligent in failing to identify the other vehicle and driver at the time.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 38-77-170, as rewritten by 2024 Act No. 180 (H.4869), Section 7, effective May 20, 2024. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "(a) the injury or damage was caused by physical contact with the unknown vehicle; (b) the accident was witnessed by someone other than the owner or operator of the insured vehicle ... (c) the insured can provide a recording of the accident, obtained electronically or otherwise, showing that the damage or injury was caused by the unknown vehicle"; and "the insured was not negligent in failing to determine the identity of the other vehicle and the driver of the other vehicle at the time of the accident."
- TRAPS: (1) **Older sources are outdated.** Before May 20, 2024 the statute had no recording option and no Rule 27 deposition path. Dashcam or surveillance video is now an independent route. (2) **The statute sets no fixed deadline for the police report** (no "24 hours" or "72 hours"). It says "within a reasonable time, under all the circumstances." (3) The witness affidavit must carry, prominently, the capitalized false-statement warning in 38-77-170(B). (4) The separate "John Doe" lawsuit provision, 38-77-180, still says "by physical contact" when describing unknown-driver suits. Do not overstate how the non-contact routes play out in litigation.

## 3. DUI, Punitive Damages, Dram Shop and Social Host Liability

### DUI (56-5-2930) and the .08 threshold (56-5-2933, 56-5-2950)
- FACT: South Carolina's DUI statute makes it illegal to drive while alcohol, drugs or a combination "materially and appreciably" impairs the driver's faculties. A separate offense, driving with an unlawful alcohol concentration, applies at a BAC of .08 or higher. At trial, a BAC of .08 or more permits an inference of being under the influence. A BAC of .05 or less creates a conclusive presumption of not being under the influence. A BAC between those two figures creates no inference either way.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 56-5-2930(A), 56-5-2933(A), 56-5-2950. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 56-5-2930: "to the extent that the person's faculties to drive a motor vehicle are materially and appreciably impaired"; 56-5-2933: "while his alcohol concentration is eight one-hundredths of one percent or more"; 56-5-2950: "if the alcohol concentration was at that time eight one-hundredths of one percent or more, it may be inferred that the person was under the influence of alcohol."
- TRAPS: **56-5-2930 itself contains no .08 standard.** The .08 per se offense is 56-5-2933. When a page cites .08, cite 56-5-2933, not 56-5-2930. Enhanced penalty tiers apply at .10 to under .16 and at .16 or more.

### Felony DUI (56-5-2945)
- FACT: Felony DUI applies when an impaired driver commits an act forbidden by law, or neglects a duty, and that proximately causes great bodily injury or death to another person. Penalties:
  - Great bodily injury: a mandatory fine of $5,100 to $10,100 and 30 days to 15 years in prison.
  - Death: a mandatory fine of $10,100 to $25,100 and 1 to 25 years in prison.
  
  No part of the mandatory sentence may be suspended.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-2945(A), (B). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "does any act forbidden by law or neglects any duty imposed by law in the driving of the motor vehicle, which act or neglect proximately causes great bodily injury or death to another person"
- TRAPS: The fines are $5,100 and $10,100, not round numbers.

### Punitive damages: the cap and its exceptions (15-32-530)
- FACT: Punitive damages require clear and convincing evidence of willful, wanton or reckless conduct (15-32-520(D)). They are generally capped at the greater of three times compensatory damages or $500,000, with the dollar figure adjusted annually for inflation. The cap rises to the greater of four times compensatory damages or $2,000,000 in two situations: the conduct was motivated primarily by unreasonable financial gain and known or approved by management, or the conduct could subject the defendant to a felony conviction. **There is no cap at all** in three situations: the defendant intended to harm and did harm the claimant; the defendant pleaded guilty to or was convicted of a felony arising from the same conduct; or the defendant acted while under the influence of alcohol, drugs other than lawfully prescribed drugs taken as prescribed, or intentionally consumed glue, aerosol or other toxic vapor, to the degree that his or her judgment was substantially impaired.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 15-32-520(D) and 15-32-530(A) through (D). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c032.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "there shall be no cap on punitive damages: ... (3) the defendant acted or failed to act while under the influence of alcohol, drugs, other than lawfully prescribed drugs administered in accordance with a prescription, or any intentionally consumed glue, aerosol, or other toxic vapor to the degree that the defendant's judgment is substantially impaired."
- TRAPS: (1) The $500,000 figure is CPI-adjusted each year under 15-32-530(D), and the current adjusted figure was not verified. Write "$500,000, adjusted annually for inflation" or avoid the dollar figure. (2) The jury is not told about the cap (15-32-530(B)). (3) The alcohol exception requires substantially impaired judgment, not simply having had a drink. (4) The section applies to actions accruing on or after January 1, 2012.

### Dram shop: sales to intoxicated persons (61-4-580, 61-6-2220)
- FACT: Beer and wine permit holders and their employees may not knowingly sell beer or wine to an intoxicated person, or to anyone under 21, on the licensed premises. Liquor-by-the-drink licensees may not knowingly sell to persons in an intoxicated condition. The word "knowingly" was added to 61-6-2220 effective January 1, 2026. Also effective that date, 61-4-580(B) begins "In addition to civil liability as provided by law." It sets administrative penalties of a $2,500 fine for a first offense, a suspension of up to 14 days for a second offense within two years, and revocation for a third offense within three years.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 61-4-580(A)(1)-(2), (B) (2025 Act No. 42, Section 4). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t61c004.php, checked 2026-09-28. S.C. Code Ann. Section 61-6-2220 (2025 Act No. 42, Section 2). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t61c006.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 61-6-2220 (eff. Jan. 1, 2026): "may not knowingly sell these beverages to persons in an intoxicated condition"; 61-4-580(A): "No holder of a permit authorizing the sale of beer or wine ... may knowingly commit any of the following acts ... (2) sell beer or wine to an intoxicated person"
- TRAPS: For claims arising before 2026, 61-6-2220 read "may not sell" without "knowingly." Both versions appear on the Code page.

### Civil liability of a bar or restaurant to third parties (Tobias)
- FACT: South Carolina lets **third parties** injured by an intoxicated patron sue the commercial seller in negligence, based on a violation of the sales-to-intoxicated-persons statutes. It does **not** let the intoxicated adult patron sue the seller for his or her own injuries. As the Supreme Court later summarized, a commercial host "is liable only to third parties, and then only when he knowingly sells alcoholic beverages to an intoxicated person."
- SOURCE: Tobias v. Sports Club, Inc., 332 S.C. 90, 504 S.E.2d 318 (1998). Holding and quotes read at https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1358670/tobias-v-sports-club-inc/, checked 2026-09-28. Citation and summary confirmed in Marcum v. Bowden, S.C. Sup. Ct. Op. No. 26259, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26259.htm, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: Tobias: "[We] explicitly retain the right of injured third parties to maintain a negligence suit against the tavern owner based on a violation of these statutes." Tobias overruled Christiansen v. Campbell, 285 S.C. 164 (Ct. App. 1985), "to the extent [it] recognize[s] a first party action." Marcum: "a commercial host is liable only to third parties, and then only when he knowingly sells alcoholic beverages to an intoxicated person."
- TRAPS: Tobias interpreted the predecessor statutes, 61-5-30 and 61-9-410, which are now 61-6-2220 and 61-4-580. The intoxicated adult has no first-party claim. For claims arising after January 1, 2026, apply the fault-allocation rules in 61-2-147 (see section 10).

### Social host liability for serving people under 21 (Marcum v. Bowden)
- FACT: An adult social host who knowingly and intentionally serves alcohol, or causes it to be served, to someone the host knows or reasonably should know is 18 to 20 years old is liable to that person and to anyone else harmed as a proximate result. The court added that a host who knowingly and intentionally serves a minor under 18 is liable to the guest and to third parties. The rule applies only to claims arising after the decision. A social host still has no liability for injuries caused by an intoxicated adult guest aged 21 or older.
- SOURCE: Marcum v. Bowden, 372 S.C. 452, 643 S.E.2d 85 (2007), S.C. Sup. Ct. Op. No. 26259 (reheard May 23, 2006, refiled Feb. 5, 2007). https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26259.htm, checked 2026-09-28. Reporter citation confirmed in S.C. Sup. Ct. Op. No. 26930, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26930.htm, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "An adult social host who knowingly and intentionally serves, or causes to be served, an alcoholic beverage to a person he knows or reasonably should know is between the ages of 18 and 20 is liable to the person served and to any other person for damages proximately resulting from the host's service of alcohol." Footnote 1: "an adult social host who knowingly and intentionally serves, or causes to be served, alcoholic beverages to a minor under 18 is liable to both the guest and third parties." And: "At present, a South Carolina social host incurs no liability to either first or third parties injured by an intoxicated adult guest." (citing Garren v. Cummings & McCrady, Inc., 289 S.C. 348 (Ct. App. 1986))
- TRAPS: The standard is "knowingly and intentionally," not mere negligence. Marcum expressly left open whether a merely negligent host is liable for a guest under 18. The duty comes from common law, not from the alcohol statutes.

## 4. Motorcycles

### Lane use and lane splitting (56-5-3640)
- FACT: A motorcycle is entitled to the full use of a lane, and no driver may deprive a motorcycle of that. Lane splitting is illegal in South Carolina: no one may ride between lanes of traffic or between rows of vehicles, or pass in the same lane as the vehicle being overtaken. Motorcycles may ride no more than two abreast in one lane. Police officers on duty are exempt from the passing and lane-splitting rules.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-3640(a) through (e). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "All motorcycles are entitled to full use of a lane and no motor vehicle shall be driven in such a manner as to deprive any motorcycle of the full use of a lane"; "No person shall operate a motorcycle between lanes of traffic, or between adjacent lines or rows of vehicles."
- TRAPS: Motorcyclists otherwise have the same rights and duties as other drivers (56-5-3610).

### Helmets and eye protection (56-5-3660, 3670, 3680)
- FACT: Riders under 21, whether operating or riding as passengers, must wear a DPS-approved helmet with a neck or chin strap, reflectorized on both sides. Operators under 21 must also wear DPS-approved goggles or a face shield unless the motorcycle has a windscreen that meets DPS specifications. Riders 21 and older are not required by statute to wear a helmet. A violation is a misdemeanor with a fine of up to $100 or up to 30 days.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 56-5-3660, 56-5-3670, 56-5-3680, 56-5-3700. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 3660: "unlawful for any person under the age of twenty-one to operate or ride upon a two-wheeled motorized vehicle unless he wears a protective helmet"; 3670: "unlawful for any person under the age of twenty-one to operate a two-wheeled motorized vehicle unless he wears goggles or a face shield"; 3680: the goggle rule does "not apply to the operator of a two-wheeled motorized vehicle equipped with a wind screen."
- TRAPS: (1) The **eye protection rule covers operators only**, not passengers, and it has a windscreen exception. SCDMV's motorcycle page (https://www.dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/types-of-vehicles/motorcycle) simplifies this to "If you're under 21, you must wear a helmet and eye protection." Follow the statute. (2) Whether a helmet law violation or not wearing a helmet can reduce damages in a civil case was not verified. Do not state a rule.

### Passengers and equipment (56-5-3630, 56-5-3650)
- FACT: A passenger may ride only if the motorcycle is designed to carry more than one person, and only on a permanent seat designed for two or on a firmly attached rear or side seat. Riders must sit astride facing forward. A passenger must be able to reach the footrests with both feet (the sidecar rule is different). A motorcycle carrying a passenger outside a sidecar or enclosed cab must have passenger footrests. Every motorcycle must have a rear-view mirror.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 56-5-3630(A), (B), (E) and 56-5-3650(A), (B). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 3630(E): "No person shall ride upon a motorcycle as a passenger unless, when sitting astride the seat, the person can reach the footrests with both feet"; 3650(A): "Any motorcycle carrying a passenger ... must be equipped with footrests for its passenger."
- TRAPS: The passenger-seating rules are in **56-5-3630**. 56-5-3650 covers only footrests and the mirror.

### Motorcycle insurance minimums
- FACT: Motorcycles must be titled and registered, and every registered motor vehicle must carry the security required by 38-77-140 through 38-77-230. That means the same 25/50/25 liability minimum and the mandatory uninsured motorist coverage that apply to cars. The auto insurance chapter's definitions expressly include motorcycles.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 56-10-10 and 56-10-20, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c010.php; Sections 38-77-30(5.5)(a)(iv), (9), (10.5)(a), 38-77-140 and 38-77-150, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php; SCDMV motorcycle page ("Just like a traditional car, truck, or SUV, you must title and register your motorcycle"), https://www.dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/types-of-vehicles/motorcycle. All checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 56-10-10: "Every owner of a motor vehicle required to be registered in this State shall maintain the security required by Section 56-10-20"; 38-77-30(10.5)(a): a policy covers "a motor vehicle of a private passenger, station wagon, or motorcycle type"
- TRAPS: Mopeds count as motor vehicles only for UM and UIM purposes (38-77-30(9)) and are excluded from "individual private passenger automobile."

## 5. Rules of the Road Most Often Cited in Crash Claims

### Left turns (56-5-2320)
- FACT: A driver turning left at an intersection, or into an alley, private road or driveway, must yield to oncoming traffic that is in the intersection or close enough to be an immediate hazard.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-2320. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "shall yield the right-of-way to any vehicle approaching from the opposite direction which is within the intersection or so close thereto as to constitute an immediate hazard."
- TRAPS: Violating this statute does not automatically put 100 percent of the fault on the turning driver. South Carolina's comparative fault still applies.

### Stop signs and yield signs (56-5-2330)
- FACT: At a stop sign a driver must stop at the marked line, or before the crosswalk, or else at the point with a view of cross traffic, and then yield to vehicles in or approaching the intersection closely enough to be an immediate hazard. At a yield sign a driver must slow to a reasonable speed, stop if needed, and yield. If a driver passes a yield sign without stopping and collides with a vehicle in the intersection, the collision is prima facie evidence of failure to yield.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-2330(b), (c). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "If such driver is involved in a collision with a vehicle in the intersection or junction of roadways, after driving past a yield sign without stopping, the collision shall be deemed prima facie evidence of his failure to yield right-of-way."
- TRAPS: The prima facie rule is written for **yield signs**, not stop signs.

### Following too closely (56-5-1930)
- FACT: A driver may not follow another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent given speed, traffic and road conditions. Outside business or residence districts, trucks and vehicles towing others must leave room for an overtaking vehicle to move in safely. Since 2017, the rule does not apply to non-lead commercial vehicles in an automated platoon, meaning vehicles subject to the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations traveling in a series using cooperative adaptive cruise control or other automated driving technology.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-1930(A), (B), (D). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "shall not follow another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent, having due regard for the speed of such vehicles and the traffic upon and the condition of the highway."
- TRAPS: The statute sets no numeric following distance, such as "3 seconds." The platooning exemption matters on truck pages.

### Too fast for conditions and speed limits (56-5-1520)
- FACT: No one may drive faster than is reasonable and prudent for existing conditions and hazards, even when under the posted limit. Speed must be controlled to avoid a collision. The default statutory maximums are:
  - 70 mph on posted interstates and freeways,
  - 60 mph on posted multilane divided primary highways,
  - 55 mph elsewhere,
  - 40 mph on unpaved roads,
  - 30 mph in urban districts.
  
  Drivers must slow when approaching intersections, curves, hillcrests and narrow bridges, and when weather or pedestrians create hazards.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-1520(A), (B), (C), (F). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "A person shall not drive a vehicle on a highway at a speed greater than is reasonable and prudent under the conditions and having regard to the actual and potential hazards then existing."
- TRAPS: Posted limits can differ under 56-5-1530 and 56-5-1540. Do not state the limit on a specific road, such as I-26 near Summerville, unless it is verified separately.

## 6. Crash Reports

### Immediate notice to police (56-5-1260)
- FACT: A driver in a crash that injures or kills anyone must notify police immediately, by the quickest means: the local police department inside a municipality, otherwise the county sheriff or the nearest Highway Patrol office.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-1260. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "shall immediately by the quickest means of communication, whether oral or written, give notice of such accident"
- TRAPS: None.

### Written report when no officer investigated (56-5-1270, SCDMV Form FR-309)
- FACT: If no officer investigated a crash that caused injury, death, or apparent total property damage of $1,000 or more, the driver or owner must send SCDMV a written report with verification of liability insurance within 15 days of the crash. SCDMV Form FR-309 (Traffic Collision Report, Not Investigated by Law Enforcement) is used for this. Failing to file properly verified insurance information is prima facie evidence that the vehicle was uninsured. An officer who investigates a qualifying crash must forward the report to SCDMV within 24 hours after completing the investigation.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-5-1270, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php; SCDMV Form FR-309, https://dmv.sc.gov/sites/scdmv/files/media/Forms/fr-309.pdf. Both checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 56-5-1270: "within fifteen days after the accident, shall forward a written report and verification of liability insurance coverage"; FR-309: "shall complete and send this form to South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles, Financial Responsibility, P.O. Box 1498, Blythewood, SC 29016-0040 within 15 days of the collision."
- TRAPS: See the $400 versus $1,000 threshold conflict under FR-10.

### FR-10 insurance verification form (56-9-350)
- FACT: After a crash with injury, death or property damage of $400 or more, the investigating officer gives each driver or owner a written insurance verification form, the FR-10. It must be completed, verified and returned to SCDMV within 15 days of the date the officer delivered it. The SC Department of Insurance explains that the officer hands out a green FR-10 form, which the driver's insurance company completes regardless of fault and may submit electronically. Failure to return it is prima facie evidence that the vehicle was uninsured.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-9-350, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c009.php, checked 2026-09-28. SC Department of Insurance Consumer Alert (April 18, 2016), https://doi.sc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/9331/Auto-Accident-Tips---Consumer-Alert?bidId=, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 56-9-350: "The completed and verified form must be returned by the operator or owner to the Department of Motor Vehicles within fifteen days from the date the form was delivered by the officer." DOI: the green form "must be completed by your insurance company – regardless of fault – and returned to the DMV within 15 days of the accident."
- TRAPS: (1) **The two statutes use different thresholds.** 56-9-350 (FR-10 and the non-investigated report) uses $400. 56-5-1270 (the report when no officer investigated) uses $1,000. Do not merge them, and if a page mentions a threshold, cite the matching statute. (2) The statute runs the 15 days from delivery of the form, while DOI says 15 days from the accident. Safest wording: "within 15 days." (3) The DOI release is from 2016. Its FR-50 fee ($6) and branch-office details are outdated, so use it only for the FR-10 description.

### Getting a copy of the collision report (Form FR-50)
- FACT: SCDMV provides copies of officer collision reports online, at any SCDMV branch, or by mail using Form FR-50 (two copies of the request). The fee is $10 per report. The official copy is available only after SCDMV has received and processed it. Online requests are not available for crashes before March 22, 2012. The full collision report is more detailed than the paperwork drivers get at the scene.
- SOURCE: SCDMV Collision Reports page, https://www.dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/collision-reports; SCDMV Form FR-50 (Rev. 01/2026), https://www.dmv.sc.gov/sites/scdmv/files/2026-01/FR-50%20Request%20for%20Copy%20of%20Collision%20Report.pdf; statutory authority in S.C. Code Ann. Section 56-9-330(2), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c009.php. All checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: SCDMV: "you may request a copy of the collision report online or by completing the Request for Copy of Collision Report (SCDMV Form FR-50) and taking it to any SCDMV branch office or mailing it"; FR-50: "The request fee is $10.00 per report."
- TRAPS: Mail requests go to SCDMV, Titles Mail-in Unit FR-50, PO Box 1498, Blythewood, SC 29016-0050, with a check or money order and no cash. Older sources give a $6 fee and only two in-person offices. Both are outdated.

## 7. UIM Stacking, and Class I and Class II Insureds

- FACT: Insurers must **offer** optional underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage up to the insured's liability limits. UIM pays when damages exceed the at-fault driver's liability limits or "any damages cap or limitation imposed by statute." When an insured has UM or UIM above the basic limits, the policy covers only up to the amount on the vehicle involved in the crash. If none of the insured's vehicles was involved, coverage is limited to the coverage on any one vehicle that carries the excess or UIM coverage. The Supreme Court has held that only a **Class I insured** may stack. Class I insureds are the named insured, the spouse, and relatives living in the household. **Class II insureds** are permissive users of the insured vehicle and guests in it, and they may not stack. A Class I insured need not own the vehicle involved to stack.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 38-77-160, and the definition of "insured" in 38-77-30(7), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php, checked 2026-09-28. Concrete Services, Inc. v. United States Fidelity & Guaranty Co., S.C. Sup. Ct. Op. No. 24773 (filed Mar. 23, 1998), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/24773.htm, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 38-77-160: "coverage is available only to the extent of coverage on any one of the vehicles with the excess or underinsured coverage." Concrete Services: "The right to stack is available only to a Class I insured"; "The two classes of insureds are: (1) the named insured, his spouse and relatives residing in his household; and (2) any person using, with the consent of the named insured, the motor vehicle to which the policy applies and a guest in the motor vehicle."
- TRAPS: (1) UIM is **optional** and must be offered. It is not mandatory. UM is mandatory. (2) 38-77-160 also bars policy clauses requiring the UIM insurer's consent before settling with the at-fault driver. The injured person must serve the UIM carrier with the pleadings, and the carrier has 30 days to appear. (3) Under 38-77-350(E), if the insured does not return the signed offer form within 30 days, the insurer must add UM and UIM at the same limits as liability. (4) Stacking outcomes depend on policy facts. Keep page language general and do not promise stacking. (5) The reporter citation 331 S.C. 506, 498 S.E.2d 865 appears only in secondary listings, so cite the opinion number and date instead.

## 8. Medical Payments Coverage (Med Pay) and PIP

- FACT: South Carolina does not require personal injury protection (PIP). It is not a no-fault state. Med pay is optional. If an insurer sells PIP, med pay or economic loss coverage, that coverage cannot be assigned or subrogated and is not subject to a setoff. Separately, an insurer paying first-party benefits such as med pay or UM may not have the claimant sign a release, subrogation assignment or agreement to deduct the payment from a liability recovery unless a disclosure statement is delivered first and at least three days pass.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 38-77-144 and 38-77-260(a). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 38-77-144: "There is no personal injury protection (PIP) coverage mandated under the automobile insurance laws of this State ... If an insurer sells no-fault insurance coverage which provides personal injury protection, medical payment coverage, or economic loss coverage, the coverage shall not be assigned or subrogated and is not subject to a setoff."
- TRAPS: No statute found in Chapter 77 requires insurers to offer med pay the way 38-77-160 requires UIM to be offered. Say "optional," not "must be offered." Rideshare drivers' personal policies may exclude med pay while the driver is logged on (58-23-1625(A)(3)).

## 9. Insurer Claim Handling and Bad Faith (only what the statutes clearly say)

### Policy-limits disclosure within 30 days (38-77-250)
- FACT: An auto insurer that is or may be liable on a claim must give the claimant's attorney, within 30 days of a qualifying written request, a sworn statement of the insurer's name, each insured's name, and the coverage limits for each known non-fleet private passenger policy, or it may provide the declarations page instead. The request must be sworn, sent by certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, and accompanied by the incident report. Fleet, umbrella and excess limits are exempt.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 38-77-250 (2011 Act No. 52, effective Jan. 1, 2012). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "shall provide, within thirty days of receiving a written request from the claimant's attorney, a statement, under oath ... the name of the insurer, the name of each insured, and the limits of coverage"
- TRAPS: The information is confidential and must be destroyed when the case ends (subsection (F)).

### Undisputed property damage (38-77-330)
- FACT: An insurer may not deny or delay a property damage claim because an injury claim from the same crash is pending. When liability and the amount are undisputed, payment is due immediately. The Director of Insurance may assess 8 percent interest on unnecessarily delayed payments.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 38-77-330. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "No claim for damage to property resulting from a motor vehicle accident may be denied or payment delayed because the person who is entitled to payment or any other person has a claim pending for bodily injury"
- TRAPS: The 8 percent interest is assessed by the Director of Insurance, not by a court.

### Improper claim practices and attorney fees (38-59-20, 38-59-30, 38-59-40)
- FACT: It is an improper claim practice for an insurer to do any of the listed acts, including failing to attempt in good faith to settle promptly and fairly when liability has become reasonably clear, if the insurer does so without just cause and often enough to indicate a general business practice. These practices are enforced by the Director of Insurance after notice and a hearing. Separately, if a policyholder sues after an insurer refuses to pay a covered claim within 90 days of demand, and the trial judge finds the refusal was without reasonable cause or in bad faith, the insurer must pay reasonable attorney fees. Those fees cannot exceed one-third of the judgment.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 38-59-20, 38-59-30, 38-59-40. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c059.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 38-59-20(4): "Not attempting in good faith to effect prompt, fair, and equitable settlement of claims, including third-party liability claims, submitted to it in which liability has become reasonably clear"; 38-59-40: "within ninety days after a demand has been made by the holder of the policy"; "may not exceed one-third of the amount of the judgment"
- TRAPS: (1) 38-59-20 requires a pattern ("general business practice") and is an administrative remedy through the Director. Do not describe it as a statutory claim an injured third party can sue on. (2) The 38-59-40 fee remedy belongs to the "holder of the policy." (3) 38-59-40(3) preserves the Tyger River doctrine (Tyger River Pine Co. v. Maryland Casualty Co., 163 S.C. 229, 161 S.E. 491), a liability insurer's exposure for unreasonably refusing to settle. The scope of that doctrine and South Carolina's common-law first-party bad faith tort were not verified.

## 10. The 2025 Tort Reform Act (Act No. 42 of 2025, H.3430)

### Dates and applicability
- FACT: H.3430 was ratified as R61 on May 8, 2025, signed by the Governor on May 12, 2025, and became Act No. 42. It takes effect January 1, 2026 and applies only to causes of action or claims arising or accruing **after** January 1, 2026, and to all policies issued after that date. The one exception is Section 61-4-523 (beer and wine at collegiate sporting venues), which took effect on the Governor's approval on May 12, 2025.
- SOURCE: Act No. 42 (H.3430), Section 11, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3430.htm, checked 2026-09-28. The Section 11 text is also reproduced in the editor's notes to 15-38-15, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c038.php
- QUOTE: "SECTION 11. This act takes effect January 1, 2026, and applies only to causes of action or claims arising or accruing after January 1, 2026, and applies to all policies issued after that date, other than Section 61-4-523 which takes effect upon approval by the Governor." "Ratified the 8th day of May, 2025." "Governor's Action: May 12, 2025, Signed"
- TRAPS: (1) The applicability test turns on when the claim **arose or accrued**, not when suit was filed. A 2025 crash sued on in 2026 falls under the old law. (2) The text says "after January 1, 2026," not "on or after," so a claim accruing on January 1, 2026 itself is ambiguous. Avoid saying "on or after." (3) The Code pages show both versions of each amended section, "effective until January 1, 2026" and "effective January 1, 2026," so make sure a page quotes the right one.

### Joint and several liability: the 50 percent threshold (15-38-15(A), (F))
- FACT: For claims under the new law, a defendant found less than 50 percent at fault pays only its percentage share. Fault is now compared against all defendants **and nondefendant tortfeasors**, plus the plaintiff's own comparative fault. The threshold does not apply to a defendant whose conduct is willful, wanton, reckless or intentional, or involves illegal or illicit drug use, sale or possession. That defendant is jointly and severally liable for all damages.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 15-38-15(A), (F), effective Jan. 1, 2026 (Act No. 42, Section 1). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c038.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "(i) the fault of all the defendants and tortfeasors; and (ii) the fault (comparative negligence), if any, of plaintiff"; "(F) The provisions of subsection (A) do not apply to a defendant whose conduct is determined to be wilful, wanton, reckless, or intentional or conduct involving the illegal or illicit use, sale, or possession of drugs, and such a defendant shall be jointly and severally liable for all damages"
- TRAPS: **15-38-15(F) trap.** The old (F) also excluded "grossly negligent" conduct and "conduct involving the use, sale, or possession of alcohol." **Act 42 removed both.** For claims arising after January 1, 2026, a defendant is no longer automatically jointly and severally liable just because alcohol was involved or the conduct was grossly negligent. Only the listed categories remain. Alcohol-related sellers are now handled by 61-2-147. The Act's own title says it removes "the exception ... applying to the use, sale, or possession of alcohol."

### Empty-chair allocation to nonparties (15-38-15(G), (H))
- FACT: A jury may now assign fault to a nondefendant tortfeasor (an "empty chair"), under several conditions:
  - The tortfeasor must be disclosed within 180 days after the suit begins, unless good cause allows later disclosure.
  - The plaintiff may add that person as a defendant, and the amendment relates back.
  - The defendant must prove the added tortfeasor's breach was a proximate cause, unless the plaintiff adds that tortfeasor as a party.
  - The plaintiff may challenge the addition under SCRCP Rules 56 and 50.
  - Settling tortfeasors go on the verdict form.
  
  A nonparty may **not** be placed on the verdict form if:
  - the nonparty is immune or cannot be sued (settled or released tortfeasors who were or could have been parties do not count under this item),
  - the nonparty's conduct was willful, wanton, reckless or intentional,
  - the defendant's liability is imputed from or based on that nonparty's fault,
  - the claim is strict liability,
  - the claim involves asbestos, or
  - the suit is brought by a governmental entity (with a PFAS exception).
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 15-38-15(G), (H), effective Jan. 1, 2026. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c038.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "the tortfeasor must be disclosed within one hundred eighty days of the commencement of the action"; "the defendant bears the burden of proof that the added tortfeasor's breach of duty was a proximate cause of the plaintiff's injuries unless the plaintiff amends his pleadings to add the tortfeasor as a party"
- TRAPS: Settlement setoff now applies only to settlements from tortfeasors "not placed on the verdict form" (15-38-15(E)).

### Liquor liability: DUI drivers on the verdict form, and the 50 percent rule for bars (61-2-147)
- FACT: When a tortfeasor was charged with DUI (56-5-2930), driving with an unlawful alcohol concentration (56-5-2933) or felony DUI (56-5-2945), that tortfeasor must appear on the verdict form if a defendant moves within 180 days of the suit's start, or later for good cause. A settling tortfeasor goes on the form regardless of timing. The plaintiff may add the charged driver as a defendant, and the amendment relates back. If a verdict is rendered against both an alcohol licensee and the charged driver, **the licensee is jointly and severally liable for 50 percent of the plaintiff's actual damages.**
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 61-2-147 (Act No. 42, Section 9, effective Jan. 1, 2026). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t61c002.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "If a verdict is rendered against both a licensee and a defendant charged under Section 56-5-2930, 56-5-2933, or 56-5-2945, then the licensee is jointly and severally liable for fifty percent of the plaintiff's actual damages."
- TRAPS: The rule turns on the driver being **charged**, not convicted. The 50 percent applies to **actual** damages, not punitive damages.

### Liquor liability insurance (61-2-145)
- FACT: Businesses selling alcohol for on-premises consumption that stay open after 5 p.m. must carry liquor liability insurance, or general liability with a liquor liability endorsement. Under the new law the policy must have an annual aggregate limit of at least $1,000,000 and per-occurrence coverage of at least 50 percent of that aggregate. The aggregate can be reduced through a risk mitigation program:
  - $250,000 off for stopping service by midnight,
  - $100,000 off each for all servers trained within 60 days, alcohol at under 40 percent of total sales, or digital ID scanning from midnight to 4 a.m.,
  - $500,000 off for 501(c)(3) nonprofits and single special events.
  
  The aggregate can never fall below $300,000. Businesses serving between midnight and 4 a.m. must use forensic digital ID scanning. Insurers must report a lapse to the Department of Revenue within 30 days.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Section 61-2-145, effective Jan. 1, 2026 (Act No. 42, Section 8). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t61c002.php, checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: "with an annual aggregate limit of at least one million dollars"; "minimum coverage of at least fifty percent of the total aggregate limit, per occurrence"; "must at all times maintain coverage with an annual aggregate limit of at least three hundred thousand dollars"
- TRAPS: The pre-2026 version required "a total coverage of at least one million dollars." Under the new version a bar's coverage may lawfully be as low as a $300,000 aggregate. Do not tell readers every bar carries $1 million.

### Mandatory alcohol server training (Title 61, Chapter 3)
- FACT: Act 42 created a statewide alcohol server certification requirement. Alcohol servers and managers at on-premises businesses must complete Department of Revenue-approved training and hold a certificate. Employers must provide training within 30 calendar days of hire if a server has no current certificate. The training is online, at least four hours, and proctored, and it covers recognizing intoxication, DUI law and liquor liability. Certificates last three years. Servers may not be impaired while serving.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code Ann. Sections 61-3-100 through 61-3-140 as enacted by Act No. 42, Section 3.A (text read in the Act), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3430.htm; rulemaking authority in 61-2-60(9), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t61c002.php. Both checked 2026-09-28
- QUOTE: 61-3-110(A): "An alcohol server or manager must complete alcohol server training and obtain an alcohol server certificate ... the licensee or permittee must provide alcohol server training within thirty calendar days of employment."
- TRAPS: The Title 61, Chapter 3 Code page itself (t61c003.php) was not fetched. The text was read from the enrolled Act. Temporary or one-time special event workers are excluded from the definition of "alcohol server."

### Other Act 42 pieces (lower relevance to crash pages)
- Sections 6 and 7 of the Act add 38-73-550, which requires an annual report by the Director of Insurance on the liquor liability market. They also amend 38-90-20 so captive insurers may write liquor liability coverage but may not issue "eroding or declining" limits that shrink as defense costs are spent. Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3430.htm, checked 2026-09-28.

## A. Federal trucking rules (49 CFR) and South Carolina adoption

### A1. Hours of service for property-carrying drivers (49 CFR 395.3)
- FACT: A property-carrying truck driver must have 10 consecutive hours off duty before driving. After coming on duty, the driver may not drive past the 14th consecutive hour. Within that 14-hour window the driver may drive at most 11 hours. After 8 hours of driving, the driver needs a break of at least 30 consecutive minutes. The break can be off duty, sleeper berth, or on duty but not driving. There is also a weekly limit. A driver may not drive after 60 hours on duty in 7 consecutive days, or 70 hours in 8 consecutive days if the carrier operates every day of the week. A driver can restart the 7- or 8-day period by taking 34 or more consecutive hours off duty.
- SOURCE: 49 CFR 395.3 (current through 2026-09-24). Full-text XML from the eCFR API at https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-24/title-49.xml?part=395, fetched with compression. The HTML page https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395/subpart-A/section-395.3 was confirmed as the real text via WebFetch. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "A driver may not drive without first taking 10 consecutive hours off duty"; "A driver may not drive after a period of 14 consecutive hours after coming on-duty following 10 consecutive hours off-duty"; "A driver may drive a total of 11 hours during the period specified in paragraph (a)(2)"; "driving is not permitted if more than 8 hours of driving time have passed without at least a consecutive 30-minute interruption in driving status ... may be satisfied either by off-duty, sleeper berth or on-duty not driving time"; "(1) Having been on duty 60 hours in any period of 7 consecutive days ... (2) Having been on duty 70 hours in any period of 8 consecutive days"; "Any period of 7 consecutive days may end with the beginning of an off-duty period of 34 or more consecutive hours."
- TRAPS: (1) The 30-minute break does not have to be off duty. Since the 2020 amendment it can be on duty but not driving. (2) The 60/70 limits count all on-duty time, not just driving. (3) The 34-hour restart is optional ("may end"). It is not a mandatory weekly rest. (4) These are property-carrier rules. Passenger carriers have separate limits in 395.5. (5) 395.1 carves out exceptions, including the short-haul exception in 395.1(e) (150 air-mile radius) and sleeper-berth provisions, so never write that every truck driver is subject to every limit. (6) Pages sometimes write "8 hours off"; the rule is 10.

### A2. ELD mandate and record retention (49 CFR 395.8, Part 395 Subpart B)
- FACT: Carriers subject to Part 395 must install ELDs and require their drivers to use them to record duty status. ELDs meeting Subpart B have been required since December 18, 2017. Paper logs are still allowed in limited cases: a driver who needs logs on no more than 8 days in any 30-day period, certain driveaway-towaway operations, and vehicles manufactured before model year 2000. Short-haul drivers who meet 395.1(e) are exempt from 395.8 altogether. A carrier must keep each driver's records of duty status and supporting documents for at least 6 months from the date of receipt. The driver must carry the previous 7 consecutive days of records while on duty.
- SOURCE: 49 CFR 395.8(a)(1)(i)–(ii), 395.8(k), 395.20, 395.22(a); 395.1(e)(1). Same eCFR API XML (part 395). Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "a motor carrier operating commercial motor vehicles must install and require each of its drivers to use an ELD to record the driver's duty status in accordance with subpart B of this part no later than December 18, 2017"; "A motor carrier shall retain records of duty status and supporting documents required under this part for each of its drivers for a period of not less than 6 months from the date of receipt"; "The driver shall retain a copy of each record of duty status for the previous 7 consecutive days."
- TRAPS: (1) The federal minimum retention is 6 months, not a year. That short window is why early evidence preservation matters, but pages must not state a longer retention period as law. (2) Not every truck has an ELD. Pre-2000 engines and 8-in-30-day drivers can lawfully use paper logs. (3) "Supporting documents" retention is also 6 months under 395.8(k)(1). Do not conflate it with 395.11's detailed definitions (not quoted here).

### A3. Commercial motor vehicle definitions (49 CFR 390.5; 49 CFR 383.5)
- FACT: For most federal motor carrier safety rules, a "commercial motor vehicle" is a vehicle used on a highway in interstate commerce that meets any one of four tests. It has a gross vehicle or combination weight rating (or actual weight) of 10,001 pounds or more. Or it is designed or used to carry more than 8 passengers, including the driver, for compensation. Or it carries more than 15 passengers, including the driver, not for compensation. Or it hauls placardable hazardous materials. The CDL rules use a higher line. A CDL is needed for combinations of 26,001 pounds or more (with a towed unit over 10,000 pounds), for single vehicles of 26,001 pounds or more, for vehicles designed for 16 or more passengers including the driver, and for placarded hazmat.
- SOURCE: 49 CFR 390.5 and 390.5T, "Commercial motor vehicle" (eCFR API XML, part 390). 49 CFR 383.5, "Commercial motor vehicle (CMV)" (eCFR API XML, part 383). Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 390.5: "Has a gross vehicle weight rating or gross combination weight rating, or gross vehicle weight or gross combination weight, of 4,536 kg (10,001 pounds) or more, whichever is greater." 383.5: "Heavy Straight Vehicle (Group B)—having a gross vehicle weight rating or gross vehicle weight of 11,794 or more kilograms (26,001 pounds or more)."
- TRAPS: (1) 10,001 lb is the federal safety-rule threshold (FMCSR) and 26,001 lb is the CDL threshold. Pages often swap them. (2) The 390.5 definition is tied to interstate commerce. For South Carolina intrastate trucks, see A7, where the SC threshold is 26,001 lb. (3) Many box trucks and large pickups with trailers are CMVs under 390.5 even though their drivers need no CDL.

### A4. Minimum insurance for interstate trucking (49 CFR 387.9; applicability 387.3)
- FACT: A for-hire motor carrier hauling general (nonhazardous) freight across state lines in a vehicle rated 10,001 pounds or more must carry at least $750,000 in public liability coverage. Higher minimums apply to hazardous cargo. The minimum is $1,000,000 for oil and most listed hazardous materials. It is $5,000,000 for certain bulk hazardous substances and explosives, poison-inhalation (Zone A) materials, and highway-route-controlled radioactive material. These are minimums, not caps on what a victim can recover.
- SOURCE: 49 CFR 387.9, Table 1 (amended as recently as 91 FR 45660, July 21, 2026, with amounts unchanged), and 49 CFR 387.3. eCFR API XML, part 387. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "(1) For-hire (In interstate or foreign commerce, with a gross vehicle weight rating of 10,001 or more pounds) | Property (nonhazardous) | $750,000"; 387.3(c)(1): "The rules in this subpart do not apply to a motor vehicle that has a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) of less than 10,001 pounds" (hazmat exceptions follow).
- TRAPS: (1) The $750,000 figure applies to for-hire interstate carriers of general freight. It is not a requirement for "every truck." Private carriers of their own nonhazardous goods and purely intrastate carriers fall outside that row. (2) Do not call $750,000 a "cap" or imply it is all that is available. Many carriers carry more, but no specific figure is verified here. (3) The $1M and $5M tiers depend on the cargo, not the truck size.

### A5. Post-accident drug and alcohol testing (49 CFR 382.303; scope 382.103)
- FACT: After a crash on a public road involving a commercial motor vehicle, the employer must test each surviving driver as soon as practicable when anyone died. When the crash caused an injury treated away from the scene, or caused disabling damage that required a tow, testing is required only if the truck driver received a citation for a moving violation. The citation must come within 8 hours for the alcohol test and within 32 hours for the drug test. If the alcohol test is not given within 2 hours, the employer must document why. It must stop trying after 8 hours. The drug test cutoff is 32 hours. Part 382 covers drivers who must hold a CDL.
- SOURCE: 49 CFR 382.303(a)–(d); 382.103(a). eCFR API XML, part 382. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "(a)(1) ... if the accident involved the loss of human life; or (2) Who receives a citation within 8 hours of the occurrence ... if the accident involved: (i) Bodily injury to any person who, as a result of the injury, immediately receives medical treatment away from the scene of the accident; or (ii) One or more motor vehicles incurring disabling damage as a result of the accident, requiring the motor vehicle to be transported away from the scene by a tow truck"; "(b)(2) Who receives a citation within thirty-two hours"; "(d)(1) If a test required by this section is not administered within two hours ... the employer shall prepare and maintain on file a record stating the reasons ... If a test ... is not administered within eight hours ... the employer shall cease attempts to administer an alcohol test"; "(d)(2) ... not administered within 32 hours ... the employer shall cease attempts."
- TRAPS: (1) Not every truck crash triggers testing. Without a death, testing depends on a citation plus an injury treated away from the scene or a tow-away. (2) Test results from law enforcement can satisfy the rule under 382.303(g). (3) Part 382 applies to CDL-required operations (382.103), not to every 10,001-lb CMV.

### A6. Driver qualification files and maintenance records (49 CFR 391.51; 396.3; 396.11; 396.21)
- FACT: A carrier must keep a driver qualification file for each driver while the driver is employed and for three years after. Some items, such as annual driving-record reviews and medical certificates, may be removed three years after they were created. Inspection, repair and maintenance records must be kept for 1 year where the vehicle is housed or maintained, and for 6 months after the vehicle leaves the carrier's control. Driver vehicle inspection reports must be kept for three months. Periodic (annual) inspection reports must be kept for fourteen months.
- SOURCE: 49 CFR 391.51(a)–(d) (eCFR API XML part 391; HTML https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-391.51 confirmed via WebFetch). 49 CFR 396.3(b)–(c) (eCFR API XML part 396; HTML https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-396.3 confirmed via WebFetch; amended 91 FR 45662, July 21, 2026). 49 CFR 396.11(a)(4) and 396.21(b)(1) (eCFR API XML part 396). Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 391.51(c): "each driver's qualification file shall be retained for as long as a driver is employed by that motor carrier and for three years thereafter." 396.3(c): "retained where the vehicle is either housed or maintained for a period of 1 year and for 6 months after the motor vehicle leaves the motor carrier's control." 396.11(a)(4): "for three months from the date the written report was prepared." 396.21(b)(1): "for a period of fourteen months from the date of the inspection report."
- TRAPS: (1) The retention periods differ by record type: 6 months for logs, 3 months for DVIRs, 1 year for maintenance, 14 months for annual inspection reports, and employment plus 3 years for DQ files. Do not write one blanket number. (2) The DQ file contents list in 391.51(b) contains date-limited provisions (e.g., medical certification items "through June 22, 2025"). Do not describe the medical-certificate paperwork in detail without rechecking.

### A7. South Carolina applies the FMCSRs to intrastate trucks of 26,001 lb or more
- FACT: South Carolina requires commercial vehicles of 26,001 pounds or more (by weight or weight rating) that operate only within the state, and their drivers, to meet the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations. The exceptions are those made by law or by SC Department of Public Safety regulation. Vehicles under 26,001 pounds are exempt unless they carry 16 or more passengers or placarded hazardous materials. The State Transport Police Division of SCDPS has exclusive authority in South Carolina to enforce the commercial motor vehicle carrier laws, including the FMCSRs, the hazardous materials rules, and size and weight laws.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 56-5-4240 (2012 Act No. 180); S.C. Code 56-5-4010(B); S.C. Code 58-23-1120. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php and https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t58c023.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 56-5-4240(A): "a commercial motor vehicle (CMV) and its driver operating in intrastate commerce with a gross vehicle weight (GVW), gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR), gross combination weight (GCW), and gross combination weight rating (GCWR) equal to or exceeding 26,001 pounds must meet the requirements of the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations, as enforced exclusively by the State Transport Police Division of the Department of Public Safety." (B): "CMVs operating below 26,001 pounds are exempt from the regulations cited in subsection (A)." (C): not exempt regardless of weight if "(1) designed or used to transport sixteen or more passengers, including the driver; or (2) used in the transportation of hazardous materials and is required to be placarded."
- TRAPS: (1) The SC intrastate threshold (26,001 lb) is higher than the federal interstate threshold (10,001 lb). A local SC delivery truck between 10,001 and 26,000 lb that never crosses state lines is generally outside the FMCSRs under 56-5-4240(B). (2) The statute allows "Except as otherwise provided by law or through regulations promulgated by the Department of Public Safety." The SCDPS regulations themselves were not reviewed, so do not describe specific intrastate variances. (3) The agency is the "State Transport Police Division" of SCDPS, not "SC Highway Patrol."

### A8. South Carolina load-securement statute (S.C. Code 56-5-4100)
- FACT: A vehicle may not be driven on a South Carolina highway unless it is built or loaded to keep its load from dropping, sifting, leaking or escaping. Since 2024, the statute also expressly requires that "any load and any covering" be "securely fastened." Trucks hauling rock, gravel, stone or similar material must meet freeboard limits (6 inches below the tops of all four walls) or be covered. A driver whose cargo falls onto the road must make every reasonable effort to have it cleaned up, and the owner, driver or carrier bears removal costs. The statute says it does not bar a damages claim. A violation of subsections (B)–(E) is a misdemeanor with a $100 fine.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 56-5-4100 (as amended by 2024 Act No. 202, eff. May 21, 2024). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "(A) No vehicle may be driven or moved on any public highway unless the vehicle is so constructed or loaded as to prevent any of its load from dropping, sifting, leaking, or otherwise escaping from the vehicle"; "(D) No person shall operate on any highway any vehicle with any load unless such load and any covering thereon is securely fastened so as to prevent such covering or load from becoming loose, detached, or in any manner a hazard to other users of the highway"; "Nothing in this section bars a claim for damages."
- TRAPS: (1) Subsection (G) exempts several cargo types from (A)–(E): seed cotton, soybeans, tobacco, poultry, livestock or silage and similar feed, paper and wastepaper, "forest products," and textile products. Do not use this statute for a log-truck or poultry-truck load claim without noting the exemption. Federal cargo securement rules in 49 CFR Part 393 were not reviewed here. (2) Subsection (D) is new in 2024. Older summaries will not include it.

## B. Premises liability in South Carolina

### B1. Duties owed to invitees, licensees and trespassers
- FACT: South Carolina sorts people who come onto property into four groups: adult trespassers, invitees, licensees and children. The duty owed depends on the group. An invitee, such as a store customer or a worker there on business, is owed reasonable care, including a warning about hidden dangers the owner knows or should know about. A licensee, such as a social guest, is owed only reasonable care in the owner's activities and a warning about concealed dangers the owner actually knows of. An adult trespasser is owed only the duty not to be injured willfully or wantonly. A utility meter reader on the property is an invitee, not a licensee.
- SOURCE: Sims v. Giles, 343 S.C. 708, 541 S.E.2d 857 (Ct. App. 2001), Op. No. 3291, filed Jan. 29, 2001. https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/3291.htm. Reporter cite confirmed in Estate of Adair v. L-J, Inc., Op. No. 4201 (S.C. Ct. App. filed Jan. 29, 2007), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4201.htm. Larimore v. Carolina Power & Light, 340 S.C. 438, 531 S.E.2d 535 (Ct. App. 2000), Op. No. 3152, filed Apr. 17, 2000. https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/3152.htm (cite confirmed in Sims). Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: Sims: "South Carolina recognizes four general classifications of persons who come on premises: adult trespassers, invitees, licensees, and children." "The owner of property owes to an invitee or business visitor the duty of exercising reasonable or ordinary care for his safety, and is liable for injuries resulting from the breach of such duty. ... The landowner has a duty to warn an invitee only of latent or hidden dangers of which the landowner has knowledge or should have knowledge." Licensee (quoting Neil v. Byrum): "(a) To use reasonable care to discover him and avoid injury to him in carrying on activities upon the land. (b) To use reasonable care to warn him of any concealed dangerous conditions or activities which are known to the possessor." "Under the totality of the relationship, the meter reader is a business invitee." Adair: "Because Adair was a trespasser, Respondents owed him no duty except the duty not to do him willful or wanton injury" (citing Nettles v. Your Ice Co., 191 S.C. 429 (1939)).
- TRAPS: (1) Social guests are licensees in SC, not invitees. (2) An invitee is not owed "the highest duty" in the sense of insurance. SC courts repeatedly hold that an owner "is not an insurer" of an invitee's safety (Larimore n.16; Wintersteen; Garvin). (3) Children are a separate category. Attractive nuisance is a distinct doctrine (see Clea v. Odom in C1). (4) Sims calls the invitee duty "the utmost duty of care." Do not turn that into "strict liability."

### B2. Slip-and-fall notice rule and rejection of "mode of operation" or "duty to prevent" liability
- FACT: To win a slip-and-fall case against a store over a foreign substance on the floor, the injured person must prove one of two things. Either the store (or its employees) put the substance there, or the store had actual or constructive notice it was on the floor and failed to remedy it. The South Carolina Supreme Court refused to make stores liable just because spills were foreseeable from how they display or sell goods. A display-related injury case also fails without evidence that the store created a dangerous condition or was on notice of it.
- SOURCE: Wintersteen v. Food Lion, Inc., 344 S.C. 32, 542 S.E.2d 728 (2001), Op. No. 25254, filed Feb. 26, 2001 (reporter cite from the CourtListener API search result, fetched 2026-09-28). https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/25254.htm. Garvin v. Bi-Lo, Inc., 343 S.C. 625, 541 S.E.2d 831 (2001), Op. No. 25245 (S.C. filed Feb. 5, 2001). https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/25245.htm. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: Wintersteen: "To recover damages for injuries caused by a dangerous or defective condition on a storekeeper's premises, the plaintiff must show either (1) that the injury was caused by a specific act of the defendant which created the dangerous condition; or (2) that the defendant had actual or constructive knowledge of the dangerous condition and failed to remedy it." "We decline to depart from traditional foreign substance analysis: a storekeeper is only liable if it places the substance on the floor, or if it has actual or constructive notice thereof." "To require shopkeepers to anticipate and prevent the acts of third parties is, in effect, to render them insurers of their customers' safety. This is simply not the law of this state." Garvin: "Absent evidence of some defective manner of stacking the boxes, or that Bi-Lo was on notice that the stacked cans had become rickety, there is simply no evidence from which a jury could find a dangerous condition was created by Bi-Lo."
- TRAPS: (1) Wintersteen's own words are "mode of display" and "duty to prevent"/"foreseeability." It does not use the phrase "mode of operation." Page copy should say the Court refused to base store liability on foreseeability or display method, rather than quote "mode of operation." (2) Garvin's full cite is 343 S.C. 625, 541 S.E.2d 831 (2001), confirmed as quoted in Easterling v. Burger King Corp., Op. No. 5404 (S.C. Ct. App. filed May 18, 2016), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5404.pdf. (3) Constructive notice can be shown through the store's own inspection routine. In O'Leary-Payne v. R.R. Hilton Head II, Inc., Op. No. 4173 (S.C. Ct. App. filed Oct. 30, 2006), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4173.htm, the court said: "because Charter should have observed the rod during an inspection, it had constructive notice." The court defined constructive notice as "a legal inference which substitutes for actual notice."

### B3. Open and obvious conditions and comparative negligence
- FACT: A South Carolina property owner generally has no duty to warn of an open and obvious condition. SC follows Restatement (Second) of Torts §343A: an owner can still be liable for an obvious danger if the owner should have anticipated the harm anyway, for example where visitors are likely to be distracted. Separately, South Carolina uses modified comparative negligence. An injured person can recover if their own negligence is not greater than the defendant's, and knowingly encountering a risk does not by itself bar recovery. In Davenport, a tenant who knew the stairway lights had been out for weeks still got to a jury. His fault was to be compared with the property owner's.
- SOURCE: Larimore v. Carolina Power & Light (above). Sims v. Giles (above), discussing Callander v. Charleston Doughnut Corp., 305 S.C. 123 (1991). Davenport v. Cotton Hope Plantation Horizontal Property Regime, 333 S.C. 71, 508 S.E.2d 565 (1998), Op. No. 24850, filed Nov. 9, 1998. https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/24850.htm (cite confirmed in O'Leary-Payne, Op. No. 4173). Nelson v. Concrete Supply Co., 303 S.C. 243, 399 S.E.2d 783 (1991), as quoted in Davenport. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: Larimore: "A landowner generally does not owe a duty to warn others of open and obvious conditions on the property. Our supreme court, however, held in Callander ... that a landowner may still be liable for injuries suffered from an open and obvious defect if the landowner should have anticipated the harm." §343A(1) as adopted: "A possessor of land is not liable to his invitees for physical harm caused to them by any activity or condition on the land whose danger is known or obvious to them, unless the possessor should anticipate the harm despite such knowledge or obviousness." Davenport: "[f]or all causes of action arising on or after July 1, 1991, a plaintiff in a negligence action may recover damages if his or her negligence is not greater than that of the defendant" (quoting Nelson). "We therefore hold that a plaintiff is not barred from recovery by the doctrine of assumption of risk unless the degree of fault arising therefrom is greater than the negligence of the defendant." "In the light most favorable to Davenport, it could be reasonably concluded that Davenport's negligence in proceeding down the stairway did not exceed Cotton Hope's negligence. Thus, it is properly submitted for jury determination."
- TRAPS: (1) Do not say open-and-obvious is "abolished" in SC. Larimore still applied it to defeat a claim. (2) Do not say an obvious hazard always bars recovery. Callander/§343A and Davenport's comparative-fault approach can let the case go to a jury. (3) The line is "not greater than": a plaintiff exactly 50% at fault can recover (reduced), and more than 50% recovers nothing. (4) Davenport kept express assumption of risk (such as a signed waiver) and primary implied assumption of risk intact. (5) Davenport's change applies to causes of action arising after Nov. 9, 1998 (O'Leary-Payne). This is irrelevant for new cases, but keep it off any "history" copy unless verified.

### B4. Landlord duties under the SC Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, and landlord injury liability
- FACT: Under the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, an SC residential landlord must: comply with building and housing codes that materially affect health and safety; make repairs and keep the premises fit and habitable; keep all common areas reasonably safe (and reasonably clean if there are more than four units); supply running water, reasonable hot water and reasonable heat; and keep electrical, gas, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air-conditioning and other supplied facilities and appliances, including elevators, in reasonably good and safe working order. A tenant can recover actual damages for the landlord's noncompliance, plus attorney's fees if it is willful. Before the Act, SC law traditionally said a landlord owed no duty to maintain leased premises in a safe condition. SC courts now recognize a negligence claim under the Act for a landlord's failure, after notice, to make necessary repairs. The Act's "fit and habitable" duty concerns the physical condition of the premises. On common areas, the SC Supreme Court held a landlord could face negligence liability for a tenant's known-vicious dog kept in a common area the landlord controlled.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 27-40-440, 27-40-510, 27-40-610, 27-40-630. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php. Robinson v. Code, Op. No. 4564 (S.C. Ct. App. filed June 12, 2009), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4564.htm. Nedrow v. Pruitt, Op. No. 3045 (S.C. Ct. App. filed Sept. 13, 1999), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/3045.htm. Fair v. United States, 334 S.C. 321, 513 S.E.2d 616 (1999), Op. No. 24905, filed Feb. 22, 1999, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/24905.htm (cite confirmed in Clea). Clea v. Odom, Op. No. 27029 (S.C. filed Aug. 22, 2011), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27029.htm. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 27-40-440(a): "(1) comply with the requirements of applicable building and housing codes materially affecting health and safety; (2) make all repairs and do whatever is reasonably necessary to put and keep the premises in a fit and habitable condition; (3) keep all common areas of the premises in a reasonably safe condition, and, for premises containing more than four dwelling units, keep in a reasonably clean condition; ... (5) maintain in reasonably good and safe working order and condition all electrical, gas, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air conditioning, and other facilities and appliances, including elevators, supplied or required to be supplied by him." 27-40-610(b): "the tenant may recover actual damages and obtain injunctive relief in a magistrate's or circuit court, without posting bond, for any noncompliance by the landlord with the rental agreement or SECTION 27-40-440. If the landlord's noncompliance is wilful, the tenant may recover reasonable attorney's fees." Robinson: "'Traditionally, under the law of South Carolina, a landlord owes no duty to maintain leased premises in a safe condition.' Young v. Morrisey, 285 S.C. 236, 239, 329 S.E.2d 426, 428 (1985)." The Act "creates a cause of action for a tenant of residential property against the landlord, 'for failure, after notice, to make necessary repairs and to do what is reasonably necessary to keep the premises in a habitable condition.'" Nedrow: "Negligence actions, such as this one, may be brought under the RLTA." "the RLTA placed an absolute duty on Pruitt to fix the heater." Clea: "the landlord could be liable where the attack occurred in a common area. There was evidence respondent had actual knowledge of the dog's vicious propensity."
- TRAPS: (1) Notice is central. Tenant remedies under 27-40-630(d) "do not arise until he has given notice to the landlord and the landlord fails to act within a reasonable time," and Robinson treats notice as part of the claim. Do not imply a landlord is liable for any defect it did not know about. (2) Smoke detectors: S.C. Code 5-25-1330 makes the rental owner responsible for supplying them, but 5-25-1380 says failure to comply "does not create a cause of action for a per se statutory violation for liability, or for negligence-based liability" (https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t05c025.php; applied in Robinson). (3) 27-40-630(c): the Act does not authorize tenant repair-and-deduct for repairs; it covers only "essential services." (4) The Act applies to residential tenancies. Commercial leases are outside it. (5) The Act's attorney-fee provisions are for tenants against landlords. Do not promise fee-shifting in injury cases.

### B5. Negligent security: businesses versus residential landlords
- FACT: A business owner in South Carolina must take reasonable action to protect its invitees against foreseeable criminal acts. SC decides foreseeability and the extent of that duty with a balancing test: the more foreseeable the crime, the more security is required. Prior crimes on the property are an important factor but not a requirement. Residential landlords are different. The general rule is still that a landlord owes tenants no affirmative duty to provide security against third-party crime. A landlord who voluntarily undertakes to provide security, such as courtesy officers, can be liable if it performs that undertaking negligently. In Wright v. PRG Real Estate Management (2019), the SC Supreme Court sent such a case to a jury.
- SOURCE: Bass v. Gopal, Inc., 395 S.C. 129, 716 S.E.2d 910 (2011), Op. No. 27054 (S.C. filed Oct. 10, 2011) (motel), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27054.htm. Wright v. PRG Real Estate Mgmt., Inc., Op. No. 27868 (S.C. filed Mar. 20, 2019), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27868.pdf. Jackson v. Swordfish Invs., L.L.C., Op. No. 26037 (S.C. filed Sept. 6, 2005), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26037.htm. Cramer v. Balcor Prop. Mgmt., Inc., 312 S.C. 440, 441 S.E.2d 317 (1994), as quoted in Wright and Jackson. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: Bass: "a business owner has a duty to take reasonable action to protect its invitees against the foreseeable risk of physical harm." "Under this test, the presence or absence of prior criminal incidents is a significant factor in determining the amount of security required of a business owner, but their absence does not foreclose the duty to provide some level of security if other factors support a heightened risk." "In replacing our imminent harm test with a balancing test ..." Wright: "It is well-settled in South Carolina that a landlord generally does not owe an affirmative duty to a tenant to provide security in and around leased premises to protect the tenant from the criminal activity of third parties." "A landlord generally has no duty to provide security to protect tenants from criminal acts of third parties, but a landlord who voluntarily undertakes to provide security measures may be liable if he negligently performs the undertaking." "we hold that under the narrow facts of this case, a jury must resolve the unique factual questions pertinent to the existence of a duty under section 323."
- TRAPS: (1) Bass v. Gopal is a motel/business-invitee case. Do not present its balancing test as the rule for apartment complexes. The residential-landlord rule is Cramer, as reaffirmed in Wright ("That is still undoubtedly the law in South Carolina"). (2) Lighting and landscaping alone do not create a duty. Wright n.3 says: "The court of appeals correctly found summary judgment was appropriate in the limited context of Respondents' actions involving the lighting and shrubbery at Wellspring. These actions do not give rise to a duty to provide security." The jury question came from the complex telling the tenant "that there are security officers" and from its newsletter saying "[s]ecurity is also [a] very top priority with us." Wright also says: "Section 323 is the standard in South Carolina when analyzing voluntarily assumed duties." (3) Bass affirmed summary judgment for the motel. A county-wide crime rate alone was "not ... sufficient to prove foreseeability." (4) Jackson v. Swordfish: a commercial landlord had no duty for a shooting inside leased premises it did not control. The Court left open a commercial landlord's duty in areas it controls. (5) Bass's full cite is 395 S.C. 129, 716 S.E.2d 910 (2011), confirmed as quoted in Easterling (COA Op. No. 5404) and Lord v. D & J Enterprises, Inc., Op. No. 27376 (S.C. filed Apr. 9, 2014), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27376.pdf. (6) Bass cuts both ways. Bass affirmed summary judgment for the motel. In Lord, a check-cashing customer shot in the store, the Court applied the Bass balancing approach and reversed summary judgment "because we find Gopal II applies to the facts of the instant case and its application warranted the denial of D & J's motion for summary judgment."

### B6. Falls or crashes on public property: SC Tort Claims Act points
- FACT: Claims against SC state agencies, counties, cities and other political subdivisions fall under the SC Tort Claims Act, which has its own rules. A suit must generally be filed within two years after the loss was or should have been discovered. The deadline is three years if a verified claim was first filed with the agency, and a claim that is filed must be filed within one year. Recovery is capped at $300,000 per person and $600,000 per occurrence (higher for claims against government-employed physicians and dentists). No punitive damages or prejudgment interest are allowed. Government entities are not liable for defects in highways, roads, streets, bridges or other public ways caused by a third party, or for missing or malfunctioning signs, signals and guardrails, unless the responsible entity fails to correct the problem within a reasonable time after actual or constructive notice. They are not liable for highway design. For parks and playgrounds, the entity must have had actual notice.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-78-60(8), (10), (15), (16); 15-78-80(a), (d); 15-78-110; 15-78-120(a)–(b). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c078.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 15-78-110: "any action brought pursuant to this chapter is forever barred unless an action is commenced within two years after the date the loss was or should have been discovered; provided, that if the claimant first filed a claim pursuant to this chapter then the action ... is forever barred unless the action is commenced within three years." 15-78-120(a)(1): "no person shall recover ... a sum exceeding three hundred thousand dollars because of loss arising from a single occurrence." (a)(2): "shall not exceed six hundred thousand dollars." (b): "No award for damages under this chapter shall include punitive or exemplary damages or interest prior to judgment." 15-78-60(15): "Governmental entities are not liable for the design of highways and other public ways ... are not liable for loss arising out of a defect or a condition in, on, under, or overhanging a highway, road, street, causeway, bridge, or other public way caused by a third party unless the defect or condition is not corrected by the particular governmental entity responsible for the maintenance within a reasonable time after actual or constructive notice." (16) (parks and playgrounds): "within a reasonable time after actual notice of the defect or condition." (8): no liability for "snow or ice conditions or temporary or natural conditions on any public way ... due to weather conditions unless the snow or ice thereon is affirmatively caused by a negligent act of the employee."
- TRAPS: (1) The general 3-year PI statute of limitations does NOT apply to Tort Claims Act cases. It is 2 years (3 with a filed claim). This is the most common error. (2) Minors' tolling in 15-3-40 does apply to Tort Claims Act actions: 15-78-110 opens with "Except as provided for in Section 15-3-40," and 15-3-40 expressly covers "an action under Chapter 78." (3) Sidewalks are not named in 15-78-60(15). The item says "other public way." Do not quote it as a sidewalk rule. (4) The $300,000/$600,000 caps are per the statute text as of this check. Do not add inflation adjustments.

## C. Dog bites

### C1. S.C. Code 47-3-110, the dog bite statute
- FACT: In South Carolina, a dog's owner, or anyone with the dog in their care or keeping, is liable for damages when the dog bites or attacks someone. The injured person must be in a public place or lawfully in a private place, including the owner's own property. A person is lawfully on private property when there performing a legal duty (for example, a mail carrier under postal regulations) or by express or implied invitation of the owner, a lawful tenant or a resident. The statute does not apply when the victim provoked or harassed the dog and that provocation was the proximate cause of the attack. It also does not apply to qualifying police dogs acting on a lawful command under a written agency policy. That exemption is lost if the handler or dog violates the policy, uses excessive force, or the bite is on a third-party bystander. The SC Supreme Court describes the statute as imposing strict liability on the owner. A landlord or property owner who is not the dog's owner is liable under the statute only if it controlled the premises and took on the dog's care or keeping.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 47-3-110 (1986 Act No. 343; 2013 Act No. 62). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t47c003.php. Clea v. Odom, Op. No. 27029 (S.C. filed Aug. 22, 2011), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27029.htm, quoting Harris v. Anderson County Sheriff's Office, 381 S.C. 357, 673 S.E.2d 423 (2009). Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 47-3-110(A): "If a person is bitten or otherwise attacked by a dog while the person is in a public place or is lawfully in a private place, including the property of the dog owner or person having the dog in the person's care or keeping, the dog owner or person having the dog in the person's care or keeping is liable for the damages suffered by the person bitten or otherwise attacked." (B)(1): "the person who was attacked provoked or harassed the dog and that provocation was the proximate cause of the attack." (B)(2)(f): "the attack or bite does not occur on a third party bystander." Clea: "Our state's 'dog bite' statute imposes strict liability against the owner of the dog or any other person having the dog in its care or keeping." Harris, as quoted: "First, the dog owner is strictly liable and common law principles are not implicated. Second, a property owner is liable when he exercises control over, and assumes responsibility for, the care and keeping of the dog. Third, a property owner is not liable under the statute when he has no control of the premises and provides no care or keeping of the dog."
- TRAPS: (1) SC has no "one bite" rule for owners under the statute. Prior viciousness is not required. (2) Trespassers are not covered. The victim must be in a public place or lawfully on private property. (3) Landlords: the general common-law rule is that a landlord is not liable for a tenant's dog on leased property (Clea, citing Gilbert v. Miller, 356 S.C. 25 (Ct. App. 2003), and Bruce v. Durney, 341 S.C. 563 (Ct. App. 2000); Bruce at https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/3208.htm). The Clea common-area exception requires evidence of the landlord's control plus knowledge. (4) The police-dog exception has six conditions and does not cover bystanders.

### C2. Dangerous animal provisions (S.C. Code 47-3-710 through 47-3-770)
- FACT: A "dangerous animal" under SC law is a dog or cat that meets one of three tests. The owner knows or should know it tends to attack unprovoked or endanger people or domestic animals. Or it made an unprovoked attack causing bodily injury, or acted in a way that reasonably caused a person to believe it would attack, off its required confinement. Or it is kept or trained for fighting. "Bodily injury" means broken bones, lacerations, skin punctures, or injury resulting in death. No animal is dangerous solely because of its breed. The owner must keep a dangerous animal securely confined (indoors, or in a secure, locked, marked pen) and safely restrained off the premises. The owner must register it with the county's local law enforcement authority, with proof of at least $50,000 in liability insurance or a surety bond. An owner whose dangerous animal makes an unprovoked attack that injures a person away from its required confinement (47-3-710(A)(2)(a)) commits a misdemeanor on a first offense (up to $5,000 or 3 years) and a felony on a second or later offense (up to $10,000 or 5 years). A person found guilty must pay the victim's medical expenses from the attack, among other costs. The article states that it does not abrogate civil remedies.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 47-3-710, 47-3-720, 47-3-730, 47-3-750, 47-3-760, 47-3-770 (Article 13, "Regulation of Dangerous Animals"). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t47c003.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 47-3-710(C): "An animal is not a 'dangerous animal' solely by virtue of its breed or species." (E): "'injury' or 'bodily injury' means (1) broken bones, (2) lacerations, (3) punctures of the skin, or (4) any physical injury resulting in death." 47-3-760(B): "(1) for a first offense, is guilty of a misdemeanor and, upon conviction, must be fined not more than five thousand dollars or imprisoned not more than three years; (2) for a second or subsequent offense, is guilty of a felony and, upon conviction, must be fined not more than ten thousand dollars or imprisoned not more than five years." (D): "A person found guilty of violating this article shall pay all expenses, including ... medical expenses incurred by a victim from an attack by a dangerous animal." (E): "proof of liability insurance or surety bond of at least fifty thousand dollars." (F): "Nothing in this chapter is designed to abrogate any civil remedies available under statutory or common law."
- TRAPS: (1) The dangerous-animal article excludes an animal that attacks a trespasser (47-3-710(B)(2)) and animals used exclusively for agricultural purposes. (2) The $50,000 insurance requirement applies only to animals that qualify as "dangerous" and must be registered. It is not a general dog-owner insurance mandate. (3) No breed bans exist at the state level under this article. Local ordinances were not checked.

### C3. Reporting a bite and the 10-day rabies quarantine
- FACT: A physician who treats an animal bite must report it to the county health department by the end of the next working day. If no physician treats the bite, the bitten adult, or the parent or guardian of a bitten child, must report it by the end of the next working day. The health department must order the owner of a dog, cat or ferret that bit someone to quarantine it, at the owner's expense, for at least ten days, with examinations permitted during that period. The statute still names DHEC. DHEC was abolished effective July 1, 2024, and its health programs were transferred to the new SC Department of Public Health (DPH). DPH's rabies page now directs animal bite reports to its county offices during business hours and to 1-888-847-0902 (option 2) nights, weekends and holidays.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 47-5-20(2), 47-5-90, 47-5-100, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t47c005.php. 2023 Act No. 60 §§ 1, 2, 14(C), 17 (editor's note to Title 44 Chapter 1), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t44c001.php. SC DPH, Rabies, https://dph.sc.gov/diseases-conditions/infectious-diseases/animal-borne-diseases/rabies. SC DPH, Laws and Regulations: Rabies, https://dph.sc.gov/professionals/permits-regulations/dph-laws-and-regulations-rabies. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 47-5-90: "Every physician after his first professional attendance upon a person bitten by a pet or other animal, by the end of the next working day, shall report the bite to the county health department ... If no physician attends to the bite, it is the responsibility of the bitten adult or the parent or guardian of a bitten minor child to report the bite by the end of the next working day to the county health department." 47-5-100: "to quarantine the animal at the expense of the owner upon his premises or at an animal shelter or other place designated in the notice for at least ten days after the animal has attacked or bitten a person." Act 60 §1: "The South Carolina Department of Health and Environmental Control and the South Carolina Board of Health and Environmental Control are abolished." Act 60 §14(C): references to renamed agencies "must be construed to mean appropriate references." DPH page: "Healthcare providers, make sure to report all animal bites to DPH." "To report an animal bite or incident between 8:30 a.m. - 5 p.m., find your county office ... On nights, weekends, or holidays, call 1-888-847-0902 (option 2)."
- TRAPS: (1) Do not say "report to DHEC." DHEC no longer exists. Say the SC Department of Public Health (DPH). (2) The 10-day minimum applies to dogs, cats and ferrets. For other animals, the department may order euthanasia and brain testing, or a quarantine under conditions it sets. (3) The quarantine can be at the owner's home "or at an animal shelter or other place designated in the notice." It is not always at a shelter. (4) The DPH 1-888 number is a live phone number. Confirm it before publishing, and never let it be confused with the firm's number.

### C4. Court approval of settlements for minors
- FACT: In South Carolina, a settlement of a child's injury claim over $25,000 (net to the child) must be approved by the circuit court, through a verified petition that states the settlement is in the child's best interests. The money is then paid through a conservator or under a probate court protective order. For claims of $25,000 or less, the circuit court or the probate court can approve. A conservator can settle those without court approval. For claims of $2,500 or less, a parent or guardian can settle without court approval and without a conservator.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 62-5-433 (as amended by 2017 Act No. 87 and 2024 Act No. 200, eff. May 21, 2024). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t62c005.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: (A)(1): "for any claim exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars in favor of or against any minor or incapacitated individual, 'court' means the circuit court of the county in which the minor or incapacitated individual resides or the circuit court in the county in which the suit is pending." (A)(2): "'Claim' means the net or actual amount accruing to or paid by the minor." (B)(1): "the verified petition must include a statement by the petitioner that, in his opinion, the proposed settlement is in the best interests of the minor." (B)(3): "The order authorizing the settlement must require that payment or delivery of the money or personal property be made through the conservator or pursuant to a protective order issued by the probate court." (D): "The settlement of a claim that does not exceed two thousand five hundred dollars ... may be effected by the parent or guardian ... without court approval of the settlement and without the appointment of a conservator."
- TRAPS: (1) The $25,000 threshold is measured on the net amount to the child, not the gross settlement. (2) The 2024 amendment rewrote (B)(3), so older summaries of payment mechanics are out of date. (3) Wrongful-death settlements follow a separate statute, 15-51-42 (D1).

### C5. Statute of limitations for minors (S.C. Code 15-3-40); age of majority
- FACT: When the person entitled to sue is under 18 when the claim arises, the time they are under 18 generally does not count toward the filing deadline. That extension is capped: it cannot push the deadline to more than one year after the disability ends, in practice one year after the child turns 18. The same tolling applies to Tort Claims Act suits against government entities.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-3-40 (1996 Act No. 234). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c003.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "If a person entitled to bring an action mentioned in Article 5 of this chapter or an action under Chapter 78 of this title ... is at the time the cause of action accrued either: (1) within the age of eighteen years; or (2) insane; the time of the disability is not a part of the time limited for the commencement of the action, except that the period within which the action must be brought cannot be extended: (a) more than five years by any such disability, except infancy; nor (b) in any case longer than one year after the disability ceases."
- TRAPS: (1) It is NOT "three years after the child turns 18." The extension cannot run past one year after the child's 18th birthday. (2) If the ordinary 3-year period (15-3-530(5)) would expire later than the child's 19th birthday (for example, a 17-year-old injured), the tolling adds nothing. Pages should say the child "may have" extra time and urge prompt review, not state a single date. (3) The statute tolls only the claim of the person under disability. Whether a parent's own claim for a child's medical bills is tolled was NOT verified. (4) The ordinary personal injury deadline is 3 years under 15-3-530(5), with discovery accrual under 15-3-535 ("within three years after the person knew or by the exercise of reasonable diligence should have known that he had a cause of action").

## D. Wrongful death and survival

### D1. SC Wrongful Death Act (S.C. Code 15-51-10 to 15-51-60)
- FACT: When someone's wrongful act, neglect or default causes a death, the responsible party is liable for damages if the person could have sued had they lived. The action is brought by the executor or administrator (the personal representative) for the family. Spouse and children come first. If there are none, parents. If there are none, heirs. Recovery is divided among those beneficiaries in intestacy shares. The probate court can reduce or deny a parent's share if the parent failed to support the decedent as a minor. The jury may award punitive ("exemplary") damages when the conduct was reckless, willful or malicious. Any wrongful death or survival settlement must be approved by a probate court, circuit court or federal district court. If no suit is pending, the personal representative petitions the probate or circuit court, and the court holds a hearing. Only a duly appointed personal representative can settle. Anyone filing a wrongful death or survival suit outside the probate court must notify the probate court within ten days.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-51-10, -20, -40, -41, -42, -50, -60. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c051.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 15-51-10: "Whenever the death of a person shall be caused by the wrongful act, neglect or default of another and the act, neglect or default is such as would, if death had not ensued, have entitled the party injured to maintain an action and recover damages in respect thereof, the person who would have been liable ... shall be liable to an action for damages." 15-51-20: "for the benefit of the wife or husband and child or children ... and, if there be no such wife, husband, child or children, then for the benefit of the parent or parents, and if there be none such, then for the benefit of the heirs ... Every such action shall be brought by or in the name of the executor or administrator of such person." 15-51-40: "the jury may give damages, including exemplary damages when the wrongful act, neglect, or default was the result of recklessness, wilfulness, or malice ... The amount so recovered shall be divided among the before-mentioned parties in those shares as they would have been entitled to if the deceased had died intestate." 15-51-41: "Any settlement of a wrongful death or survival action must be approved by either a probate court, circuit court, or United States District Court." 15-51-42(A): "Only a duly appointed personal representative ... shall have the authority to settle wrongful death or survival actions." 15-51-42(F): "must notify the probate court of this action within ten days after the filing of the action."
- TRAPS: (1) Family members do not file in their own names. The personal representative brings the case for them. (2) Beneficiaries are fixed by statute (spouse and children, then parents, then heirs), not by the decedent's will. (3) 15-51-42(A) cross-references "Section 62-1-201(30)" for the personal representative definition, but the current definition is numbered 62-1-201(33). Cite the concept, not the subsection number. (4) Whether wrongful death proceeds are shielded from the decedent's creditors is NOT verified (see NOT VERIFIED). The statute says the proceeds are divided "as if" personal assets of an intestate estate, and 15-51-42(B) requires the settlement petition to list "the appropriate creditors" (relevant to survival proceeds). Do not write that creditors can or cannot reach wrongful-death money.

### D2. Survival action (S.C. Code 15-5-90) and funeral expenses (15-5-100)
- FACT: A deceased person's own injury claims survive their death and are brought by the personal representative for the estate. Wrongful death damages benefit the family; survival damages benefit the estate. Survival damages can include the decedent's conscious pain and suffering before death. Reasonable funeral expenses can be recovered in either the survival or the wrongful death action, but in only one of them.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-5-90, 15-5-100, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c005.php. Carson v. CSX Transp., Inc., Op. No. 27186 (S.C. filed Nov. 7, 2012), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27186.pdf. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 15-5-90: "Causes of action for and in respect to ... any and all injuries to the person or to personal property shall survive both to and against the personal or real representative ... of a deceased person." 15-5-100: "Damages recoverable under either SECTIONS 15-5-90 or 15-51-10 may include reasonable funeral expenses, but such funeral expenses shall be sought in only one action." Carson: "Unlike damages in a wrongful death action, which are for the benefit of the decedent's family, damages in a survival action are for the benefit of the decedent's estate. ... a survival claim may only be filed by the personal representative of the decedent's estate." The Court also treated evidence "that Decedent experienced conscious pain and suffering before his death" together with the funeral and burial receipts as survival-action damages.
- TRAPS: (1) Funeral expenses belong in one action only, so pages should not list them as recoverable in both. (2) Medical expenses before death are commonly listed as survival damages. That is consistent with 15-5-90 covering "any and all injuries to the person," but no SC case quote enumerating medical bills was captured in session. Say "the decedent's own losses, such as conscious pain and suffering." (3) In Carson, a beneficiary's comparative negligence could not be imputed to the estate in the survival action.

### D3. Wrongful death deadline (S.C. Code 15-3-530(6))
- FACT: A South Carolina wrongful death action must be filed within three years, and the period runs from the date of death.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-3-530(6). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c003.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "(6) an action under Sections 15-51-10 to 15-51-60 for death by wrongful act, the period to begin to run upon the death of the person on account of whose death the action is brought."
- TRAPS: (1) The period runs from the date of death, not the date of the accident. (2) When a government entity is the defendant, the Tort Claims Act's 2-year period (15-78-110) controls (B6). (3) A survival claim is the decedent's own injury claim and follows the ordinary personal injury period (15-3-530(5)). Its interaction with the date of death was NOT verified, so do not state a separate survival deadline.

### D4. Appointing a personal representative through the probate court (S.C. Code Title 62, Article 3); Dorchester County Probate Court
- FACT: To act as personal representative, a person must be appointed by court order, qualify, and receive letters. Administration begins when the letters issue. The first proceeding is filed in the county where the decedent lived at death. Priority for appointment goes in this order: the person named in a probated will, the surviving spouse who is a devisee, other devisees, the surviving spouse, then other heirs. Creditors can seek appointment after 45 days. A person under 18 cannot serve. With limited exceptions, no appointment proceeding may begin more than ten years after the death. The SC Judicial Branch courthouse directory lists the Dorchester County Probate Judge at 5200 E Jim Bilton Blvd, St. George, SC 29477-8020, office (843) 832-0136.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 62-3-103, 62-3-108(A)(1), 62-3-201(a), 62-3-203(a), (e), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t62c003.php. S.C. Code 62-1-201(33), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t62c001.php. SC Judicial Branch Courthouse Search, Dorchester, https://www.sccourts.org/courts/courthouse-search/dorchester/. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 62-3-103: "to acquire the powers and undertake the duties and liabilities of a personal representative of a decedent, a person must be appointed by order of the court, qualify, and be issued letters. Administration of an estate is commenced by the issuance of letters." 62-3-201(a)(1): "in the county where the decedent had his domicile at the time of his death." 62-3-203(a): "(1) the person with priority as determined by a probated will ... (2) the surviving spouse of the decedent who is a devisee ... (3) other devisees ... (4) the surviving spouse ... (5) other heirs ... (6) forty-five days after the death of the decedent, any creditor." 62-3-203(e)(1): no person is qualified who is "under the age of eighteen." Directory: "Probate Judges ... 5200 E Jim Bilton Blvd, St George, SC 29477-8020, Office: (843) 832-0136."
- TRAPS: (1) The official county site (dorchestercountysc.gov) returned 403 to every fetch, so any Summerville satellite probate office was NOT verified. Use only the St. George address, or link the sccourts directory. (2) Do not name the sitting probate judge on firm pages. Tara Frost's approved bio includes Associate Probate Judge service in Dorchester County through June 2026, and naming the judge risks implying influence (project rule). (3) Wrongful death proceeds pass to statutory beneficiaries, but the personal representative is still needed to bring and settle the claim (15-51-20, 15-51-42).

### D5. Punitive damages in wrongful death, and SC punitive damages caps
- FACT: Punitive (exemplary) damages are available in a South Carolina wrongful death action when the wrongful act was reckless, willful or malicious. Punitive awards in SC civil actions are generally capped at the greater of three times compensatory damages or $500,000, adjusted for inflation. The cap rises to four times or $2,000,000 in certain cases. There is no cap when the court finds intent to harm, a related felony conviction or plea, or that the defendant was impaired by alcohol or non-prescribed drugs to the point judgment was substantially impaired.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-51-40, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c051.php. S.C. Code 15-32-530(A)–(D) (2011 Act No. 52, applies to actions accruing on or after Jan. 1, 2012), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c032.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 15-51-40: "including exemplary damages when the wrongful act, neglect, or default was the result of recklessness, wilfulness, or malice." 15-32-530(A): "an award of punitive damages may not exceed the greater of three times the amount of compensatory damages awarded to each claimant entitled thereto or the sum of five hundred thousand dollars." (C)(3): no cap when "the defendant acted or failed to act while under the influence of alcohol, drugs, other than lawfully prescribed drugs administered in accordance with a prescription ... to the degree that the defendant's judgment is substantially impaired."
- TRAPS: (1) The $500,000 figure is CPI-adjusted under 15-32-530(D), and the current adjusted amount was NOT verified. Say "a statutory cap tied to compensatory damages" or give the statute's base figures with "adjusted annually." (2) The DUI no-cap rule is useful for drunk-driving pages, but it must be the court's determination. Never promise punitive damages. (3) Punitive damages are unavailable against government entities under the Tort Claims Act (15-78-120(b)).

## E. Catastrophic injury: liens and caps

### E1. SC Medicaid's recovery rights against injury settlements
- FACT: When Medicaid pays for an injured person's care, the person is treated as having assigned to SC Department of Health and Human Services (SCDHHS) the right to recover those payments from a liable third party or insurer, up to the amount Medicaid paid. SCDHHS is also automatically subrogated to that extent. SCDHHS can intervene in the injury case, sue on its own, and settle or compromise its claim. When the recipient has a lawyer, SCDHHS may reduce what it is owed by 25% as its share of attorney's fees, and it may share in other litigation costs. Its assignment or subrogation right is superior to other reimbursement rights. A lawyer with written notice of the department's rights before disbursing funds can be liable to the department for improper disbursement.
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 43-7-410(B), 43-7-420(A), 43-7-430(A), 43-7-440(A)(1)–(6), (D). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t43c007.php. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 43-7-420(A): "An applicant or recipient, only to the extent of the amount of the medical assistance paid by Medicaid, is considered to have assigned his right to recover an amount paid by Medicaid from a third party or private insurer to the department." 43-7-430(A): "The department automatically is subrogated, only to the extent of the amount of medical assistance paid by Medicaid." 43-7-440(A)(6): "reduce an amount due to the department by twenty-five percent if the applicant or recipient has retained an attorney." 43-7-440(D): "An assignment or subrogation right of the department is superior to any right of reimbursement, subrogation, or indemnity of a third party or recipient."
- TRAPS: (1) The statute frames Medicaid's right as assignment and subrogation, not as a "lien." Use "Medicaid's right to be repaid" or "subrogation." (2) The 25% reduction is discretionary ("may"). (3) The department is SCDHHS, not DHEC or DPH.

### E2. General hospital lien statute in SC

This supports, but does not prove, the absence of a general hospital lien statute.
- SOURCE: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/title29.php; https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t29c015.php; scstatehouse.gov Code search. Checked 2026-09-28.
- TRAPS: Do not publish "South Carolina has no hospital lien law" on the strength of this search alone. If a page needs the point, say that repayment claims from health insurers, Medicaid and Medicare are common after an injury settlement (E1, E3), without addressing hospital liens.

### E3. Medicare Secondary Payer (42 U.S.C. 1395y(b)(2))
- FACT: When Medicare pays injury-related medical bills that a liability or auto insurer is responsible for, the payment is conditional, and Medicare must be reimbursed from the settlement, judgment or award.
- SOURCE: 42 U.S.C. 1395y(b)(2)(A), (B)(i)–(ii). https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title42-section1395y&num=0&edition=prelim. Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: "the term 'primary plan' means ... an automobile or liability insurance policy or plan (including a self-insured plan) or no fault insurance"; "Any such payment by the Secretary shall be conditioned on reimbursement to the appropriate Trust Fund"; "a primary plan, and an entity that receives payment from a primary plan, shall reimburse the appropriate Trust Fund for any payment made by the Secretary ... if it is demonstrated that such primary plan has or had a responsibility to make payment."
- TRAPS: Keep this to one line on pages. Interest after 60 days, double damages, and the website procedures (1395y(b)(2)(B)(ii)–(vii)) exist but are beyond page scope.

### E4. Damages caps: the noneconomic cap is a medical malpractice rule
- FACT: South Carolina's statutory cap on noneconomic damages (pain and suffering and similar losses) appears in the medical malpractice statute. It limits noneconomic damages in medical malpractice judgments to $350,000 per claimant against a single provider or institution, and $1,050,000 per claimant across all health care defendants. Both amounts are adjusted annually for inflation. The caps do not apply to gross negligence, recklessness, fraud or record tampering, and they do not limit economic damages. A search of the SC Code for "noneconomic damages" found this in Title 15, and one Title 44 hit (a provision referencing the controlled-substances chapter, 44-53, that lists recoverable damages; it was not opened). No general noneconomic cap for car, truck, premises or dog-bite negligence cases was found. Two other SC limits still matter in injury cases: the punitive damages cap (15-32-530, D5) and the Tort Claims Act caps on government defendants (15-78-120, B6).
- SOURCE: S.C. Code 15-32-210(9), 15-32-220(A)–(F) (2005 Act No. 32), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c032.php. scstatehouse.gov Code search, "noneconomic damages" and "non-economic damages" (2 matches each: Title 15, Title 44). Checked 2026-09-28.
- QUOTE: 15-32-220(A): "In an action on a medical malpractice claim when final judgment is rendered against a single health care provider, the limit of civil liability for noneconomic damages of the health care provider is limited to an amount not to exceed three hundred fifty thousand dollars for each claimant." (C): "the limit of civil liability for noneconomic damages for all health care institutions and health care providers is limited to an amount not to exceed one million fifty thousand dollars for each claimant." (D)(1): "The provisions of this section do not limit the amount of compensation for economic damages." (E): the limits "do not apply if the jury or court determines that the defendant was grossly negligent, wilful, wanton, or reckless."
- TRAPS: (1) Do not write "South Carolina has no damages caps." That is wrong because of the punitive cap (15-32-530) and the Tort Claims Act caps (15-78-120). Write "South Carolina's cap on pain-and-suffering damages applies to medical malpractice claims" instead. (2) The $350,000 and $1.05M figures are base amounts. The current CPI-adjusted amounts were NOT verified, so cite them as "base amounts, adjusted annually." (3) The Frost PI site has no med-mal practice area. Keep the cap mention to a single clarifying line.

## 3. SCDMV collision reports and the FR-10

- FACT: After a collision, you can request the official collision report from the SC Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV) online, at any SCDMV branch, or by mail using Form FR-50 (Request for Copy of Collision Report). The fee is $10.00 per report.
  - SOURCE: https://dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/collision-reports (HTTP 200). Form FR-50, Rev. 01/2026, https://dmv.sc.gov/sites/scdmv/files/2026-01/FR-50%20Request%20for%20Copy%20of%20Collision%20Report.pdf (HTTP 200), which says "The request fee is $10.00 per report." Checked 2026-09-28.

- FACT: Online requests need the requester's personal information first, then details that identify the collision. Collisions before March 22, 2012 cannot be requested online. Drivers with out-of-state licenses may not be able to use the online system and can call SCDMV at 803-896-5000, go to a branch, or mail the request.
  - SOURCE: same page. Checked 2026-09-28.
- FACT: Mail requests go with two copies of the FR-50 and a $10 check or money order to SCDMV, Titles Mail-in Unit FR-50, PO Box 1498, Blythewood, SC 29016-0050.
  - SOURCE: same page. Checked 2026-09-28.
- FACT: The Highway Patrol also directs people to SCDMV for a copy of the collision report, form TR-310.
  - SOURCE: https://scdps.sc.gov/schp/contact/troops, FAQ. Checked 2026-09-28.
- FACT, on the FR-10: The Highway Patrol calls the FR-10 the "mandatory insurance verification form". Anyone who loses one should contact the troop office where the incident happened.
  - SOURCE: https://scdps.sc.gov/schp/contact/troops, FAQ. Checked 2026-09-28.
- FACT: Under state law, the investigating officer gives the operator or owner a form to complete and verify liability insurance coverage. The completed and verified form "must be returned by the operator or owner to the Department of Motor Vehicles within fifteen days from the date the form was delivered by the officer". Failing to return it "is prima facie evidence that the vehicle was uninsured".
  - SOURCE: S.C. Code Section 56-9-350, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c009.php (HTTP 200). Checked 2026-09-28.
- FACT: If no law enforcement officer investigated a crash that caused injury, death or at least $1,000 of property damage, the driver or owner must file Form FR-309 (Traffic Collision Report, Not Investigated by Law Enforcement) with SCDMV within 15 days.
  - SOURCE: S.C. Code Section 56-5-1270, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php (HTTP 200). The Highway Patrol troops FAQ. Form FR-309, https://dmv.sc.gov/sites/scdmv/files/media/Forms/fr-309.pdf (HTTP 200). Checked 2026-09-28.
- FACT: A driver in a crash with injury or death must immediately notify the local police department if the crash is inside a municipality. Otherwise the driver notifies the county sheriff or the nearest Highway Patrol office.
  - SOURCE: S.C. Code Section 56-5-1260, same chapter page. Checked 2026-09-28.
- TRAPS:
  - **The statute never uses the name "FR-10".** The FR-10 name comes from SCDPS, and the FR-50 has an "FR-10 No." field. Section 56-9-350 describes the form.
  - **The two statutes use different dollar thresholds.** Section 56-9-350 says $400 of property damage and Section 56-5-1270 says $1,000. Do not print a dollar threshold for the FR-10. The 15-day return runs from the date the officer delivered the form, not from the crash date. The FR-309 15 days does run from the crash date.
  - Tell readers the driver or owner returns the FR-10. Do not say the insurer files it.
  - SCDMV does not give an online fee separately. The $10 is printed for mail and on the FR-50 form. Write "a $10 fee per report" without splitting it by channel.

Do not say "until the child reaches adulthood" without that limit (the live dog bite FAQ oversimplifies).
- **Claims against government: SC Tort Claims Act.** Section 15-78-110: two years from when the loss was or should have been discovered, or three years if a verified claim was filed first. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c078.php . Section 15-78-120: caps of $300,000 per person and $600,000 per occurrence, no punitive damages. This is the deadline and cap that competitor blogs omit when SCDOT, a county, a town or a school bus is involved.
- **Modified comparative negligence, 51% bar.** Nelson v. Concrete Supply Co., 303 S.C. 243, 399 S.E.2d 783 (1991): a plaintiff "may recover damages if his or her negligence is not greater than that of the defendant", with the award reduced by the plaintiff's share, for causes of action arising on or after July 1, 1991. https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1265650/nelson-v-concrete-supply-company/ . Exactly 50% still recovers; 51% or more is barred. Plaintiff fault is compared against the combined fault of all defendants.
- **Joint and several liability and the "empty chair", changed January 1, 2026.** Section 15-38-15 as amended by **2025 Act No. 42 (H.3430)**: a defendant under 50% of total fault pays only its share; the jury now apportions fault to nondefendant tortfeasors (disclosed within 180 days) and settling tortfeasors, with exceptions for wilful, wanton, reckless or intentional conduct and illegal drug conduct. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c038.php . **Most competitor content still describes the pre-2026 version.** Verify the accrual/filing-date applicability before relying on it for a specific crash date.
- **Minimum auto liability limits 25/50/25.** Section 38-77-140. **Uninsured motorist coverage is mandatory** at the same minimums, plus $25,000 property damage, Section 38-77-150. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c077.php . Carriers must offer underinsured motorist coverage up to the insured's liability limits, but buying it is optional (Section 38-77-160, same page).
- **Seat belt non-use is not evidence of negligence.** Section 56-5-6540(C): a violation "is not negligence per se or contributory negligence, and is not admissible as evidence in a civil action." https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php
- **Helmets required only under 21.** Section 56-5-3660 (same Title 56 Chapter 5 page). Riders 21 and older have no helmet mandate.
- **Crash reporting.** Section 56-5-1260: a driver in an injury or fatal crash must notify the local police department inside a municipality, otherwise the county sheriff or the nearest SC Highway Patrol office. Reports are requested from SCDMV online or with **Form FR-50**. https://dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/collision-reports
- **Dog bites, strict liability.** Section 47-3-110: the owner or keeper is liable when the person was in a public place or lawfully in a private place, including the owner's property; defenses are provocation or harassment, and police dogs on duty. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t47c003.php
- **Wrongful death and survival.** Section 15-51-10 (cause of action), 15-51-20 (brought by the executor or administrator for the spouse and children, then parents, then heirs). https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c051.php . Survival of the victim's own claim: Section 15-5-90. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c005.php
- **Punitive damages cap.** Section 15-32-530: generally the greater of three times compensatory damages or $500,000, rising to four times or $2 million in listed circumstances. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c032.php
- **Workers' comp (reference only, topic excluded).** Notice within 90 days, Section 42-15-20; claim within two years, Section 42-15-40. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t42c015.php
