Can an Injured Uber Rider Also Use a Personal Auto Policy?

Sometimes, depending on the coverage you bought. Underinsured motorist coverage, called UIM, pays when the at-fault driver's limits fall short of the harm. South Carolina insurers must offer UIM under Section 38-77-160, though buying it is optional. The rideshare statute itself requires no UIM on the Uber.

Your household policy usually treats you as a Class I insured. The Supreme Court of South Carolina used that label in its 1998 Concrete Services decision. Under the policy on someone else's car, a guest counts as Class II. Only Class I insureds may stack, meaning add UIM from more than one policy together. If none of your own cars was in the crash, the statute caps you at the UIM on a single one of them. Frost Law Group reads your declarations page before anyone counts on that money.

Fault Rules When a Pedestrian Meets an Uber at the Curb

Under Section 56-5-3230, drivers owe due care to avoid striking anyone on foot or on a bicycle. It also calls for proper precaution on seeing a child, or a person who is obviously confused, incapacitated or intoxicated. Pedestrians carry duties too. Anyone crossing outside a crosswalk must yield to traffic under Section 56-5-3150. Section 56-5-3130 separately bars darting off a curb in front of a close vehicle. A jury can divide fault between the two, and a walker whose share tops 50% recovers nothing.

Cyclists Near Uber Pickup and Drop-Off Spots

A Rear Door Opened Into a Cyclist's Path

When the Other Driver Has No Insurance or Leaves the Scene

Uninsured motorist coverage, or UM, pays for harm caused by a driver with no insurance. In some cases it also pays when no one can identify the driver. South Carolina makes UM mandatory under Section 38-77-150, and both rideshare tiers require it at that basic level. The exclusion can reach the driver's own UM and underinsured motorist coverage. It is permission, not a requirement. A Hit and Run Driver Under the 2024 Rewrite of Section 38-77-170

South Carolina rewrote its unknown-driver rules in 2024, effective May 20, 2024. To reach UM benefits when the at-fault driver stays unknown, the injured person must meet three conditions. Police must receive a report within a reasonable time, and a qualifying proof route must exist. Finally, the statute bars the claim if the injured person's own negligence explains why no one identified the other driver.

Contact, a Witness or the Uber Driver's Dashcam

The proof can be physical contact with the unknown car. It can also be an eyewitness who is neither the owner nor the driver of the insured car. That witness signs an affidavit carrying the statute's printed warning about false statements. Read literally, that description fits a rider in the back seat. A third route is video or another recording showing the unknown car caused the crash, such as a dashcam clip. The witness and recording routes are less clear in a lawsuit, because Section 38-77-180 still speaks of physical contact.

Calling Police From the Roadside

Section 38-77-170 sets no fixed hour count for the police report, only a reasonable time under all the circumstances. Separately, Section 56-5-1260 requires immediate notice of any injury crash. Inside Summerville that notice goes to the town police. Outside a town, the driver calls the county sheriff or the nearest State Highway Patrol office. A driver who calls police from the scene and asks the rider to note the fleeing car helps satisfy both rules.

Log-In and Acceptance Times in the Three-Year Trip Record

Will Uber Hand Over a Rider's Trip Data?

Not on request from just anyone. Section 58-23-1670 lets the company share a rider's or driver's personally identifiable information with outsiders in three situations.
