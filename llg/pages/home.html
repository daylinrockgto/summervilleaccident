Types of Cases Our Summerville Personal Injury Attorneys Represent

Every injury claim in South Carolina turns on two questions. Who was careless, and what did that carelessness cost you? Frost Law Group handles personal injury cases built on those questions. They range from highway wrecks to slips in a grocery aisle and dog attacks. The type of case decides which laws apply and which insurance policies may pay.

Fault does not have to be all or nothing. South Carolina uses a rule called modified comparative negligence. It compares your share of the blame with the other side's share. If a jury finds you partly at fault, any award shrinks by your percentage. You recover nothing only when your share passes 50%. The split depends on the evidence, and the Summerville personal injury attorneys at Frost Law Group review it before offering any view.

Lawyers call the money a claim seeks damages. Economic damages cover losses with a receipt, such as medical bills, lost pay and future care a physician documents. Noneconomic damages cover losses no receipt can show, such as pain and the activities you can no longer enjoy. Which damages a claim supports depends on the injuries, the proof and the coverage. No lawyer can promise an amount.

Motor Vehicle Accident Injuries

South Carolina recorded 143,801 traffic collisions in 2024, and those wrecks killed 1,040 people. The figures come from the Department of Public Safety's yearly Traffic Collision Fact Book. Behind each crash sits a collision report and at least one insurance policy. Section 38-77-140 sets the floor at $25,000 of bodily injury coverage per injured person and $50,000 per crash. Every policy must also include uninsured motorist coverage, which steps in when the driver at fault carries no insurance.

South Carolina requires no personal injury protection, the coverage that pays your own bills regardless of fault. That is why it is not a no-fault state. After a wreck that injures or kills someone, Section 56-5-1260 requires the drivers to notify police right away. The officer's report then goes to SCDMV, which keeps the official copy. SCDMV describes that copy as more detailed than the paperwork drivers get at the scene, and a lawyer can order it.

Slip and Fall Lawyers

A store does not automatically owe you for every spill on its floor. The South Carolina Supreme Court set out the test again in Wintersteen v. Food Lion in 2001. An injured shopper must show the store put the hazard there. Failing that, the shopper must show the store knew about the hazard, or should have found it, and did nothing. Lawyers call the should-have-found-it part constructive notice. A store's own inspection logs can help prove it.

Your reason for being on the property matters as well. An owner owes a paying customer more care than a social guest. Tenants can also rely on Section 27-40-440, which requires landlords to keep common areas reasonably safe. Frost Law Group takes slip, trip and fall claims from stores, restaurants and apartment complexes around Summerville. A fall in a public park or another government building runs through the South Carolina Tort Claims Act. That law sets different deadlines and notice rules.

Dog Bite Attorneys in Summerville, South Carolina

South Carolina has no "one free bite" rule. Under Section 47-3-110, a dog's owner, or anyone caring for the dog, is liable when it bites or attacks. The statute protects a person in a public place and a person legally on private land. That includes the owner's own yard. Courts call the statute strict liability. That means a bite victim does not have to show the owner was careless or knew the dog was dangerous.

The statute does not protect a trespasser or someone who provoked or harassed the dog into attacking. Doctors must tell the county health department about a bite they treat by the end of the next working day. Frost Law Group represents adults and children in dog bite and dog attack claims. The work can start with that health department report and the medical records.

Wrongful Death Accident Injury Attorneys in Summerville, South Carolina

Traffic crashes killed 1,040 people in South Carolina in 2024, including 37 in Dorchester County. When a careless or reckless driver causes a death, Section 15-51-10 gives the family a claim for its own losses. That claim rests on one condition. The person who died must have had a valid injury claim had he or she lived. A personal representative brings the case. That is the executor or administrator the probate court appoints.

Consider a head-on crash on Ashley River Road that kills a father of two. Section 15-51-20 puts his wife and children first in line to share any recovery. A separate survival claim under Section 15-5-90 belongs to his estate. It covers his own losses before death, such as conscious pain and suffering. Frost Law Group represents surviving families in wrongful death claims after a fatal crash, including crashes involving a drunk driver. A family has three years from the death to file suit.
