Where Car Accidents Happen in Summerville

Where car accidents happen in Summerville shows up only in county data, because the state publishes no town totals. In Dorchester County, home to most of Summerville's residents, Dorchester Road had the most collisions in 2024, with 519. Its crossings at Old Trolley Road, Ladson Road and Bacons Bridge Road made the county's top five intersections for collisions.

Those counts come from the 2024 edition of the South Carolina Traffic Collision Fact Book, the newest one on the SCDPS Fact Book listing. SCDPS, the state Department of Public Safety, ranks roads and intersections by route number. It relies on the names officers wrote on their reports. Parts of Summerville also lie in Berkeley and Charleston counties, so one town's crashes land in three county tables. A count shows where collisions clustered, and it decides nothing about fault in any one crash.

Does the US 17 Figure in the Dorchester Count Mean Main Street?

Nobody can say for certain from the public records. The Fact Book labels that route "US-17," yet SCDOT's 2024 Dorchester inventory shows no plain US 17 station at all. It lists eight US 17 Alternate stations instead, and US 17A is Main Street in Summerville. So the 360 figure is safest to read as the US 17 corridor exactly as officers wrote it. On US 17A, the stretch running from 5th Street North to the Berkeley line averaged an estimated 33,100 vehicles a day.

Who Investigates a Crash on Each Road

Any of three agencies may investigate a crash near Summerville, the town police, the Highway Patrol or the county sheriff's office. Section 56-5-1260 governs only whom the driver notifies. For an injury crash inside a town, that is the local police department. Outside a town, the driver alerts the sheriff's office for that county or whichever Highway Patrol office is closest.

In Dorchester County during 2024, the Highway Patrol investigated 1,517 collisions, 23 of them fatal. The Summerville Police Department investigated 1,321 there, 3 of them fatal, and the Sheriff's Office investigated 590. Each figure counts only the crashes that agency worked, so none of them is a Summerville total.

Troopers carry a large share statewide, 80,380 of 143,801 collisions and 746 of 951 fatal crashes in 2024. Each agency keeps its own files, so Frost Law Group can track down a South Carolina accident report from whichever one wrote it.

When Does the Summerville Police Department Take the Crash?

Inside town limits, Section 56-5-1260 directs notice to the Summerville Police Department, at 108 North Cedar Street. Its 2024 caseload reached past Dorchester County, with 416 Berkeley County crashes and another 21 across the Charleston County line. The town holds an estimated 5,798 residents in Berkeley and 2,655 in Charleston. Officers in its Traffic Safety Unit have training in investigating collisions at the scene, in technical investigation and in reconstruction. Copies of its reports come through CrashDocs, an outside portal the department directs people to.

When Do Troopers or Deputies Handle It Instead?

Troopers and deputies work crashes outside town limits and some inside them. Highway Patrol Troop Six covers Beaufort, Berkeley, Charleston, Colleton, Dorchester and Jasper counties. Its Post A serves Berkeley and Charleston from North Charleston, while Post B serves Colleton and Dorchester from Walterboro. Across its six counties, Troop Six investigated 9,380 collisions in 2024, 100 of them fatal. The Patrol's Troop Nine carries the MAIT name, short for Multi-disciplinary Accident Investigation Team.

What Does the Crash Location Change About a Claim

The location shapes which agency writes the report, which counties can host a lawsuit and whether a government road claim is possible at all. It can even decide whether the crash enters the state count. Private property sits outside the Fact Book entirely, and SCDPS treats a parking lot as private even when the public uses it. A public-road crash enters the count when it injures or kills someone or does $1,000 or more in property damage.

Which County Court Can Hear a Crash Lawsuit?

A plaintiff suing a South Carolina resident has two options under Section 15-7-30(C). The suit can go to the defendant's county of residence as of the day the claim arose. It can also go to the county that saw the most substantial part of the conduct at issue. Against a driver from out of state, subsection (D) allows that same county or the injured person's home county. Dorchester County's courthouse, 5200 East Jim Bilton Boulevard, St. George, sits in the First Judicial Circuit. Berkeley and Charleston counties sit in the Ninth.

What Changes When a Road Defect or Missing Sign Played a Part?

A claim against a government body follows the Tort Claims Act, and under that law no government body answers for highway design. Liability for a third-party defect, or for a missing or broken sign or signal, attaches only when the agency in charge of upkeep had actual or constructive notice. Constructive notice means the agency should have known. The agency must also have failed to correct the problem within a reasonable time.

Under Section 15-78-120, an agency's liability has a ceiling of $300,000 per person and $600,000 per occurrence. Section 15-78-100(b) sends such a suit to circuit court in the county of the act or omission.

The two-year limit in Section 15-78-110 starts at discovery of the loss, or at the point it should have come to light. Filing a verified claim with the agency first stretches that limit to three years. For that verified claim, Section 15-78-80(d) sets a receipt deadline of one year from that same discovery date.
