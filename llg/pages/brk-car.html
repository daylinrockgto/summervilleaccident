The Posted Limit Is Only a Ceiling Under Section 56-5-1520

Subsection (A) of the speed statute measures a lawful speed against the actual and potential hazards then existing. It also requires speed control that avoids any person or vehicle on the highway or lawfully entering it. Subsection (B) treats speed limits as maximum lawful speeds, except when a special hazard requires going slower.

Subsection (F) lists places that call for reduced speed. They include intersections, railway grade crossings, curves, hillcrests, narrow bridges and winding roads. Weather, pedestrians, other traffic and highway conditions count as hazards too. On US 176 in a downpour, a driver holding the posted number may still break the rule. Whether a speed was reasonable depends on the road, the light and the traffic that day.

Does an Officer's Speed Entry Settle Who Was at Fault?

No. Section 56-5-1290 covers the crash reports required by Sections 56-5-1260 to 56-5-1280. None of them can be evidence of either party's negligence, or of due care, at a trial for damages. An officer who testifies may still look at the report to refresh memory. The report also names the people the officer interviewed, as Section 56-5-1270 requires, so it points to witnesses.

South Carolina applies modified comparative negligence. A jury assigns each driver a percentage of fault, then reduces the injured person's award by his or her own percentage. Recovery ends once that percentage passes 50%. A speeding driver and a driver who pulled out too soon can both carry fault. No lawyer can promise a percentage, and Frost Law Group bases any view of the split on proof rather than the report's codes.

A Passenger's Claim Against the Driver's Liability Coverage

When your own driver runs off the road, you may be able to claim against that driver's liability insurance. Section 38-77-140 requires at least 25/50/25. That means up to $25,000 for any one person's injuries and $50,000 for all injuries in the crash, plus $25,000 for property. Three hurt passengers on a minimum policy may have to share that $50,000. Their own household policies may add underinsured coverage. How much can depend on the Supreme Court's 1998 Concrete Services opinion, which sorts insureds into Class I and Class II for stacking, the combining of coverage from several vehicles. Class I, the only group allowed to stack, takes in the named insured, a spouse and relatives residing in the same household. A Class II insured, such as a guest in the insured car, may not stack.

A Swerve With No Contact and Uninsured Motorist Coverage

Dashcam Footage After the May 2024 Rewrite

Before May 20, 2024, the statute offered no recording option. The rewritten section accepts physical contact with the unknown vehicle. Your Own Uninsured and Underinsured Limits

Act No. 42 and Claims Arising After January 1, 2026

A defendant found under 50% at fault has generally owed just its own percentage of the damages ever since Section 15-38-15 took effect on July 1, 2005. The 2025 law known as Act No. 42 left the threshold alone and changed how fault is counted, but only for a claim whose arising or accrual date falls after January 1, 2026. In those newer claims, the jury also weighs the fault of wrongdoers who are not parties. The defense generally must name them within 180 days after the suit begins.
