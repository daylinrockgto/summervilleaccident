Lawyers call an injury catastrophic when it permanently changes how a person works, moves or lives without help. The label turns on consequences rather than on one body part. A crushed hand can end a machinist's trade, while an office worker with the same fracture may go back to work. Catastrophic injury lawyers in Summerville follow those consequences through each stage of life. What any stage adds to a claim depends on medical proof that a lawyer gathers and tests.

Great Bodily Injury Under Sections 56-5-1210 and 56-5-2945

South Carolina's traffic code uses a related term. Sections 56-5-1210 and 56-5-2945 apply the label great bodily injury to harm that creates a substantial risk of death. It also includes serious, permanent disfigurement and a protracted loss or impairment of how a body part or organ works. Those sections make it a felony to leave the scene of such a crash, or to cause such harm while driving impaired.

A criminal charge does not decide the civil claim. Still, a lawyer can read the criminal file beside the medical records.

Spinal Cord, Brain and Limb Injuries From Car Wrecks

Spinal cord damage can cause paralysis below the level of the injury. A traumatic brain injury can change memory, mood and judgment long after the other wounds heal. Amputations, deep burns and crushed pelvises or legs also tend to bring lifelong limits. Pedestrians and bicyclists have no vehicle around them to absorb a crash. Who Handles the Claim When an Injured Adult Cannot?

A severe brain injury can leave an adult unable to manage money or make legal decisions. Section 62-5-433 sets special settlement rules for claims belonging to a minor or an incapacitated individual. A settlement paying such a person over $25,000 net requires circuit court approval. The court is the one in the county where the person lives or where the suit is pending. For a Dorchester County resident, that court sits at the Dorchester County Courthouse in St. George.

A conservator, the person a court appoints to manage the injured person's money, files a verified petition, meaning a sworn written request. If no conservator exists, a guardian or a guardian ad litem files it instead. A guardian ad litem is a person the court appoints to protect the injured person's interests in the case. The petition must state that the settlement serves the person's best interests.

The court's order then sends the money through the conservator or under a protective order, a probate court order that sets terms for holding the money. Frost Law Group can prepare for that court approval early, since the process controls who signs and where the money goes. Whether a person counts as incapacitated turns on that person's own medical and legal facts.

Chart Entries a Catastrophic Injury File Needs

Billing summaries show what care cost, but they say little about why a patient needed it or what lies ahead. EMS Run Reports and the First Trauma Assessment

An ambulance crew's run report records vital signs, level of consciousness and what the patient said at the scene. The trauma team's first assessment then lists the injuries found in the minutes after arrival. Together they can tie the injury to the crash before any other event can intervene. A brain injury claim may lean on these early notes about confusion or lost consciousness.

Operative Notes, Intensive Care Charts and Imaging

Surgeons dictate what they found and what they repaired, including any plates, rods or screws they placed. Intensive care notes track ventilator days, infections and setbacks that can shape the long-term outcome. CT and MRI studies show the spinal cord or brain damage that later testimony will explain. A lawyer can request the images themselves, not only the radiologist's summary.

Often the discharge summary is the first document that looks ahead. It may order inpatient rehabilitation, a wheelchair, home nursing or follow-up with a neurosurgeon. How Many Years of Future Care Can a Claim Count?

Future care lasts as long as the injured person lives, so life expectancy sets the length of the plan. When a case must establish life expectancy, Section 19-1-150 requires every court to receive the statute's own table as evidence. The table gives a 40-year-old man 38.33 more years and a 40-year-old woman 42.00. Lawmakers set the current table in 2004 Act No. 209, basing it on the 2001 Commissioners Standard Ordinary Mortality Table.

The same statute lets the fact finder weigh evidence of the person's health, constitution and habits beside the table. A defense lawyer may argue that a spinal cord injury shortens life, and treating physicians may respond. How a jury balances the table against the medical proof varies from case to case.

How a Summerville Catastrophic Injury Attorney Measures Lost Earning Capacity

What would a 35-year-old HVAC technician have earned over the rest of a career if a crash on Dorchester Road had not ended it? Catastrophic injury lawyers in Summerville build that answer from work records, medical limits and vocational and economic testimony. Pay stubs alone cannot supply it, because they only show the past. South Carolina courts count lost earning capacity among the losses a personal injury suit can compensate, apart from wages already missed.

Earning capacity means the ability to earn. It looks at what the person could have done, not only at what the person earned last year. A student, a parent home with young children or a worker between jobs may still have a claim. The proof looks different for each, and a lawyer tests it against the medical limits before anyone assigns a number.

Missed Paychecks Versus a Reduced Future

Lost wages cover pay already missed between the crash and trial, proved with pay stubs, tax returns and employer letters. Lost earning capacity covers the work and pay the injury keeps out of reach after trial. Some people return to lighter work at lower pay, so the claim covers the difference rather than the whole wage. Frost Law Group can gather the work history that shows where a career was headed, such as a pending promotion or an apprenticeship.

Vocational Rehabilitation Counselors and the Jobs That Remain

A vocational rehabilitation counselor compares the person's education, skills and work history with the limits the treating physicians set. The counselor then identifies which jobs remain realistic and what they pay in the local labor market. For a welder who can no longer climb or lift, that analysis may point toward lower-paid inspection or dispatch work. The defense may hire its own counselor, so the medical limits underneath need clear documentation.

Economists, Lost Benefits and Retirement Income

An economist turns the counselor's findings into dollars. The analysis can include lost raises, employer benefits and retirement income. An economist may also reduce future losses to one present-day figure. A sum paid now can earn interest before those losses occur. Each result rests on assumptions about work life and wages, and a lawyer tests every assumption before relying on it.

How a Summerville Severe Injury Lawyer Handles Medicaid and Medicare Repayment

Section 43-7-420 treats a Medicaid recipient as having assigned the right to recover Medicaid's payments to SCDHHS, the state agency that runs Medicaid. That right runs against a liable third party or insurer, and only up to the amount Medicaid spent. Section 43-7-430 adds an automatic subrogation right in the same amount. Subrogation lets SCDHHS collect directly from the at-fault side. The statutes frame this as assignment and subrogation, not a lien.

Suppose Medicaid paid for a long MUSC admission after a crash on Main Street. Frost Law Group can identify that claim early, because a catastrophic hospital stay can make Medicaid's figure large. How much any program finally takes depends on its itemized figure, any reduction it grants and facts a lawyer confirms.

Cooperation Duties and Continued Medicaid Eligibility

A person with a spinal cord injury may depend on Medicaid long after the crash. Under Sections 43-7-420(B) and 43-7-430(B), continued Medicaid eligibility depends on cooperating with SCDHHS as it enforces its rights. The statutes say cooperation includes reimbursing the department from payments received from a third party or insurer. Receiving Medicaid also counts as authorizing insurers and medical providers to release the information SCDHHS needs.

Collection Tools Section 43-7-440 Gives SCDHHS

Section 43-7-440 lists what the department may do to collect. It can join the injured person's lawsuit, bring its own suit or settle its claim for less. Subsection (A)(4) even lets it sue the Medicaid recipient directly. Its repayment right also outranks any other party's right of reimbursement, subrogation or indemnity. That priority affects how any settlement gets divided, and who gets paid first.

The 25% Reduction and Shared Litigation Costs

When the injured person has hired a lawyer, Section 43-7-440(A)(6) lets SCDHHS take 25% off its claim. The department may also share in other litigation costs. Its share equals the amount owed to the department divided by the total settlement, applied to those costs. Both reductions are discretionary, so a lawyer can ask for them in writing rather than assume them.

Written Notice and the Duty Before Paying Out

Section 43-7-440(A)(6) also speaks to the lawyer who handles the settlement money. A lawyer is not liable to the department for an improper settlement or payout without written notice of its rights before the funds go out. That notice has to arrive by the mail method the statute names. With notice in hand, a lawyer who pays out funds improperly can answer to the department.

Medicaid Managed Care Plans and Casualty Recovery

Some South Carolina Medicaid members receive care through a managed care plan rather than fee-for-service Medicaid. SCDHHS says both must pursue casualty recoveries, meaning repayment for care after an accident for which a third party is liable. The agency tells attorneys to contact the member's plan directly about a casualty case. Identifying the right plan early sends the repayment request to the office that holds the figure.

Medicare Conditional Payments on Crash Injuries

Section 1395y(b)(2) of Title 42 of the United States Code makes Medicare's payments conditional when an auto or liability insurer should cover the injury. A settlement or award must reimburse those payments. The Medicaid assignment in Section 43-7-420 expressly excludes rights to Medicare benefits, so each program's claim stands on its own statute. A lawyer can total both figures before any money goes out.

Damages Caps and Coverage Limits a Summerville Catastrophic Crash Attorney Confronts

Many families assume South Carolina caps pain and suffering in every injury case. The state's noneconomic damages cap appears in Section 15-32-220, which governs medical malpractice claims. Economic damages fall outside that cap, and a crash claim against a driver is not a malpractice claim.

Two other statutes can limit a crash claim, the punitive damages cap and the Tort Claims Act, though in practice the insurance limits often matter more. Frost Law Group can map the limits and the coverage before anyone puts a value on a catastrophic case. What any claim may bring depends on the available coverage and the strength of the proof, and a lawyer reviews each before naming a figure.

Punitive Damages and the Impaired Driver Exception

Section 15-32-530 limits most punitive awards to three times compensatory damages or an inflation-adjusted $500,000, whichever is greater. The statute keeps the cap from the jury. The cap itself does not apply when the trial court finds that alcohol, or a drug used outside a lawful prescription, substantially impaired the defendant's judgment. A felony conviction or guilty plea arising from the same conduct also lifts it. Frost Law Group handles drunk driving injury claims, where that exception can matter, but no lawyer can promise punitive damages.

Government Vehicles and the Tort Claims Act

A crash with a county maintenance truck on Central Avenue puts the claim against the county under the South Carolina Tort Claims Act. Recovery under Section 15-78-120 stops at $300,000 for one person and $600,000 for one occurrence, with no punitive damages.

Under Section 15-78-110, suit generally has to begin within two years after the loss came to light or should have. The period reaches three years only if a verified claim goes to the county before suit. Section 15-78-80(d) gives that verified claim its own deadline, since the county must receive it within one year of that same date.

Section 38-77-160 describes underinsured motorist coverage as reaching damages "in excess of any damages cap or limitation imposed by statute." Whether a policy pays in that gap depends on its wording and on facts a lawyer checks.

Insurance That Runs Out Before the Care Does

A catastrophic injury can outlast every policy in the case. Catastrophic injury lawyers in Summerville can list every policy that might respond, including household policies held by relatives. A relative's household policy may respond even when the injured person rode in someone else's car. The order in which those policies pay depends on their language and on the statutes. The $25,000 Minimum and the Sworn Limits Statement

Section 38-77-140 requires only $25,000 in bodily injury liability coverage for one person. Under Section 38-77-250, a claimant's attorney may demand the limits in a sworn written request, which goes with the incident report as the statute directs. The insurer must then disclose its personal auto limits under oath within 30 days, or send the declarations page. That duty skips fleet, umbrella and excess coverage, where larger limits may sit. Frost Law Group handles tractor-trailer and commercial truck crashes too, where federal rules require $750,000 or more in coverage when a for-hire carrier moves general freight across state lines.

Underinsured Motorist Coverage on the Injured Person's Own Policy

Underinsured motorist coverage, called UIM, pays when damages exceed the at-fault driver's liability limits. Section 38-77-160 obliges insurers to offer it, up to the insured's own liability limits, and buying it remains the customer's choice. The coverage generally follows the vehicle involved in the crash. If none of the insured's own vehicles was involved, recovery stops at the coverage on any one vehicle carrying UIM.

Class I Insureds and Stacking

Stacking combines the UIM limits on more than one vehicle. In the 1998 Concrete Services decision, Opinion No. 24773, the Supreme Court of South Carolina allowed only Class I insureds to stack. Class I includes the named insured, plus a spouse or relative who lives in that household. Guests and permissive drivers fall in Class II, so a stacking question turns on the injured person's place under the policy.

Umbrella Policies Without Underinsured Coverage

Section 38-77-161 says no excess or umbrella policy has to provide uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. A family with a large umbrella policy may learn that it adds nothing when the other driver is the one underinsured. Some umbrella policies include the coverage anyway, and only the policy itself can settle the point. A lawyer can ask for every umbrella and excess policy in the household.

Does the Claim End if the Injured Person Dies Before It Resolves?

No. Section 15-5-90 lets injury claims survive the injured person's death, and the estate's personal representative brings them. Frost Law Group handles survival claims that carry an injured person's case forward for the estate. If the crash injuries caused the death, the family may also have a wrongful death claim. Section 15-3-530 gives that claim three years from the date of death. Which claims apply depends on the medical cause of death and the dates in the file.
