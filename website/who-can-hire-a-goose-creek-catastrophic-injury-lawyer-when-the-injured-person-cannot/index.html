Who Can Sign for an Injured Adult in South Carolina?

Three kinds of representatives can usually act on an injured adult's claim in South Carolina. One is an agent under a durable power of attorney, whose claims powers come from Section 62-8-212 of the South Carolina Code. Another is a conservator the probate court appoints. The third is a guardian ad litem appointed for the lawsuit under Rule 17 of the South Carolina Rules of Civil Procedure. The catastrophic injury attorneys at Frost Law Group start by learning which of the three already exists.

Marriage alone does not carry the power to sign for an adult. Even after a court appoints a conservator, Section 62-5-407 says the person keeps every right the order does not remove. The right to bring a lawsuit is one of the rights the order can remove. Which path fits your family depends on documents and medical records a lawyer has to review first.

A Durable Power of Attorney Signed Before the Crash

A financial power of attorney may already answer the question. South Carolina's Uniform Power of Attorney Act took effect January 1, 2017. Under Section 62-8-104, a power of attorney created under it is durable unless it says incapacity ends it. A document that lets the agent do every act the principal could do includes the general claims power, under Section 62-8-201(c). Check a home safe, a file drawer or the office of the lawyer who drafted it for the original.

What the Claims and Litigation Power Lets an Agent Do

Section 62-8-212 spells out the claims power in plain terms. Unless the document narrows it, an agent holding the general claims power can take each of these steps for the injured person.

  • The agent can bring and maintain a claim or lawsuit to recover the damages the principal suffered.
  • The agent can settle the claim, propose a compromise or accept one.
  • The agent can sign the release and the other papers tied to a settlement.
  • The agent can receive the money paid to resolve the claim.

Section 62-8-201(g) makes those acts bind the principal as if the principal had acted personally. If a court later finds the person incapacitated, Section 62-5-433 requires court approval of any settlement over $25,000.

Recording the Document in Berkeley County Before the Agent Acts

Here is a step that is easy to miss. Section 62-8-109(c) lets an agent act after the principal's incapacity only if the power of attorney has been recorded like a deed. It goes on record in the county where the principal lives at the time of recording. For a Goose Creek resident, that office is the Berkeley County Register of Deeds in Moncks Corner. Recording can happen before or after the incapacity, but until it does, the agent cannot use the document.

Some documents take effect only on a future event, such as incapacity. If the document names no one to make that call, Section 62-8-109(a)(2) accepts a written finding from a physician or licensed psychologist. A document signed before 2017 follows the older law in place when it was signed, under Section 62-8-403(c).

Why a Hospital Consent Form Does Not Reach the Injury Claim

A hospital may ask a spouse to sign consent forms under South Carolina's Adult Health Care Consent Act. Section 44-66-30 ranks who decides health care for a patient unable to consent. A court-appointed guardian and a health care agent come first, then a spouse, adult children and parents. Under Section 44-66-20(8), two physicians must each examine the patient and confirm the inability to consent in writing, with an exception for emergencies.

That statute covers health care decisions and nothing else. It says nothing about lawsuits, contracts with lawyers or insurance releases. A health care agent gets the patient's own access to medical records under Section 62-5-505. Federal privacy rules in 45 C.F.R. 164.502(g)(2) treat a person with legal power over an adult's health care decisions as the patient for the records relevant to that role. Neither rule grants any power over a money claim.

How Does a Goose Creek Family Ask the Probate Court for a Conservator?

A relative files a summons and petition in the probate court of the county where the injured adult lives. Section 62-5-403 calls for one when no agent under a durable power of attorney can protect the person's property, and Section 62-5-401 fixes the county. The judge must find a basis by clear and convincing evidence, under Section 62-5-404(B). A conservator may then prosecute and settle claims under Section 62-5-422, subject to Section 62-5-433.

Section 62-5-408 sets the order in which the court considers candidates. After anyone another court has already appointed as conservator, the list runs this way.

  • A person the injured adult nominated before the incapacity comes first.
  • An agent named in a financial power of attorney comes next.
  • The spouse follows the agent.
  • An adult child, then a parent, follow the spouse.
  • The nearest relative willing to serve comes after the parents.

The court may pass over a higher-priority person when the injured adult's best interest calls for it, under Section 62-5-408(B). A spouse may also nominate someone else in writing to serve in the spouse's place. The full text sits in the South Carolina Probate Code's rules for persons under disability.

Which Probate Court Hears a Goose Creek Case

Goose Creek sits wholly inside Berkeley County. The Census Bureau's 2020 place-by-county file lists the city in Berkeley County only. Summerville and Ladson, by contrast, each cross into Charleston County. The guardianship and conservatorship statutes use different venue rules, though, so one family can end up in two probate courts. Which two depends on where your loved one lives and where they are being treated.

Berkeley County for the Conservatorship

Section 62-5-401 places a protective proceeding in the county where the alleged incapacitated individual resides. For a home inside Goose Creek city limits, that is the Berkeley County Probate Court at 300-B California Avenue in Moncks Corner. Frost Law Group's page on Berkeley County injury claims covers that courthouse, and the Judicial Branch's Berkeley County courthouse listing shows the probate judge's office there. A Goose Creek mailing address outside the city limits still needs a county check before anyone files.

Charleston County When the Patient Is at MUSC

Guardianship, which covers decisions about the person, follows a looser rule. Section 62-5-302 allows a guardianship case where the adult resides or is present. The Department of Public Health lists MUSC in Charleston as the Lowcountry's Adult Level I trauma center and Trident Medical Center as Adult Level II. A Goose Creek patient admitted to MUSC is present in Charleston County while there. A guardianship petition may then go to the Charleston County Probate Court at 100 Broad Street. The conservatorship still belongs in Berkeley County, and Section 62-5-102 allows the two to be consolidated only when both are pending in the same court. The firm also handles Charleston County injury claims.

What Happens After the Petition Is Filed

The process protects the injured adult as much as the family. Under Section 62-5-403(A), the petitioner serves the summons, the petition and a notice of the right to counsel on the injured adult. If no lawyer the adult chose appears within 15 days after the proof of service is filed, Section 62-5-403(B) has the court appoint one. Within 30 days of that filing, the court also appoints a guardian ad litem and an examiner. Tara L. Frost's years as a Dorchester County Magistrate Judge are described on her attorney profile.

The Examiner's Report and the Hearing

The examiner must be a physician or nurse practitioner, or at the court's discretion a physician assistant or psychologist. Section 62-5-403(D) requires a notarized report on the incapacity, a diagnosis and the person's ability to exercise the rights a court might remove. It is due at least 48 hours before any hearing that uses it, and it may rest on an examination from the 90 days before filing. In an uncontested case, Section 62-5-403(C) allows a temporary consent order that becomes final after 30 days unless someone asks for a formal hearing.

Emergency and Temporary Orders

A full case takes time a family may not have when evidence is at risk or bills are due. Section 62-5-108 offers two faster routes, and each one carries its own proof rules. The judge decides which fits. The court may also treat an emergency motion as a motion for temporary relief instead.

Emergency Orders Without Notice

An emergency order needs a verified petition showing immediate and irreparable harm before notice can be given. It must include an affidavit from a physician or nurse practitioner, or in the court's discretion a physician assistant or psychologist, who examined the adult within 30 days before filing. A hearing follows within 10 days of the order unless the court finds good cause for another date. An order continued after that hearing lasts no more than six months unless it says otherwise.

Temporary Orders After Notice

A temporary hearing comes with notice to the adult, the adult's counsel and the guardian ad litem. It may not be held sooner than 10 days after service on all interested parties, unless the court finds a shorter time reasonable. The supporting medical affidavit can rest on an examination from the prior 45 days. Temporary orders expire six months after they issue unless the order sets another term.

Does the Three-Year Deadline Stop While Someone Is Incapacitated?

Sometimes, and only within tight limits. Section 15-3-530(5) gives most injury claims three years. If a person entitled to sue is “insane” when the claim accrues, Section 15-3-40 leaves the time of that disability out of the count. It also caps the extension at five years, and it ends the extension one year after the disability ceases.

The statute does not define insane, and it asks about the person's condition when the claim accrued. A brain injury that begins with the crash itself raises a question a court decides on the facts. Treat the three-year date as real until a lawyer has tested the tolling argument against the medical records. No one can promise a judge will accept it.

How the Five-Year Cap and One-Year Limit Work Together

Say a crash on Red Bank Road in March 2026 leaves a man unable to manage his affairs until March 2030. His normal deadline, March 2029, passes while he is incapacitated. If the tolling rule applies, subsection (b) still stops the extension at March 2031, one year after he recovers. If he never recovers, subsection (a) stops it at five years past the normal date, in March 2034. A short incapacity often adds nothing, because one year after recovery can fall before the normal three-year date.

Claims Against the City of Goose Creek, Berkeley County or SCDOT

Government defendants fall under the South Carolina Tort Claims Act. Section 15-78-110 requires suit within two years after the loss was or should have been discovered. It allows three years if a verified claim was filed first, and Section 15-78-80(d) requires that claim within one year. Section 15-78-110 opens with the words “except as provided for in Section 15-3-40,” so the disability rule reaches these claims too. Section 15-78-120 caps recovery at $300,000 per person and $600,000 per occurrence. Whether the act applies turns on who owned the vehicle or the road.

Can a Guardian ad Litem Bring the Lawsuit Instead?

Yes. Rule 17(c) of the South Carolina Rules of Civil Procedure lets a conservator sue for an incompetent person, and a person with no appointed representative may sue through a guardian ad litem. Under Rule 17(d)(1), the court hearing the case, the probate judge, the clerk of court or the master-in-equity can make the appointment. For a Goose Creek resident, that includes the Berkeley County Clerk of Court and Master-in-Equity in Moncks Corner. A 1986 amendment lets the appointment come before the suit is filed.

A relative or friend applies under Rule 17(d)(5), after notice to the person's guardian or, if there is none, to whoever the person lives with. Under Rule 17(d)(2), the guardian ad litem can have no interest adverse to the injured adult and no ties to the defense lawyer.

A guardian ad litem can carry a case, but the money needs a home. Once the injured adult has been found incapacitated, Section 62-5-433 sends any settlement over $25,000 to the circuit court where the person lives or the suit is pending. The approval order routes payment through a conservator or a probate court protective order.

Can a Spouse File a Separate Claim After a Catastrophic Injury?

Yes. Section 15-75-20 of the South Carolina Code lets a person sue for a tortious violation of the right to the companionship, aid, society and services of a spouse. That is the spouse's own claim, often called loss of consortium. The spouse can hire a lawyer for it without any court appointment. The statute bars it from including damages the injured spouse already recovered.

The spouse's claim keeps its own clock. Section 15-3-40 pauses time only for a person under a disability, and a healthy spouse is not under one. Plan around the three-year limit in Section 15-3-530(5) for that claim. What it adds depends on the marriage and the evidence, which a lawyer reviews with the family.

How Does a Family Get the Goose Creek Crash Report?

It depends on which agency worked the crash. Section 56-5-1260 has drivers report an injury crash inside a municipality to the local police department. Outside one, the report goes to the county sheriff or the nearest Highway Patrol office. Inside city limits, that means the Goose Creek Police Department at 519 North Goose Creek Boulevard, Building C. Frost Law Group's guide on how to get a South Carolina accident report covers the statewide steps.

The department keeps collision reports from the past 14 days in its office. A not-at-fault party who asks in person gets a free copy, as does a parent or guardian when that party is a juvenile. Everyone else pays $5, and federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act limits apply. The firm's page on Goose Creek car accident claims covers that records counter too.

Every investigating officer, city or state, must send the report to SCDMV within 24 hours after finishing the investigation, under Section 56-5-1270. A Highway Patrol or Berkeley County Sheriff's Office report is requested there, online or on Form FR-50. A mailed request costs $10 per report.

Questions Goose Creek Families Ask When a Loved One Cannot Decide

These questions come from spouses, parents and adult children sitting in hospital waiting rooms. Each answer below names the rule behind it, so you can read the source yourself or bring it to a consultation. Your own family's facts can change the result, so use these as a starting point for a conversation.

Does a Power of Attorney Stop Working When Someone Is in a Coma?

Not if it is durable. Section 62-8-104 makes a power of attorney created under the 2017 act durable unless it says incapacity ends it. The agent still has to record it in the county where the person lives before acting, under Section 62-8-109(c).

What if the Injured Person Dies Before the Case Ends?

The injury claim survives. Section 15-5-90 carries it to the personal representative of the estate. If the injuries caused the death, Section 15-3-530(6) gives three years from the date of death to bring a wrongful death claim. Frost Law Group's page on survival action claims explains how the two fit together.

Can the Defense Blame Someone Who Is Not in the Lawsuit?

For claims arising or accruing after January 1, 2026, yes, within limits, because 2025 Act No. 42 rewrote Section 15-38-15. A jury can now assign fault to someone outside the lawsuit whom the defense discloses within 180 days after suit begins, or later for good cause. The defense must prove that party's fault unless you add it as a defendant. A defendant under 50% at fault pays only its share, unless its conduct was willful, wanton, reckless or intentional or involved illegal drugs.

Who Pays the Costs of a Conservatorship Case?

Section 62-5-105(C) makes petitioners responsible for their own attorney's fees and the costs of the case unless the court orders otherwise. Under subsection (B), the court sets reasonable pay for the guardian ad litem, appointed counsel and the examiner. Subsection (A) lets the court award costs from the protected person's assets when justice and equity require it.
