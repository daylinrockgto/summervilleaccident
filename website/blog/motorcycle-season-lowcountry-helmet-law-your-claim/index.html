Motorcycle season in the Lowcountry: the helmet question and your claim

Motorcycle accidentsBy Tara L. FrostPublished September 16, 2026

The first warm weekend brings riders out on Highway 61 and the two-lane roads toward Ridgeville, and the first crash brings the same phone call.

The call

“A car turned left in front of me at the Dorchester Road light. I went over the hood. I wasn't wearing a helmet. The adjuster says that's a problem.” We hear a version of this every season, and the answer is the same every time: the helmet has nothing to do with who caused the crash.

What the law says

South Carolina requires helmets and eye protection only for riders and passengers under 21 (S.C. Code § 56-5-3660). Adult riders may choose. A choice the legislature made legal is not negligence, and it cannot make you at fault for a driver who failed to yield. The helmet law explained.

What insurers do with it

Two things. First, they raise it as a general discount, hoping you will accept a lower number because you feel you were partly to blame. That is not how comparative negligence works; fault is about causing the crash. Second, in a case with a head injury, they may argue a helmet would have reduced it. That is a damages argument, it applies only to the head injury, it requires a doctor's testimony, and it has no effect on the broken leg, the road rash or the shattered wrist. We answer it with medical evidence, and often the mechanism of the crash answers it for us.

The crash that actually happens

Left turns across the rider's lane at the Dorchester Road and Old Trolley Road intersection, the shopping-center entrances on North Main Street, Bacons Bridge Road and Highway 17-A through Nexton. The driver “never saw” the motorcycle. That is an admission, not a defense: drivers must look before turning, and a motorcycle in its lane with its headlight on was there to be seen. Lane-change crashes on I-26 and rear-end crashes at signals make up most of the rest.

The safety point, plainly

We represent riders, and we will say it: helmets prevent deaths. The NHTSA and the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety publish the numbers. Wear one. And if you did not, and a careless driver hurt you, you still have a claim.

Frost first: if an adjuster says the claim is worth less because of the helmet, do not argue with them. The argument is ours to make, with a doctor.

Written by Tara L. Frost, Attorney at Law · Personal injury. Reviewed by Jack C. Frost.

Questions people ask

Can the insurance company reduce my claim because I was not wearing a helmet?

Not for fault. At most, in a case with a head injury, the defense can argue with medical evidence that a helmet would have reduced that injury.

Is lane-splitting legal in South Carolina?

No. Riding between lanes is prohibited; two motorcycles may share a lane side by side.
