Why Personal Injury Attorneys in Dorchester County File Claims in St. George

The Dorchester County Courthouse takes its address from East Jim Bilton Boulevard, which SCDOT's 2024 traffic inventory lists as US 78. The Clerk of Court works inside that building. A Dorchester County injury lawsuit from Frost Law Group goes to that clerk, even though the firm's office is in Summerville.

South Carolina's Circuit Court is a court of general jurisdiction, so no dollar cap limits the civil claims it hears. Its civil branch is the Court of Common Pleas. Its criminal branch is the Court of General Sessions. A criminal charge against the driver and your claim for damages travel on separate dockets, even inside one building.

Many people who call personal injury attorneys in Dorchester County expect the courthouse to be in Summerville, the county's largest town. Only certain courts sit there. The filing deadline matters more than the building, though, since a missed deadline can end a claim before it starts.

Three Courts Inside the Troy Knight Judicial Complex

Three courts share one building on Deming Way in Summerville, the Troy Knight Judicial Complex at number 212. They are the Summerville Magistrate Court, Family Court and the county's Central Bond Court. The magistrate court reopened there on July 1, 2024, after renovation work. If police arrested the driver who hit you, a bond hearing may happen in that building. A claim seeking more than $7,500, though, falls outside any magistrate's civil power under Section 22-3-10.

Criminal Charges in General Sessions Beside Your Civil Claim

Some crashes lead to felony charges, which the Court of General Sessions hears in St. George. Felony DUI under Section 56-5-2945 applies when an impaired driver causes great bodily injury or death. Great bodily injury, in plain terms, is harm serious enough to threaten death, leave serious permanent disfigurement, or impair a body part or organ for a long time. Leaving the scene of an injury crash is a misdemeanor under Section 56-5-1210. Great bodily injury or a death raises the offense to a felony.

A felony plea or conviction can matter to the civil claim. Punitive damages exist to punish a wrongdoer, not to repay a loss, and they usually carry a cap. Section 15-32-530 removes it when the defendant pleaded guilty to, or was convicted of, a felony arising from the same conduct. Frost Law Group can watch the General Sessions docket for a plea or verdict that bears on the claim.

Wrongful Death Claims Begin at the Probate Court in St. George

A wrongful death case cannot start until someone holds the legal power to bring it. Under Section 15-51-20, the personal representative of the person who died files the action on the family's behalf. The probate court in the county where that person lived makes the appointment. It then issues letters, the court papers that prove the representative's power to act. For a Dorchester County resident, that court shares the courthouse address on East Jim Bilton Boulevard.

Under Section 15-51-41, no wrongful death or survival settlement is final until a probate, circuit or federal district court approves it. Frost Law Group can guide the family and the appointed representative through both steps.

How Long Do You Have to Sue in the Court of Common Pleas?

Section 15-3-530 gives most personal injury suits a three-year limit. Under Section 15-3-535, that period runs from discovery, meaning the date a person knew, or should reasonably have known, that a claim existed. A wrongful death action also gets three years, counted from the date of death instead of the date of the wreck.

Suing a county, a town or a state agency brings in the South Carolina Tort Claims Act. Two years is the ordinary window there, per Section 15-78-110, counted from when the claimant discovered the loss or reasonably should have. It reaches three years only for a claimant who sent the agency a verified claim before suing. The agency has to receive that claim, under Section 15-78-80(d), no later than one year after the discovery date. A child hurt before turning 18 may gain time under Section 15-3-40, though never past one year after the 18th birthday. Which limit controls turns on facts a lawyer must check.

Crashes No Officer Came Out to Investigate

County Road Defects and the Tort Claims Act

Some rural wrecks involve the road itself, such as a missing stop sign or a damaged guardrail. The South Carolina Tort Claims Act sets a narrow rule for a missing sign or a road defect a third party caused. Liability generally attaches only if the responsible agency had notice and still left the problem uncorrected for an unreasonable time. The Act also bars claims over highway design. Recovery tops out at $300,000 per injured person and $600,000 across one occurrence, and no punitive damages are allowed. Whether a road claim exists turns on maintenance records and proof of notice, which a lawyer can request from the agency.

Right-of-Way Crashes at Crossroads and Driveways

A left-turning driver must yield under Section 56-5-2320, including when turning into a driveway or a private road. The duty runs to any oncoming vehicle near enough to create an immediate hazard. Section 56-5-2330 covers stop signs and yield signs. Picture a pickup turning left off US 178 into a driveway across an oncoming car's path. The turning driver owed the duty to yield, though a speeding oncoming driver may share the fault.

Speed Too Fast for Rain, Curves, or Traffic

Section 56-5-1520 makes speed a question of conditions, not only of the posted sign. It calls for slower driving near intersections and curves, over hillcrests and narrow bridges, and when weather or pedestrians create hazards. Speed for conditions appeared on reports for 2 of the county's fatal collisions in 2024. It also appeared in 159 other injury collisions. Tire marks, crush damage and time-stamped video can test an officer's speed entry.

Run-Off-Road Entries in the County's Fatal Wrecks

A run-off-road crash can involve one car or several. Of the 339 run-off-road listings in 2024, 8 came from fatal collisions and 7 from serious injury collisions. A passenger hurt when the driver left the road may have a claim against that driver's liability coverage. The result depends on why the car left the pavement, which the physical evidence has to show.

Sometimes an unknown driver forces a car off the road without touching it. Since the 2024 rewrite of Section 38-77-170, a recording of the crash can support an uninsured motorist claim in that case. The older route, an independent witness's signed affidavit, still works too. Two more conditions apply. The crash must reach police within a reasonable time, and you cannot have been careless in failing to identify the fleeing car and its driver. Guidance written before May 20, 2024, leaves the recording route out. Dashcam and doorbell systems may record over old footage, so a lawyer can ask owners to keep it.

Can Both Drivers Share Fault When One Failed to Yield?

Yes. Under modified comparative negligence, the rule South Carolina uses, a jury may divide fault between drivers by percentage. If a jury puts more than 50% of the fault on you, you recover nothing. At 50% or less, your own share comes off the award. The factor code on a report does not set those percentages, and no lawyer can predict the split before the evidence is in.

Ambulance, Emergency, and Imaging Records

The EMS patient care report notes what the crew found at the scene, including complaints of pain and vital signs. Emergency department notes, X-rays, CT scans and discharge instructions come next. When a patient moves between hospitals, each facility keeps its own chart. Repayment Claims Against an Injury Recovery

Health coverage that paid crash-related bills may expect repayment from a settlement or verdict. Those claims change how much of a recovery reaches the client, so a lawyer identifies them early. Private health plans may claim repayment under their own terms. Two government programs follow statutes of their own.

Medicaid Payments and SCDHHS Subrogation Rights

If Medicaid paid the hospital, Section 43-7-420 treats the patient's recovery rights as assigned to SCDHHS, the state Medicaid agency, up to the amount spent. With a lawyer on the claim, Section 43-7-440 allows the agency to reduce what it takes by 25%. That reduction is discretionary.

Medicare Conditional Payments

Medicare treats its crash-related payments as conditional when an auto or liability insurer owes the bills. Under 42 U.S.C. 1395y(b)(2), that money must go back to Medicare from the settlement, judgment or award. Any settlement figure means little until the repayment amount is clear.

Magistrate Court Limits a Dorchester County Injury Lawyer Explains to Clients

Section 22-3-10 of the South Carolina Code sets the line. A magistrate may hear a claim for injury to a person or property only if the damages sought stay at or under $7,500. That power is concurrent, meaning the circuit court's civil side can hear the same small claims. Dorchester County runs magistrate courts in Summerville and in St. George. Frost Law Group can explain the limit before anyone files, since filing with a magistrate caps what a claimant can ask for at $7,500.

The cap covers every item of damages claimed, not only medical bills. Lost pay, future care and pain and suffering all have to fit inside that same $7,500. Of the county's 3,958 collisions in 2024, 3,057 damaged only property. For personal injury attorneys in Dorchester County, that makes the $7,500 line a common early question. Choosing a court means weighing the injuries and the proof, and the right choice rests on facts no general rule can settle.

Counterclaims That Move a Case to Common Pleas

Section 22-3-30 covers a situation that can arise after a wreck. Suppose the other driver answers a magistrate claim with a counterclaim larger than $7,500. Both claims then move to the Common Pleas docket for the judicial circuit. In Dorchester County, that means the First Judicial Circuit and the St. George courthouse. A small property claim can grow into a circuit court case this way.

Judgments, New Trials, and Appeals From a Magistrate

A party who wants a new trial in magistrate court has ten days from the judgment to ask, under Section 22-3-1000. The right to appeal lasts thirty days. Appeals from a magistrate go to the Circuit Court, which holds limited appellate power over magistrate cases. Those windows run far shorter than the three-year limit for most injury suits. Missing either one can leave a magistrate's judgment standing for good.

Traffic Tickets a Magistrate May Hear After a Crash

A magistrate's criminal docket covers most offenses with a maximum fine of $500, a maximum jail term of 30 days, or both. Magistrates also set bail, hold preliminary hearings and issue warrants. A ticket written after a Dorchester County wreck may therefore end up in magistrate court. The ticket case and the injury claim are separate proceedings. Testimony in the traffic case may bear on fault, but its result does not by itself decide the civil claim.
