What Catastrophic Injury Attorneys in Summerville Mean by a Life-Changing Injury

In 2024, 81 people left Dorchester County crashes with serious injuries, according to the state Department of Public Safety's Traffic Collision Fact Book. Those 81 came from 60 serious injury collisions, and another 1,226 people had other injuries. At Frost Law Group, a serious injury code on a crash report starts the inquiry instead of ending it. The code records an officer's view at the roadside, and only the medical chart supplies a diagnosis.

Lawyers call an injury catastrophic when it permanently changes how a person works, moves or gets through an ordinary day. The word describes consequences, not a list of body parts. A crushed foot can end a lineman's career, while a teacher with the same fracture may return to a classroom. A claim has to follow those consequences through each stage of a life. What any one stage adds to a claim depends on medical proof that a lawyer gathers and tests.

Those stages run in a rough order. Trauma care comes first, then rehabilitation, then changes to a home and a vehicle. The working years follow, and later life brings care needs of its own. Each stage leaves records, and each record can support a different part of the claim.

Crashes, Falls and Attacks Behind a Lifelong Injury

Car and truck wrecks cause many life-changing injuries, yet they are not the only source. Riders and walkers have no vehicle frame around them. Dorchester County's 2024 crash victims included 66 motorcyclists, 7 of whom died, and 36 pedestrians, 4 of whom died. Frost Law Group handles car wreck claims that leave a driver or passenger with permanent harm. It also takes on serious falls and dog attacks.

Each cause carries its own liability rules. A store fall claim generally needs one of two showings. Either the owner's own act created the hazard, or the owner knew of it, actually or constructively, and left it unremedied. Constructive knowledge means knowledge the law infers from the circumstances. The state Supreme Court applied that rule in its 2001 Wintersteen v. Food Lion decision. A dog attack claim rests on Section 47-3-110, the state's dog bite statute. The medical path after the injury, and the proof of the years ahead, look much the same whatever the cause.

According to DPH, a trauma center keeps organized teams that respond promptly under a surgeon who directs trauma care. Emergency departments without that designation focus on evaluating, stabilizing and transferring injured patients. Each hospital in that sequence writes its own chart, and the claim needs every one of them.

That move creates a second chart, a second set of images and a note explaining the transfer.

Traumatic Brain Injury Claims a Summerville Serious Injury Lawyer Builds

A 24-year-old rider goes down on Bacons Bridge Road after a car turns across his lane. His helmet cracks, but he answers the paramedics' questions and goes home from the emergency room that night. Later his sister notices that he repeats questions and loses his temper over small things. Changes like those can point to a brain injury that the first records never named.

MedlinePlus, the National Library of Medicine's health site, describes traumatic brain injury as sudden damage to the brain. It can follow a blow, bump or jolt to the head, or an object that pierces the skull. The site lists motor vehicle crashes as the most common cause in young adults and falls as the most common cause in adults 65 and older. Concussions are mild brain injuries from which most people recover, while severe injuries can bring coma, serious physical and psychological symptoms and permanent disability.

Records That Tie a Brain Injury to the Crash or Fall

A brain injury claim often turns on the earliest notes, written before anyone had a reason to argue. Those notes can place confusion, a lost moment of consciousness or a head strike at the scene. Later records show whether the symptoms grew, faded or settled into something permanent. Frost Law Group handles motorcycle crash claims, where a cracked helmet and scene photographs can join the medical file as proof of the impact.

Paramedic Run Reports and the Glasgow Coma Scale

An ambulance crew records vital signs, alertness and what the patient said at the scene. MedlinePlus explains that clinicians may use the Glasgow coma scale to gauge how severe a brain injury is. The scale measures a person's ability to open the eyes, speak and move. A low score in the field, or one that drops at the hospital, can help link the injury to the event.

CT Scans, MRI and Neuropsychological Testing

MedlinePlus lists CT scans and MRI among the imaging tests used to diagnose a brain injury. It also describes neuropsychological tests that check how the brain is functioning. Repeat testing can show whether problems with memory and attention eased or stayed. A lawyer can request the images themselves, along with each written report.

Changes in Memory, Mood and Judgment That Families Notice

MedlinePlus lists mood changes and trouble with memory, concentration and thinking among the symptoms even a mild brain injury can cause. It adds that a brain injury can raise the risk of anxiety, depression and post-traumatic stress disorder. A spouse, a coworker or a supervisor may notice those changes before any physician does. When a family brings those observations to catastrophic injury attorneys in Summerville, the next step is matching each one to the medical record.

Written accounts help most while memories of the person's old habits are fresh, and Frost Law Group can collect them early. A coworker's note about missed steps on a familiar job, or a spouse's dated log of lost tempers, shows a before and after. Specific, dated observations usually make testimony about an unseen injury easier for a jury to credit.

Who Settles the Claim When a Brain Injury Leaves an Adult Incapacitated?

Section 62-5-433 sets approval rules for claims of minors and incapacitated individuals, and a severe brain injury can place an adult in that second group. When the net amount to the person exceeds $25,000, a verified petition, meaning a sworn written request, goes to a circuit court. For a Dorchester County resident, one choice is the circuit court that hears cases at the county courthouse in St. George. It can also be the circuit court in the county where the suit is pending.

A conservator, the person a court appoints to manage another's money, files the petition. Without a conservator, a guardian or a guardian ad litem, someone appointed to protect the injured person's interests in the case, files it instead. The approval order must route the money through the conservator or a protective order from the probate court. Whether an adult counts as incapacitated turns on medical and legal facts a lawyer reviews.

Spinal Cord Injury and Paralysis Cases a Summerville Catastrophic Injury Lawyer Handles

A discharge summary after a spinal cord injury lists the diagnosis and the orders for care outside the hospital. MedlinePlus, drawing on the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke, explains that the cord carries signals between the brain and the body. Most injuries begin with a blow that breaks or dislocates vertebrae. Pieces of bone then tear into cord tissue or press on the nerves that carry those signals.

A tractor-trailer crash on I-26 is one way that kind of blow happens. Frost Law Group handles commercial truck and 18 wheeler crash claims. For for-hire interstate carriers of general freight, 49 CFR 387.9 sets a minimum of $750,000 in liability coverage. That figure is a floor, not a limit on what an injured person may claim. How far any coverage reaches depends on the policies and facts a lawyer confirms.

Complete and Incomplete Cord Injuries

MedlinePlus calls a spinal cord injury a medical emergency and notes that immediate treatment can reduce long-term effects. It sorts these injuries into complete and incomplete. After a complete injury, the cord cannot send signals past the level of the damage, and the body loses movement and feeling from that level down. After an incomplete injury, some movement and sensation remain. That difference touches nearly every entry in a life care plan, including the type of wheelchair and the daily hours of help.

Rehabilitation Records That Show Where Progress Stopped

MedlinePlus says later treatment usually includes medicines and rehabilitation therapy, and that mobility aids and assistive devices may help with daily tasks. Physical and occupational therapists chart each session and record which goals a patient met. When gains level off, therapists often call it a plateau, and that note can mark when a limit began to look permanent. A lawyer can collect therapy records through the final visit, not only through the first discharge.

Home and Vehicle Changes After Paralysis

Picture a Knightsville mother who comes home in a power wheelchair to a house with front steps and a tub she can no longer use. Each barrier has a cost, and a claim cannot count a cost that no one has priced. A life care planner may work with an occupational therapist, a contractor or a mobility dealer to put a figure on each change. Each figure then has to match what the physicians ordered.

Ramps, Wider Doors and Roll-In Showers

A wheelchair user may need a ramp or lift at the entrance, wider doorways and a bathroom rebuilt around a roll-in shower. Some older houses cannot take those changes at a reasonable cost. The realistic answer may then be a move to a single-level home, and the claim should say so. Contractor bids, receipts and photographs of the house before the work support each figure.

Hand Controls, Lifts and Adapted Vans

Paralysis can end ordinary driving. A driver rehabilitation evaluation may call for hand controls or a van with a ramp or lift. Adapted vans cost more than ordinary cars and wear out on their own schedule. A claim that prices one van for a 30-year-old leaves out every van that person will need afterward.

Amputation, Burn and Scarring Claims Summerville Injury Attorneys Take On

South Carolina's traffic code already names these harms. Sections 56-5-1210 and 56-5-2945 define great bodily injury to include serious, permanent disfigurement and a protracted loss or impairment of how a bodily member or organ works. Those sections make it a felony to leave the scene of such a crash, or to cause such harm while driving under the influence. Frost Law Group can read that criminal file beside the medical records, though a criminal charge does not decide the civil claim.

Limb loss, deep burns and disfiguring scars change how a person moves or looks for the rest of a life. Their costs keep arriving after the wound closes, in replacement prosthetics, repeat surgeries and scar treatment. How a jury values those losses varies with the evidence, and no lawyer can predict the figure.

Limb Loss and the Prosthetics That Follow

MedlinePlus lists traffic crashes among the injuries that cause people to lose all or part of an arm or leg. Many amputees use an artificial limb, and learning to use one takes time, with physical therapy to help. The same source notes that sadness, anger and frustration are common during recovery, and that medicine or counseling can help. A claim can count the cost of that counseling beside the physical care.

Phantom Pain and Skin Problems at the Socket

Some amputees feel phantom pain, meaning pain that seems to come from the missing limb. MedlinePlus also notes skin problems in people who wear an artificial limb. Each complaint belongs in the chart, with the date and the treatment that followed. Those entries show a jury what daily life with a prosthetic involves.

Replacement Limbs Over a Working Life

A prosthetic limb may need replacing more than once in a lifetime, and a growing child can outgrow one. Fitting notes and replacement records from the prosthetics clinic give a life care planner the schedule to price. The planner can then count each future limb, not only the first. Without that schedule, an insurer may treat one device as the whole cost.

Burns From Vehicle Fires and Hot Engine Parts

MedlinePlus describes a burn as tissue damage from heat, chemicals, electricity, sunlight or radiation. Third-degree burns damage or destroy the deepest layer of skin and the tissue underneath. Burns can cause scarring and infection, and serious ones may need treatment to clean the wound and replace the skin. A vehicle fire after a crash, or a rider pinned against a hot engine, can cause this kind of harm. MedlinePlus also describes inhalation injury from breathing smoke, a risk for anyone trapped in a burning car.

Facial Scars After a Dog Attack

A dog attack on a child's face can leave scars the child carries into adulthood. The dog's owner or keeper generally answers under Section 47-3-110, which the state Supreme Court has described as imposing strict liability, meaning liability without proof of carelessness. Photographs taken at intervals show how a scar heals, tightens or fades. The plastic surgeon's notes add the medical side, including any revision surgery the scar may need later.

Can a Scar Count in the Claim After It Stops Hurting?

Yes. Pain and disfigurement are separate losses. In a 2003 opinion, the Supreme Court of South Carolina listed the losses a personal injury action compensates. Disfigurement appears there beside pain and suffering, medical expenses and lost earning capacity. Photographs, the surgeon's notes and people who knew the injured person before can all show the change. How a jury weighs a scar turns on where it sits, how it affects daily life and how well the records document it.

How a Summerville Severe Injury Attorney Proves Future Care and Lost Earning Capacity

How many power wheelchairs will a 25-year-old need over a lifetime, and what will each one cost? A catastrophic claim has to answer questions like that with evidence. Two bodies of proof carry most of the weight, the cost of future care and the loss of future earning power.

Juries hear these figures from witnesses, not from lawyers. A treating physician states what care the injury requires, and other witnesses price that care and measure the lost work. Frost Law Group also explains how car accident claims are valued in South Carolina, and the same caution applies here. Any value rests on proof a lawyer has reviewed, and no one can promise a figure.

Building a Life Care Plan From the Physicians' Orders

A life care plan is a common way, though not a legal requirement, to document future needs. The planner starts from the treating physicians' orders and lists each need, how often it recurs and what it costs today. Entries can include therapy, medication, equipment, home health aides and help with household tasks. The plan is only as strong as the orders beneath it, so a lawyer checks each entry against the chart.

Replacement Cycles for Chairs, Limbs and Vans

Equipment wears out, and a plan that lists each item once undercounts the need. A good plan states how long a power wheelchair, a prosthetic or an adapted van usually lasts, then repeats the cost on that schedule. The vendors and therapists who supply those items can explain the cycle. A lawyer compares each cycle with the physicians' orders.

Paid Attendants and Hours Families Log

Some patients need help to bathe, dress, eat or move from bed to chair. Home health aides, facility staff or a spouse who leaves a job may provide it. A daily log of caregiving hours shows a planner how much help the injured person needs on an ordinary day. Paid attendant care then goes into the plan at local rates.

Life Expectancy and the Table in Section 19-1-150

Future care lasts as long as the injured person lives, so life expectancy sets the length of the plan. When a civil case must establish life expectancy, Section 19-1-150 requires courts to receive the statute's own table as evidence. The table gives a 25-year-old man 52.51 more years and a 25-year-old woman 56.40. Lawmakers adopted the current table in 2004 Act No. 209, based on the 2001 Commissioners Standard Ordinary Mortality Table.

The same statute lets the fact finder weigh evidence of the person's health, constitution and habits along with the table. The defense may argue that paralysis or a brain injury shortens life, and treating physicians may answer that argument. How a jury balances the table and the medical testimony varies from case to case.

Lost Earning Capacity and the Work That Remains

Lost wages cover pay missed between the injury and trial, proved with pay stubs, tax returns and employer letters. Lost earning capacity looks forward, at the ability to earn that the injury took away. Proof of where a career was headed can support that part of the claim. Common examples include a pending promotion, an apprenticeship contract or a supervisor's letter about the next step up.

Vocational Rehabilitation Counselors and Local Jobs

A vocational rehabilitation counselor compares the person's education, skills and work history with the limits the physicians set. The counselor then identifies jobs that remain realistic and what they pay in the local labor market. For an electrician who can no longer climb, that may mean lower-paid estimating or dispatch work. The defense may hire its own counselor, so the medical limits underneath need clear support.

Economists, Benefits and Present Value

An economist converts the counselor's findings into dollars, including lost raises, employer benefits and retirement income. The economist may also reduce future losses to a present value, one sum paid now that can earn interest before those losses arrive. Each result rests on assumptions about work life and wages. A lawyer tests those assumptions before relying on them.

Periodic Payments and the Structured Settlement Protection Act

A settlement or judgment can pay damages over time instead of in one sum, an arrangement called a structured settlement. South Carolina's Structured Settlement Protection Act governs a later sale or transfer of those payment rights, not how the settlement begins. Under Section 15-50-40, a transfer takes effect only after a court finds in advance that it serves the payee's best interest. The court also looks at whether the payments were meant for future income or medical needs.

Medicaid, Medicare and Health Plan Repayment a Summerville Injury Lawyer Resolves

Families often hear that Medicaid holds a lien on an injury settlement. South Carolina's statute uses different terms. Section 43-7-420 treats a Medicaid recipient as having assigned a right to SCDHHS, the department that administers the state's Medicaid program. It is the right to recover Medicaid's payments from a third party or private insurer.

Section 43-7-430 adds an automatic subrogation right, which lets the agency step into the recipient's place to recover what Medicaid paid. The agency's separate power to file its own suit comes from Section 43-7-440. Both rights reach only as far as the amount Medicaid spent, and their full text appears in Chapter 7 of Title 43 of the South Carolina Code.

After a long admission at MUSC and inpatient rehabilitation, Medicaid's spending can grow large. Because repayment claims can take a large share of a catastrophic settlement, Frost Law Group's catastrophic injury attorneys in Summerville can request the itemized Medicaid figure before anyone agrees to a number.

Cooperation Duties That Protect Medicaid Eligibility

A person with a spinal cord injury may rely on Medicaid for years after the crash. Sections 43-7-420(B) and 43-7-430(B) make continued eligibility depend on cooperating with SCDHHS as it enforces its rights. The statutes say cooperation includes reimbursing the department from money received from a third party or private insurer. Receiving Medicaid also counts as permission for insurers and medical providers to release the information the department needs.

What Section 43-7-440 Lets SCDHHS Do to Collect

Section 43-7-440 lists the department's collection tools. It may join the injured person's lawsuit, bring its own suit, settle or compromise its claim, and even sue the recipient. Under subsection (D), its rights come ahead of any other party's right of reimbursement, subrogation or indemnity. That priority affects how a recovery gets divided. Subsection (C) separately voids certain private insurer contract terms. A policy issued or renewed after June 11, 1986, cannot deny or reduce benefits because the insured qualifies for Medicaid.

The Discretionary 25% Reduction and Shared Costs

When the recipient has hired a lawyer, subsection (A)(6) lets SCDHHS reduce its claim by 25%. The department may also share other litigation costs by cutting its claim by a proportion of those costs. That proportion equals the amount owed to the department divided by the total settlement. Both reductions are permissive, so a lawyer asks for them in writing rather than assuming them.

Written Notice Before Settlement Money Goes Out

The statute also speaks to the lawyer who holds settlement funds. A lawyer is not liable to the department for an improper payout without written notice of its rights before disbursement. Once that notice arrives, a lawyer who pays out funds without accounting for the claim can answer to the department.

Medicare's Conditional Payments Under Federal Law

Section 1395y(b) of Title 42 of the United States Code treats an auto or liability insurer as a primary plan. When Medicare pays for care that such a plan should cover, the payment is conditional, meaning it carries a duty to repay Medicare. A primary plan, and anyone who receives payment from one, must reimburse Medicare once a judgment, a settlement or other proof shows the plan's responsibility. A settlement counts even when no one admits fault. If repayment does not reach Medicare within 60 days after it receives notice, the statute lets it charge interest.

Medicaid's assignment in Section 43-7-420 excludes Medicare benefits, so each program's claim rests on its own law. A person covered by both programs may face two repayment claims from one recovery, and each figure belongs in the math before any money moves.

Health Plan Subrogation Under Section 38-71-190

Health insurers that write policies in South Carolina can add a subrogation clause. That contract term lets the insurer recoup its payments from the insured's recovery against the person at fault. Section 38-71-190 limits the amount to benefits the insurer already paid for the injury. An insured who considers the claim unfair can petition the Director of Insurance, and a finding that the subrogation is inequitable and unjust ends it. The insurer must also pay attorneys' fees and costs out of the amounts it recovers.

Section 38-33-50(C) applies the same rule to HMO contracts issued on or after January 1, 1988. The statutes send appeals of the Director's decision to the Administrative Law Court. Whether either rule reaches a self-funded employer plan, a federal plan or an out-of-state policy is a separate question. A lawyer can ask each plan for its policy language before agreeing to any repayment figure.

Can a Med Pay Insurer Take Back What It Paid?

Generally no. Section 38-77-144 bars assignment and subrogation of med pay an insurer sells. That coverage is also not subject to a setoff, meaning a deduction of those payments from other money owed to you. Underinsured motorist benefits under Section 38-77-160 are not subject to subrogation and assignment either.

Section 38-77-260 protects people who receive first-party benefits, such as med pay. A release or subrogation document the insurer takes when paying them is void unless an approved disclosure statement came first and at least three days passed. How much any program or plan finally takes still depends on its itemized figure, any reduction it grants and the policy wording a lawyer reads.

Policy Limits, Punitive Damages and Deadlines a Summerville Catastrophic Injury Attorney Weighs

The first figure a lawyer looks for in a catastrophic case is the liability limit on each policy that could pay. Section 38-77-140 demands only $25,000 of bodily injury liability coverage per person. A catastrophic injury can exceed that amount before the patient leaves the trauma center.

Three kinds of limits work at once, and Frost Law Group can map each of them before anyone discusses a number. Insurance limits decide how much money exists to pay a claim. Statutory caps limit certain kinds of damages, and filing deadlines decide whether a claim survives at all.

Sworn Limits Statements and Coverage Past the Minimum

A claimant's lawyer can invoke Section 38-77-250 with a sworn written request that includes a copy of the incident report. Within 30 days, the auto insurer owes an answer under oath listing the limits of each known non-fleet private passenger policy. Sending the declarations page satisfies the duty too. Subsection (E) leaves fleet limits, umbrella coverage and excess coverage out of that duty. In a catastrophic case, the larger policies may sit in exactly those places.

Underinsured Motorist Coverage in the Household

UIM, the underinsured motorist coverage on a household's own policies, pays toward damages that outstrip the at-fault driver's liability limits. An auto insurer has to offer UIM under Section 38-77-160, at limits as high as the buyer's own liability coverage. The buyer may decline it. The same statute forbids any UIM clause making the injured person get the insurer's consent before settling with the at-fault driver. Two more questions decide how far the coverage reaches.

Resident Relatives Who Count as Class I Insureds

The insured, as Section 38-77-30(7) uses the word, includes the named insured's resident spouse and resident relatives. The statute covers them "while in a motor vehicle or otherwise." Citing that phrase, the Supreme Court of South Carolina's 2013 Carter decision described UIM as "personal and portable" for these Class I insureds. So a person struck while walking, or while riding in a friend's car, may still reach a household policy. Section 38-77-160 still limits stacking, which means combining coverage from more than one vehicle. Guests and permissive drivers are Class II insureds, whose coverage stays tied to the insured vehicle.

Umbrella Policies That Leave Out UIM

Section 38-77-161 says no excess or umbrella policy has to provide uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. A family with a large umbrella policy may find that it adds nothing when the other driver carries too little insurance. Some umbrella policies include the coverage anyway, and only the policy language settles the question. Each umbrella or excess policy in the household therefore needs its own reading.

Claims Against a Government Driver or Agency

A claim against the town, the county or a state agency runs under the South Carolina Tort Claims Act. That holds for a crash with a government vehicle and for a fall on public property. The act's Section 15-78-120 lets one person collect no more than $300,000 and one occurrence no more than $600,000, and it bars punitive damages. Section 38-77-160 describes UIM as reaching damages beyond any cap or limitation a statute sets. Whether a given policy pays in that gap turns on its wording.

Against a government defendant, Section 15-78-110 generally allows a two-year window to sue. The count starts on the day the claimant discovered the loss, or the day a reasonable person would have. Only a claimant who first presents a verified claim to the agency gets three years. The agency must have that verified claim in hand no more than a year past the same discovery date, a limit set by Section 15-78-80(d).

Punitive Damages and the Cap in Section 15-32-530

Punitive damages punish conduct instead of repaying a loss. Section 15-32-520(D) sets a high bar, proof by clear and convincing evidence, and the conduct shown must be willful, wanton or reckless. Any defendant may ask for a separate trial phase on those damages. Section 15-32-530 generally limits the award to the greater of three times compensatory damages or $500,000, and the statute keeps that cap from the jury. The Revenue and Fiscal Affairs Office adjusts that dollar figure each year. Its 2026 memo sets the figure at $739,245, taking effect on publication in the State Register.

The ceiling climbs to four times the compensatory award or $2,000,000, whichever is greater, when the court makes certain findings. Conduct that could bring a felony conviction is one of them. It disappears when the court finds intent to harm or a felony plea or conviction from the same conduct. It also disappears when alcohol, or drugs used outside a lawful prescription, substantially impaired the defendant's judgment. NHTSA's estimate puts alcohol-impaired driving deaths in Dorchester County at 19 for 2024, up from 8 in 2023.

Frost Law Group handles drunk driving injury claims, where that last exception can matter. No lawyer can promise punitive damages, and each cap exception rests on a finding by the trial court. Section 15-32-220 limits noneconomic damages, the pain and suffering part of a claim, in medical malpractice actions. A crash, fall or dog attack claim against a driver, property owner or dog owner is not a malpractice claim.

Section 15-3-530 and the Discovery Rule for Injury Claims

Section 15-3-530(5) gives most personal injury claims three years. Section 15-3-535 ties the start of those three years to knowledge, meaning actual awareness of the claim or the awareness reasonable diligence would have brought. For a child, time spent under 18 does not count under Section 15-3-40. The extension still ends no later than a year after the 18th birthday. That section also mentions a person who is "insane" when the claim arises, with its own limits. Whether a severe brain injury fits that old statutory word is a question to test early, never an assumption to rely on.

What Happens to the Claim if the Injured Person Dies Before It Ends?

The claim continues. Section 15-5-90 lets injury claims survive the injured person's death, and the estate's personal representative brings them. If the injuries caused the death, the family may also have a wrongful death claim. Section 15-3-530(6) allows three years from the date of death to file it. Frost Law Group handles wrongful death claims alongside the survival claim when both apply. Which claims fit depends on the medical cause of death and the dates in the file.
