What to Do After an Injury in Summerville

Short on time? Do these, then call us. We handle the rest.

  1. Get checked out. Go to the emergency room or urgent care, even if you feel okay. Many injuries show up days later.
  2. Report it. Call 911 for a crash, tell the manager about a fall, report a dog bite to animal control. Get the report number.
  3. Take photos. The scene, what caused it, your injuries. Get witness names before they leave.
  4. Don't give a recorded statement to the other side's insurance company.
  5. Keep every bill and receipt, and track the days of work you miss.
  6. Talk to a lawyer before you sign anything. A release ends your claim.

Summerville Personal Injury FAQs

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in South Carolina?

Usually three years from the injury to file a lawsuit.1 Claims against a city, county or state agency must be filed within two years.2 Evidence disappears much sooner, so call early.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can still recover if you were 50% or less at fault. Your amount is reduced by your share of the blame.3 Do not accept an adjuster's opinion on fault. That is a question the evidence answers.

What does it cost?

The case review is free. You never pay out of pocket. We only collect a fee if we recover compensation for you, so there's no risk in reaching out. If there is no recovery, you owe no fee and no case costs.

Do I need a lawyer for a minor injury?

Ask before you decide. Some "minor" injuries turn into months of treatment, and the insurer's first offer is made before anyone knows that. A short call costs nothing, and if the claim is small enough to handle yourself, we will tell you so.

The insurance company already offered me money. Should I take it?

Not before you talk to a lawyer. A quick check comes with a release, and a release ends your claim for good, even if your injuries turn out to be worse than you thought.
