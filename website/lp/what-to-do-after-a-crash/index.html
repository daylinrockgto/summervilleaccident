The Eight Steps

  1. Get to safety and call 911. Move out of traffic if you can. Ask for police and, if anyone is hurt, an ambulance. The officer's report becomes the record of what happened.
  2. Get checked out, even if you feel okay. Go to the emergency room or urgent care. Adrenaline hides pain, and neck, back and head injuries often show up days later. The visit also creates the record an insurer will ask for.
  3. Photograph everything. Both cars, the plates, the road, skid marks, traffic signs and your injuries. Take more than you think you need.
  4. Exchange information and get witness names. The other driver's name, phone and insurance card. Anyone who saw the crash, before they drive away.
  5. Get the crash report. Summerville Police handle crashes in town and the Highway Patrol handles I-26 and the highways. We can obtain the report for you and read it with you.
  6. Report the crash to your own insurer. Give the basics, the date, the place and the vehicles. Do not guess about fault or describe your injuries as minor.
  7. Do not give the other driver's insurer a recorded statement. Give them your name and your attorney's number. Do not sign anything and do not accept a quick check. A release ends your claim for good.
  8. Talk to an attorney before the deadline. South Carolina generally gives you three years to file a lawsuit.1 Claims against a city, county or state agency must be filed within two years.2 Evidence disappears much sooner.

What Not to Do

Do not post about the crash

Insurers read social media. A photo of you at a cookout becomes "proof" you were not hurt.

Do not skip appointments

Gaps in treatment are used to argue you healed, or were never hurt. Keep every visit.

Do not accept the first offer

A quick check arrives before anyone knows what your injuries will cost. Cashing it can end the claim.

Do not throw anything away

Bills, receipts, the damaged car seat, the helmet. Keep it all until we tell you otherwise.

Do not guess about fault

Not to the other driver, not to the adjuster. The evidence answers that question.

Do not wait

Camera footage is overwritten, witnesses move and deadlines run. Sooner is better, and the call is free.
