South Carolina car accident laws, explained

Seven statutes decide most car accident claims in South Carolina. Here they are in plain English, with the citation for each.

Get a free case review(843) 419-6653
The short answer

South Carolina is an at-fault state with modified comparative negligence (recover if 50 percent or less at fault). Drivers must carry at least 25/50/25 liability coverage plus uninsured motorist coverage. Drivers must stop after a crash and report it when there is injury or significant damage. Failing to wear a seat belt cannot be used against you in a civil case. Texting while driving is illegal. Suit must be filed within three years.

1. Fault and comparative negligence

The driver who causes a crash is responsible for the harm. If the injured person shares fault, the recovery is reduced by their percentage, and barred at 51 percent. At-fault explained and the 51 percent rule.

2. Minimum insurance

Every registered vehicle must carry liability coverage of at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury and $25,000 for property damage (S.C. Code § 38-77-140), plus uninsured motorist coverage at the same minimums (S.C. Code § 38-77-150). Insurers must offer underinsured motorist coverage (S.C. Code § 38-77-160). Driving without insurance is a crime and leads to license and registration suspension, but plenty of people do it, which is why UM coverage is required.

3. Duty to stop, help and exchange information

A driver in a crash involving injury or death must stop at the scene, give their name, address, registration and license, and render reasonable assistance (S.C. Code § 56-5-1210). Leaving is a felony when someone is hurt. What to do after a hit-and-run.

4. Duty to report

A crash with injury, death or property damage of $1,000 or more must be reported immediately to the local police, the sheriff or the Highway Patrol (S.C. Code § 56-5-1260). If no officer investigates, the driver must send Form FR-309 to the SCDMV within 15 days (S.C. Code § 56-5-1270). The officer's collision report (TR-310) is the foundation of every claim.

5. The seat-belt evidence rule

Adults must wear seat belts, but a violation is not negligence and is not admissible as evidence in a civil case (S.C. Code § 56-5-6540). An adjuster who says “you weren't wearing your seat belt, so we are reducing the offer” is bluffing.

6. Phones and distraction

Texting while driving has been illegal in South Carolina since 2014 (S.C. Code § 56-5-3890). In 2025 the General Assembly went further with a hands-free law that prohibits holding a phone while driving. A violation is evidence of negligence in a civil case, and phone records can be subpoenaed. Distracted-driving crashes.

7. Deadlines

Three years to file suit for injury or property damage, two years against government entities, three years from death for wrongful death (S.C. Code § 15-3-530). The statute of limitations.

Laws that surprise people

  • Punitive damages have no cap when the at-fault driver was drunk. The usual cap on punitive damages does not apply to a defendant whose judgment was impaired by alcohol or drugs (S.C. Code § 15-32-530). Drunk-driving crash claims.
  • Diminished value is recoverable. A repaired car is worth less than one that was never wrecked, and South Carolina lets you claim the difference from the at-fault driver's insurer.
  • Your medical bills are your evidence, even if insurance paid them. South Carolina generally lets you present the amount billed, not just the amount your health insurer paid.
  • Rideshare drivers carry special coverage. Uber and Lyft must provide at least $1,000,000 in liability coverage during a ride (S.C. Code Title 58, Chapter 23, Article 16 (Transportation Network Company Act)). Uber and Lyft crashes.
  • Motorcycle riders over 21 need not wear a helmet. (S.C. Code § 56-5-3660). What that means for a claim.

Questions people ask

Is South Carolina a no-fault state?

No. It is an at-fault state. The at-fault driver's insurer pays; there is no PIP requirement.

What is the minimum car insurance in South Carolina?

$25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, $25,000 for property damage, plus uninsured motorist coverage at the same limits.

Do I have to report a minor accident in South Carolina?

Yes if anyone was hurt or property damage is $1,000 or more, which today is nearly any crash involving body damage. Report it to law enforcement immediately, and to the DMV within 15 days if no officer investigated.

Is it illegal to use a phone while driving in South Carolina?

Texting has been banned since 2014, and a 2025 hands-free law prohibits holding a phone while driving. Either violation is powerful evidence in an injury claim.
