Why Personal Injury Attorneys in Georgetown County Center Their Work on US 17

US 17 enters Georgetown County from Charleston County carrying about 9,100 vehicles a day, by SCDOT's 2024 estimate. At the Horry County line, the count tops 40,000. Frost Law Group reads the county's crash data road by road. No road comes close to US 17. The 2024 Traffic Collision Fact Book put 629 of the county's 1,484 collisions on US 17, about 42% of the total.

Georgetown County has no interstate, and the Fact Book logged zero interstate crashes there. US primary routes absorbed 839 of the year's collisions instead. US 17 also runs 67.5 centerline miles through the county, longer than any other US or SC route. One crash on it can involve a local commuter, a delivery truck and a family bound for the beach, each with its own insurer.

The counts rise again after that, to 29,100 past Waterford Road and 34,800 past South Causeway Road. US 17 Business, a separate route, carried 5,100 and 5,700 vehicles a day on its two segments.

Matching the location to the right segment comes first. After that, the useful question is which cameras, businesses or witnesses might have seen the crash.

Coverage Limits When One US 17 Crash Injures Several People

South Carolina's minimum liability coverage, set by Section 38-77-140, is 25/50/25. Read that as $25,000 per injured person, $50,000 for every injury in one crash combined and $25,000 for property. In a four-car wreck near Wesley Road, three injured people could end up splitting one driver's $50,000. The Fact Book's formula put the county's 2024 economic loss from crashes at $78,165,500, down from $87,219,500 in 2023. That public estimate says nothing about any one person's claim.

Uninsured motorist coverage comes standard on every South Carolina auto policy, because Section 38-77-150 requires it. Coverage for an underinsured driver, called UIM, works differently. Under Section 38-77-160, each insurer has to offer UIM, while purchase stays optional for the policyholder. UIM can pay when the at-fault driver's limits run short, up to your own UIM limits. A policy held by a relative who lives with you may apply as well. To learn what the other driver carries, a lawyer can send a sworn written request under Section 38-77-250. The insurer then generally has 30 days to disclose the limits on a personal auto policy. Whether a given policy pays depends on its terms.

How South Carolina Divides Fault After a Multi-Car Wreck on Ocean Highway

South Carolina uses modified comparative negligence, a rule that weighs the injured person's own fault against everyone else's. Someone whose own share of fault passes 50% recovers nothing. A smaller share cuts the award by that same percentage. Among defendants, Section 15-38-15 has limited each one under 50% at fault to its own percentage since 2005. Act No. 42 widened the comparison for claims arising after January 1, 2026. A jury in those cases may also assign fault to someone who is not a party. The Act also ended the older exceptions that made a defendant answer for everything whenever alcohol or gross negligence was involved.

Picture a three-car wreck near Sandy Island Road. One driver brakes hard, a second follows too closely and a third glances at a phone. The jury compares all three, plus any fault it finds in the injured person. Section 15-38-15 generally requires a defendant to name such a nonparty within 180 days of the suit's start. Frost Law Group applies these same fault rules in the car accident claims it handles around Summerville. How a jury would split fault in a real crash depends on the evidence, and no lawyer can promise the result.

Curves, Hillcrests and Narrow Bridges in Section 56-5-1520

Under Section 56-5-1520, a speed under the posted limit can still be unlawful. The test is whether the speed was reasonable and prudent for the road, the traffic and the hazards. The statute tells drivers to slow down near curves, hillcrests, narrow bridges and intersections, and in bad weather. Its default ceiling is 55 mph on most roads outside urban districts, though posted limits can differ. A driver doing 50 through a wet curve on US 521 may still violate it.

When a Driver Forces You off US 701 and Keeps Going

Section 38-77-170 governs an uninsured motorist claim when nobody can identify the at-fault driver. The General Assembly rewrote it in 2024 and added new ways to prove a phantom vehicle existed. Three conditions apply. Police must hear about the crash within a reasonable time. The injured person must have acted with reasonable care in trying to identify the other driver. And one of three kinds of proof must exist.

Contact, an Affidavit or Dashcam Video Under the 2024 Rewrite

Physical contact between the unknown vehicle and yours is the first route. Second, a witness who neither owned nor drove the insured vehicle can sign an affidavit describing the crash. Without an affidavit, the insured can ask a court under Rule 27 to order the witness deposed before suit. The third route, new in 2024, is a recording, such as dashcam footage, that shows the unknown vehicle caused the crash.

Reporting the Crash Within a Reasonable Time

Nothing in Section 38-77-170 counts the hours or days a driver has to call the police. Reasonableness is the test, judged from everything that happened after the crash. A driver carried from a ditch on US 701 by ambulance may call police later than one who drives home. Whether a given delay was reasonable depends on the report record and the medical timeline.

Impaired Drivers, Punitive Damages and the Bar That Served Them

Punitive damages punish reckless conduct rather than repay a loss, and Section 15-32-530 usually caps them. The cap does not apply if alcohol, or drugs outside a valid prescription, left the defendant's judgment substantially impaired. For claims arising after January 1, 2026, alcohol licensees such as bars face an added rule in Section 61-2-147. Suppose a jury returns a verdict against a bar and against a driver who faced a DUI charge. The bar, as a licensed seller, then answers jointly and severally for 50% of the plaintiff's actual damages. In practice, the injured person may collect up to that half from the bar. No lawyer can promise punitive damages. Section 15-32-520 requires clear and convincing proof of willful, wanton or reckless conduct.

Can a Road Hazard on a State Highway Lead to an Injury Claim?

Sometimes, under narrow rules. Claims against SCDOT, the county or a town follow the state's Tort Claims Act, which has its own rules. Under Section 15-78-60, government bodies carry no liability for highway design. For a road defect a third party created, the agency answers only for leaving it uncorrected too long. A missing or malfunctioning sign falls under a similar rule. That clock runs from actual or constructive notice, meaning the agency knew or should have known.

Section 15-78-120 caps one person's recovery at $300,000 and one occurrence at $600,000, and the Act sets its own filing deadline. Whether a stop sign knocked flat on a county road meets the notice test depends on maintenance records. A lawyer would review both the records and the timing before advising on a claim against a government agency.

Can a Georgetown County Crash Case Be Filed Closer to Summerville?

Sometimes. Section 15-7-30 lets a suit against a South Carolina resident proceed where the defendant lived when the wreck happened. It may also go where the bulk of the negligent driving took place. Say a Moncks Corner driver rear-ends you on Ocean Highway near Pawleys Island. The case could proceed in Georgetown County, where the wreck happened, or in Berkeley County, where the driver lives. Frost Law Group handles Berkeley County injury cases too, in the Ninth Circuit.

A driver from outside South Carolina triggers a different rule. The statute then allows the county of the crash or the injured person's home county at the time. With several defendants, a county that is proper for any one defendant can hear the claims against all of them. Which option fits depends on the defendants and the proof.

The General Three-Year Rule for Georgetown County Injury Suits

Most injury suits against a private driver or company carry a three-year deadline under Section 15-3-530. Section 15-3-535 starts those three years when the injured person learns of the claim or reasonably should have learned of it. For most crash victims, that is the crash date itself. A child may get extra time under Section 15-3-40, which pauses the clock while the child is under 18. That extra time ends no later than a year past the 18th birthday. For a teenager hurt close to 18, the regular three-year period may already reach further, and then the pause adds nothing.

Tort Claims Act Limits for an SCDOT Truck or County Vehicle

A crash involving an SCDOT truck, a county vehicle or a city police cruiser comes under the Tort Claims Act. Its general limit, in Section 15-78-110, is two years from the day the loss was found, or should have been found. Presenting a verified claim to the agency involved before suing can extend the time to three years. The third year is available only when the claim reaches the agency no more than a year after that same date, per Section 15-78-80(d). Punitive damages are off the table against a government defendant under Section 15-78-120.

The Personal Representative and the Death-Date Deadline

The family's wrongful death suit has three years, counted from the death rather than the crash, under Section 15-3-530(6). Only a personal representative can bring it, meaning the person the probate court appoints to handle the estate. Section 62-3-201 opens the estate where the person was domiciled at death, meaning the place the law treats as that person's home. A Georgetown County resident's estate therefore opens in Georgetown County's probate court. Any wrongful death settlement also needs a court's approval under Section 15-51-41.
