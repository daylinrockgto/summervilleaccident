How Truck Accident Lawyers in Berkeley County Compare I-26 and US Route Crashes

Interstates saw 36 fatal commercial vehicle collisions across South Carolina in 2024, while US primary routes saw 43. Interstates still logged more commercial vehicle crashes overall, 1,054 against 818, in the SCDPS Traffic Collision Fact Book. That gap shapes how Frost Law Group looks at a Berkeley County truck crash. Berkeley has I-26 and I-526, plus busy surface routes in US 52, US 17A and US 176.

Berkeley's own route table leans the same way for all vehicles. The Fact Book counted 606 interstate collisions in the county, 2 of them fatal. US primary routes carried 2,153 collisions and 16 fatal ones. Those county figures cover every kind of vehicle, since the Fact Book publishes no Berkeley truck total. They work as a map of where serious wrecks cluster, not as proof of what caused any one wreck.

Interstate Truck Crashes Between Jedburg Road and College Park Road

Berkeley's stretch of I-26 logged 460 collisions in 2024, and 2 were fatal. Another 84 caused lesser injuries to 116 people. Daily traffic on the I-26 run linking US 17A and College Park Road averaged an estimated 89,700 vehicles in SCDOT's 2024 count. In traffic that dense, a loaded tractor-trailer that brakes late can shove one car into the next. Frost Law Group can map where each vehicle sat before impact, since a chain crash can involve several insurers.

Turns, Side Roads and Signals on Berkeley's US Routes

US 52 recorded 653 collisions in Berkeley in 2024, with 10 fatal crashes that killed 10 people. That is five times the fatal count for I-26 inside the county. SCDOT's county file lists junction after junction on US 52. They run from St. James Avenue past Cypress Gardens Road to Black Oak Road. Each junction is a place where a slow truck can turn across traffic or pull out from a stop.

Section 56-5-2320 holds a left-turning driver back until oncoming traffic is no longer an immediate hazard. A truck leaving a side road with a stop sign owes the same yield under Section 56-5-2330. Who held the right of way turns on signal timing, sight lines and the truck's speed. Frost Law Group can check those details against the report before taking a position.

Is an I-26 Truck Crash Less Serious Than One on US 52?

Not by default. Across South Carolina, 460 of the 1,496 commercial vehicle collisions with other injuries in 2024 happened on interstates, more than on any other route category. Interstates also carried 32 serious injury collisions involving commercial vehicles. A truck crash that kills no one can still break bones or end a career. Neither Roper St. Francis Berkeley Hospital nor HCA Healthcare's Moncks Corner ER is among the trauma centers the state Department of Public Health lists. The weight of a claim rests on the medical records, which a lawyer reads in full before offering any view.

Heavy Trucks on US 52 and US 17A Near Moncks Corner and a Berkeley County Trucking Lawyer

US 52 meets US 17A at North Live Oak Drive in SCDOT's 2024 traffic file for Berkeley County. On that Moncks Corner street, the county's bond court sits at number 223 and a freestanding ER at number 401. Frost Law Group handles truck crashes along both routes, from Summerville and Goose Creek to the Williamsburg County line.

Traffic thins as each route runs north. SCDOT estimated 44,900 vehicles a day on US 52 just past St. James Avenue. The count fell to 20,000 approaching North Live Oak Drive and 4,800 near the Williamsburg line. US 17A dropped from 54,000 between the Dorchester line and I-26 to 6,700 near the Williamsburg line. Those counts give context, never a measure of one driver's care. SCDOT's file gives one total for all vehicles, so it does not say how many were trucks.

Truck Crashes Where US 52 and US 17A Meet at Live Oak Drive

SCDOT labels part of US 17A as North Highway 52 in its Berkeley file. Its busiest US 17A count north of Myers Road, 29,600 vehicles a day, sits between two US 52 junctions. A crash report from that stretch may name the road either way. Frost Law Group can match the officer's road label to SCDOT's route number before relying on any traffic figure. A tractor-trailer turning onto US 52 there can block the road, and the diagram must show exactly where.

Police Notice and Crash Reports Around Moncks Corner

When a crash hurts or kills anyone, Section 56-5-1260 says the driver must alert police right away. Within Moncks Corner or Goose Creek, that means the municipal police. Outside city and town limits, word goes to the Berkeley County Sheriff's Office or to the Highway Patrol office closest to the wreck. Jack C. Municipal Officers in Moncks Corner and Goose Creek

Moncks Corner's own officers worked 622 Berkeley collisions in 2024. Two of those were fatal, and 15 caused serious injuries. Goose Creek's department handled 1,284, more than any other municipal department in the county. A municipal officer's report may carry the truck supplement that SCDPS uses to count a commercial vehicle crash. Frost Law Group can check that the supplement exists and that its weight and carrier entries match the truck's paperwork.

Troopers on the Rural Stretches North of Moncks Corner

Troopers worked 3,468 of Berkeley's 6,536 collisions in 2024 and 31 of its 37 fatal crashes. Troop Six covers Berkeley through Post A, based at the Charleston Patrol Office in North Charleston. Once SCDMV has processed a trooper's report, it sells the official copy for a $10 fee per report. A requester can order it online, visit a branch, or mail in Form FR-50. That official copy carries more than the short form handed over at the roadside.

State Rules for In-State Trucks a Berkeley County Tractor Trailer Attorney Applies

Section 56-5-4240 takes the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and applies them to commercial vehicles that never leave South Carolina. The line is 26,001 pounds, counting actual weight or weight rating for a single truck or a combination. That number matters first, since a Berkeley gravel hauler can land on either side of it.

For trucks in interstate commerce, the federal definition in 49 CFR 390.5 starts lower, at 10,001 pounds. In 2024, collisions involving carriers operating only inside South Carolina numbered 413 statewide, with 25 fatal crashes and 28 deaths. Carriers working only in interstate commerce had 1,541 collisions and 64 fatal ones. Frost Law Group applies the same weight lines to truck crash claims in Summerville and across Berkeley. Which rules bound a given truck is a question its paperwork answers.

Trucks Under 26,001 Pounds That Stay in South Carolina

Subsection (B) of Section 56-5-4240 exempts in-state commercial vehicles under 26,001 pounds from the federal rules. A landscaping truck or a small dump body that never crosses the state line may fall in that group. Subsection (C) overrides the weight test in two cases. A vehicle designed or used to carry 16 or more people, counting the driver, stays covered at any weight. So does a truck hauling hazardous materials that must carry placards. Other law and SCDPS regulations may add exceptions, which Frost Law Group can check case by case.

Traffic Code Duties That Bind a Truck at Any Weight

Leaving the federal rules behind does not free a truck from the traffic code. Every truck on US 52 or I-26 owes the same Chapter 5 duties as a car. Some of those duties name trucks directly, which matters when the federal rules do not apply. Frost Law Group can build claims against exempt trucks on these statutes and use them against covered trucks as well.

Spacing Between Trucks Outside Business and Residence Districts

Section 56-5-1930's general rule bars tailgating, judged by speed, traffic volume and road condition. Its subsection (B) adds a rule for trucks traveling outside a business or residence district. When conditions permit, a truck following another truck must leave room for a passing vehicle to pull in safely. On US 52 north of Moncks Corner, a car passing two trucks may otherwise have nowhere to go.

Speed for Conditions With a Full Load

Officers wrote Driving Too Fast for Conditions on Berkeley reports 1,561 times in 2024, more than any other factor. Under Section 56-5-1520, a truck's speed must stay reasonable and prudent for actual conditions, even under the posted limit. The driver of a full trailer on a wet I-26 ramp still has to match speed to the pavement. A speed code on a report is one officer's judgment. Frost Law Group can test it against skid marks, damage and truck data.

Brakes, Lights and Steering Under Section 56-5-4410

Under Section 56-5-4410, nobody may drive or move a truck so unsafe that it endangers a person or property. The same section forbids driving without lights, brakes and steering in proper condition and adjustment. An owner who knowingly lets such a truck onto the highway breaks it too. That can reach the business that kept a worn-out dump truck in service, not only the driver behind the wheel.

Who Enforces the Truck Safety Rules on Berkeley Roads?

The State Transport Police Division of SCDPS does. Section 56-5-4010(B) gives it sole power in South Carolina to enforce the federal carrier regulations, hazardous material rules and size and weight limits. In Berkeley, it seldom writes the crash report. The 2024 Fact Book credits it with 2 of the county's 6,536 collision investigations. Frost Law Group can ask whether the division inspected the truck afterward, because any inspection would leave its own paperwork.

Load Securement and Gravel Truck Claims With a Berkeley County Truck Crash Lawyer

A fist-sized stone bounces off a dump truck's tailgate on US 17A and punches through the windshield behind it. South Carolina has a statute written for that moment. Under Section 56-5-4100, a truck's build or loading must stop cargo from dropping, sifting, leaking or escaping. Frost Law Group handles falling-load claims under its subsections, which reach the driver, loader, owner and carrier.

Statewide figures show how often these trucks turn up in serious wrecks. The Fact Book counted 315 dump-body trucks in South Carolina's 2024 commercial vehicle collisions, 10 of them in fatal crashes. Trucks with grain, chip or gravel bodies added 64, with 6 in fatal crashes. Construction and maintenance ranked second among the recorded uses of those commercial vehicles, behind property hauling.

Six Inches of Freeboard or a Secure Cover

Subsection (B) targets trucks hauling rock, gravel, stone and similar material that could blow, leak, sift or drop. At the loading point, the load must sit at least six inches under the top of all four walls. An uneven load must keep its sides six inches down, and its peak may not rise past the tops. Otherwise a tarp or other suitable cover must secure it, or the truck's body must keep it from escaping. The same subsection reaches trucks carrying garbage or waste to a disposal site.

The Loader's Duty to Sweep Loose Gravel From the Truck

Subsection (C) names two people, the loader of the vehicle and its driver. Before the truck enters a public highway, they must sweep loose gravel off its running boards, fenders and bumpers. The loader may work for a quarry, a sand pit or a building site rather than the trucking company. That wording can bring a second business into a claim when leftover stone flies off a bumper on I-26. For truck accident lawyers in Berkeley County, who loaded the truck, where and when, bears on which companies to name.

Fastened Loads and Covers After the 2024 Amendment

Act No. 202 of 2024 added subsection (D), effective May 21, 2024. It bars operating any vehicle unless the load and any covering are securely fastened. The fastening must keep both from working loose and from endangering other drivers. The same act repealed Section 56-5-4110, an older section titled "Loads and covers must be firmly attached." Older summaries of South Carolina law may still cite the repealed section. A flapping tarp or a snapped strap now falls under subsection (D) directly.

The $100 Fine, Cleanup Costs and a Separate Injury Claim

When fallen cargo endangers travel, subsection (E) requires the driver to make every reasonable effort to clear it at once. The driver also pays for the cleanup. If the driver does not act, SCDOT or any officer may remove cargo that blocks the road or endangers safety. The owner, driver or carrier then bears the reasonable costs. Violating subsections (B) through (E) is a misdemeanor carrying a $100 fine. The statute then says outright that it does not block a damages claim. The ticket and the injury claim run separately. Whether a violation helps prove fault depends on how the load failed, which a lawyer examines.

Log, Poultry and Paper Loads the Statute Excludes

Subsection (G) removes several cargoes from subsections (A) through (E). Exempt cargoes include seed cotton, soybeans, tobacco, poultry, livestock, feed grain, paper, forest products and textile products. A log truck shedding bark on US 52 falls outside the statute, so that claim needs another footing, such as ordinary negligence. The Fact Book counted 102 log-body trucks in the state's 2024 commercial vehicle collisions, 9 of them in fatal crashes.

Who Pays When a Rock From an Unknown Gravel Truck Cracks Your Windshield?

If you know which truck it was, the claim runs against its driver, its carrier and possibly the business that loaded it. If the truck keeps going, uninsured motorist coverage may apply under Section 38-77-170, rewritten in 2024. First, someone has to report the crash to police, and the report has to come within a reasonable time. Second, the claim needs physical contact with the truck, a signed affidavit from an outside witness or a recording that shows the truck did the harm. Your own carelessness in failing to note the truck's identity can defeat the claim. Spilled gravel can put a motorcycle rider down without any contact with the truck. Evidence Deadlines After a Berkeley County Semi Crash for a Trucking Accident Attorney

After a truck crash, Frost Law Group can write the carrier a demand to preserve its records. Federal rules set a minimum keeping period for each kind of file, and several run in months, not years. Tara L. Frost served as a Dorchester County Magistrate Judge until August 2025. Her years on that bench showed her how a court weighs the evidence placed before it.

Picture a tractor-trailer that rear-ends a sedan at the College Park Road exit. Its driver's logs, inspection reports, maintenance file and any post-crash drug test each sit on a separate federal clock. Exact dates turn on when the carrier made each record, so a lawyer counts forward from each record's date.

Post-Crash Alcohol and Drug Testing Under 49 CFR 382.303

The federal rule at 49 CFR 382.303 lists which public-road crashes require a trucking employer to test its driver. Its reach stops at drivers whose jobs call for a commercial driver's license. Not every crash triggers a test, and the testing windows close within hours. Frost Law Group can ask early whether a test happened, when it happened and who ran it.

Deaths, Injuries and the Citation Requirement

After a fatal crash, the carrier tests each of its surviving drivers who was doing safety-sensitive work, ticket or no ticket. Without a death, testing applies only if the truck driver receives a moving-violation citation. The crash must also have sent someone straight to treatment away from the scene. A tow-away with disabling damage works too. Police breath, blood or urine results can satisfy the rule if they meet testing standards and reach the carrier.

Two-Hour, Eight-Hour and 32-Hour Testing Windows

That citation triggers an alcohol test only if it comes within 8 hours, and a drug test only if it comes within 32. If an alcohol test does not happen within 2 hours, the carrier must write down why. Once 8 hours pass, the carrier gives up on the alcohol test and records the same explanation. Drug testing stops at 32 hours, again with a written reason. A separate rule, 49 CFR 382.209, keeps a driver who owes an alcohol test off alcohol for 8 hours or until the test, whichever is first.

How Long a Carrier Keeps Test Records

Retention periods for testing records come from 49 CFR 382.401, and they differ by result. The carrier keeps records tied to the collection process, including papers on decisions about post-accident tests, for at least two years. 49 CFR 382.405 limits release of those records but permits disclosure in civil actions under a separate federal rule.

Negative Results for One Year

The minimum for a negative or canceled drug test result is one year. The same minimum applies to an alcohol result under 0.02. After that year, the federal rule no longer requires the carrier to keep it. A clean result still shows when the test happened, which matters when the timing is in dispute. Frost Law Group can ask for these results by date.

Positive Results and Refusals for Five Years

An alcohol result of 0.02 or higher and a verified positive drug result carry a five-year minimum. So does paperwork showing a driver refused a required test. A refusal can matter in a crash case, and the record of it sits in the carrier's own file. Whether a result or refusal affects fault is a question the full record answers, not the test alone.

Logs, Inspection Reports and Repair Files

The federal minimum for hours records and their supporting documents is six months, under 49 CFR 395.8. Driver vehicle inspection reports carry a three-month minimum under 49 CFR 396.11. Maintenance records stay one year where the carrier houses or services the truck, plus six months after it leaves the carrier's control. Frost Law Group also handles hours-of-service and fatigue claims, which rest on those logs and the receipts behind them.

State Forms and Filing Deadlines After a Berkeley Wreck

South Carolina runs its own clocks. The FR-10 insurance form an officer hands out has a 15-day return window to SCDMV under Section 56-9-350. Under that statute, the driver or owner is the one who must return it. Most injury suits must reach the courthouse within three years, per Section 15-3-530. A county dump truck or an SCDOT maintenance truck brings in the Tort Claims Act instead. Section 15-78-110 usually allows two years to sue, measured from when the loss was found, or reasonably should have been. The window grows to three years only if the claimant first files a verified claim. Section 15-78-80(d) also caps the time for the agency to receive that claim at one year from the same point. Frost Law Group can identify the truck's owner from the registration, because who owns the truck decides which deadline controls.

What Should You Photograph on a Gravel Truck at the Scene?

Start with the load, if you can do it safely. Photograph the load's height against each wall, any tarp over it and the straps holding that tarp. Capture loose stone left on the bumpers and fenders, surfaces the loader and driver must clear under Section 56-5-4100. Then shoot the spill, your car's damage and any company name or numbers on the cab door. A lawyer can turn those photos into requests aimed at the carrier and the business that loaded the truck.
