How Growth Shapes the Work of Personal Injury Attorneys in Berkeley County

The 2020 Census counted 229,861 people in Berkeley County. By July 1, 2025, the Census Bureau estimated 274,666, an increase of about 19% in five years. At Frost Law Group, that growth changes where Berkeley claims start, which agency investigates them and where they can end up in court.

More residents did not bring a higher death rate in 2024. The 2024 Traffic Collision Fact Book counted 6,536 Berkeley collisions, including 37 fatal crashes that killed 37 people. Using its own population estimate, the Department of Public Safety put the county's motor vehicle death rate at 13.9 per 100,000 residents. That rate fell from 23.8 in 2023, and the county's fatal collision ranking eased from 5th of 46 counties to 9th. Among the eight counties where Frost Law Group takes injury cases, only Beaufort's 13.3 was lower.

Injuries tell a different story. Officers logged 83 serious injury collisions and 1,372 other injury collisions in Berkeley that year. Those crashes outnumbered the fatal ones nearly 40 to 1. Across all of the county's collisions, 103 people suffered serious injuries and 1,997 suffered other injuries.

Where Berkeley's New Residents Settled

Moncks Corner grew faster than any other municipality in the county. The Census Bureau estimated 13,440 residents there in mid-2020 and 20,612 on July 1, 2025, a rise of about 53%. Goose Creek went from 46,335 to 52,010 over the same span. Hanahan climbed from 20,580 to 22,415.

Most of the added population landed outside any town limit. Unincorporated Berkeley held 155,010 residents in the 2025 estimate, up from 128,374 in mid-2020, and that is more than half the county. It includes Census-designated places such as Sangaree, Pimlico and Pinopolis. Outside municipal limits, a wreck generally goes to the Highway Patrol or the sheriff, and that shapes the report you later receive.

More Drivers, Registrations and Miles Since 2023

SCDMV counted 197,824 licensed drivers in Berkeley County in 2024, up 6,101 from the year before. Vehicle registrations rose by 8,189 to 230,793. SCDOT figured that vehicles traveled about 2.34 billion miles on Berkeley roads in 2024, roughly 62 million more than in 2023.

The Fact Book also puts a dollar figure on a year of crashes. Its formula estimated $226,411,700 in economic loss for Berkeley County in 2024. That number is a public accounting estimate and says nothing about what one person's claim may be worth. A single claim rests on the client's own bills, pay records and treatment plan.

Does a Summerville Address Put a Crash in Berkeley County?

Not by itself. Summerville's town limits reach into Berkeley and Charleston counties as well as Dorchester. The Census Bureau estimated that 5,798 town residents lived on the Berkeley side in 2025, while most of Summerville sits in Dorchester County.

SCDOT's interchange diagrams also place I-26 Exits 194, 197, 199 and 203 in Berkeley. The county on the report matters, because the line affects which agency responds and which courthouses may hear the case.

Speed for Conditions, the Leading Crash Factor a Berkeley County Accident Lawyer Sees

A late-afternoon shower rolls across US 176, and the car ahead brakes for a turning truck. A driver still at the posted limit slides into its bumper. An officer may code that wreck as Driving Too Fast for Conditions. No factor appeared more often on Berkeley County reports in 2024.

The Fact Book counted 1,561 listings of that factor in Berkeley, ahead of 1,454 for Failed to Yield Right of Way. Dorchester and Charleston counties show the reverse order, with failure to yield on top. Each report has room for up to five factors, so these totals count entries rather than separate crashes. To Frost Law Group, the speed entry is the officer's opinion, and the physical evidence has to test it.

How the 1,561 Speed Entries Split by Severity

Most of those entries came from crashes with property damage only, 1,204 in all. Another 340 came from other injury collisions, 15 from serious injury collisions and 2 from fatal ones. That still leaves 357 speed listings tied to someone who was hurt.

Timing adds context. Berkeley's busiest crash window in 2024 ran from 3 PM to 6 PM, with 1,631 collisions. The most fatal crashes, 10 of the 37, fell between 6 PM and 9 PM. The overnight hours ran deadlier than their volume suggests, with 10 more fatal crashes among 578 collisions between midnight and 6 AM. Those patterns are background for personal injury attorneys in Berkeley County, not proof of what happened in any single wreck.

Failure to Yield Leads Among Injury Crashes

Speed tops the total count, but failure to yield appears more often on reports from crashes where people were hurt. Officers tied 416 failure-to-yield listings to other injury collisions, 21 to serious injury collisions and 3 to fatal ones. That makes 440 injury-related listings, against 357 for speed.

A driver who pulls onto US 52 from a side street without a safe gap can cause a broadside crash. The left-turn duty in Section 56-5-2320 and the stop and yield sign rules in Section 56-5-2330 may decide that kind of claim. Sight lines, signal timing and the angle of damage on each car usually supply the proof.

What Section 56-5-1520 Requires Under the Posted Limit

Section 56-5-1520 judges speed by the road and the weather, not only by the sign. It forbids any speed beyond what is reasonable and prudent for the road as it is, counting hazards present and hazards that may develop. The driver must also hold speed down enough to avoid a collision.

The statute calls for slower speeds when approaching an intersection, a curve, the top of a hill or a narrow bridge. Weather and pedestrians count as hazards too. Rain on the curve of an I-26 exit ramp can make a speed under the posted number unreasonable. Whether an officer's speed entry holds up depends on damage patterns, video and witness accounts, which a lawyer reviews before forming a view.

Rear Impacts and the Following-Distance Rule

Tailgating has its own statute. Section 56-5-1930 forbids trailing another vehicle closer than is reasonable and prudent, judged by speed, traffic and the condition of the road. The statute names no set number of car lengths or seconds. Officers listed Followed Too Closely 618 times on Berkeley reports in 2024, and 110 of those listings came from crashes with injuries.

A rear-impact claim may pair that rule with the speed statute when a driver closed fast on stopped traffic. The damage to both bumpers and any dashcam footage from either car can confirm or undercut the coded factors.

Run-Off-Road Entries on Berkeley's Fatal Crash Reports

Speed for conditions leads on volume, but a different entry shows up in the deadliest crashes. Officers listed Ran Off Road 635 times in Berkeley in 2024, and 14 of them appeared on fatal collision reports. The speed entry, by comparison, appeared on only 2.

When a single car leaves the road, the insurance picture changes. A hurt passenger may look to the driver's own liability policy. If another vehicle forced the driver off and kept going, Section 38-77-170 may open an uninsured motorist claim. Since its 2024 rewrite, that statute also accepts a recording of the crash, alongside physical contact or a signed witness affidavit.

Can Both Drivers Share Blame for Speed on a Wet Road?

Yes. Under South Carolina's modified comparative negligence rule, a jury weighs each person's share of fault. A plaintiff whose share tops 50% gets nothing, and any lesser share shrinks the award by the same percentage. Cases with several defendants add a second rule, one Section 15-38-15 has carried since 2005. A defendant below 50% at fault generally pays only its own share. Act No. 42 changed something else for claims arising after January 1, 2026. It lets the jury weigh the fault of a driver nobody sued, when a defendant names that driver in time. How a jury would divide fault turns on the evidence in that crash, and no lawyer can promise a split.

Merge and Lane-Change Crashes Under Section 56-5-1900

Interchanges force lane changes, and South Carolina has a statute for them. Section 56-5-1900 requires a driver on a road with marked lanes to stay within a single lane as nearly as practicable. The driver may not leave that lane until first making sure the move can be made safely. Officers listed Improper Lane Usage or Change 927 times on Berkeley reports in 2024, third among all factors.

Most of those listings came from crashes without injuries. Still, 96 came from other injury crashes, 5 from serious injury crashes and 1 from a fatal crash. When a merge goes wrong near an exit, turn signals, mirror sight lines and dashcam video tend to settle who moved first.

Proof That Fades After an Interchange Wreck

Once the lanes reopen, the scene itself is gone. What remains is the officer's diagram, photos, the damage to each vehicle, witness names and any video from dashcams or nearby businesses. Business cameras may record over old footage on a routine cycle, so asking early, in writing, matters. Photos taken before repairs preserve the damage pattern. Witness names on the report help only if someone reaches those witnesses while memories are fresh.

What if No Officer Came to Your Berkeley Crash?

Some crashes never get an officer at the scene, and Section 56-5-1270 covers that gap. Form FR-309 serves that purpose and is due within 15 days of the crash. Parking lots raise a separate point, because the Fact Book leaves out crashes on private property. Children Hurt in Berkeley Crashes

Requesting Your Chart Under the Federal Access Rule

A federal privacy regulation, 45 CFR 164.524, gives patients a right to copies of their own medical records. The hospital must act on a request within 30 days of receiving it. It may take one extension of up to 30 more days, but only with written notice. The rule permits a reasonable, cost-based fee for copying labor, supplies and postage.

A request for complete records, including imaging reports and nursing notes, beats a one-page summary. How strongly those records support a claim depends on what they say, and a lawyer reads them in full before offering any estimate.

Can a Berkeley Crash Lawsuit Be Filed Outside Moncks Corner?

Sometimes. Venue, the county where a civil case is tried, is governed by Section 15-7-30. Against a South Carolina resident, the suit may go to the county that was the driver's home when the claim arose. A second option is the county where the bulk of the careless driving happened. With several defendants, a county proper for any one of them can host the whole case.

Take a driver from the Dorchester side of Summerville who causes a wreck at the Jedburg Road interchange. The case could be filed in Berkeley, in the Ninth Circuit, or in Dorchester, in the First Circuit. Frost Law Group handles Dorchester County injury claims as well and can talk through that choice with the client. If the at-fault driver lives out of state, the statute points to the county of the crash or the county where the injured person lives.

Deadlines That Run After a Berkeley Wreck

Several filing clocks can run at once. The one that controls depends on who caused the crash, whether a government vehicle was involved and whether someone died. A claim filed after its deadline can be dismissed however clear the fault. An insurer's open claim file generally does not pause any of those clocks. A lawyer can identify each deadline that may apply and track it until the case is filed or resolved.

Section 15-3-530 and the Date a Claim Accrues

Three years is the general limit for an injury suit under Section 15-3-530. The Section 15-3-535 clock starts on the date the hurt person discovered the claim or, using reasonable diligence, could have. In a crash case, that date usually matches the day of the collision itself.

Two Years When the County or a Town Is Responsible

A crash involving a Berkeley County vehicle or a town police car falls under the Tort Claims Act. Its clock, in Section 15-78-110, gives two years from the day the injured person discovered the harm, or reasonably could have. Filing a verified claim with the responsible agency first buys a third year. Section 15-78-80(d) sets a catch, though, since that claim must arrive within one year of discovery. Section 15-78-120 limits the award to $300,000 for one person, with a $600,000 ceiling for a single occurrence. It allows no punitive damages, the extra award meant to punish a wrongdoer.

Wrongful Death Estates in the Berkeley County Probate Court

A wrongful death action must be filed no later than three years after the death, under Section 15-3-530(6). The estate's personal representative files it on behalf of the family. Section 62-3-201 opens the estate in the county of the person's home at death. For a Berkeley resident, that means the probate court at 300-B California Avenue, and Section 15-51-41 requires court approval of any settlement.

Magistrates, the $7,500 Limit and a Civil Injury Claim

Berkeley's magistrate offices include a Central Criminal/Traffic court at 223 North Live Oak Drive in Moncks Corner, where the county's bond court also sits. Others sit at 653 Red Bank Road in Goose Creek and 137 Hood Street in St. Stephen. A traffic ticket from the crash moves through magistrate or municipal court, apart from any injury claim. Section 22-3-10 caps a magistrate's civil cases at $7,500 in controversy, so larger injury claims are filed in Common Pleas.

Car, Truck, and Motorcycle Crash Claims Our Berkeley County Lawyers Take On

No South Carolina auto policy may leave out uninsured motorist coverage, a rule set in Section 38-77-150. Liability minimums under Section 38-77-140 are 25/50/25. That shorthand means $25,000 per person and $50,000 per crash for bodily injury, plus $25,000 for property damage.

The state minimums govern a sedan on US 52 or a motorcycle on US 176. An interstate for-hire tractor-trailer on I-26 must carry a far higher federal minimum instead. Each vehicle type brings its own statutes, records and insurance layers. US routes alone logged 2,153 Berkeley collisions in 2024, with 16 of the county's 37 fatal crashes.

Car and SUV Collisions on Berkeley Roads

Frost Law Group's work on Berkeley car crash claims starts with every policy that might pay. Section 38-77-160 obliges each auto insurer to offer underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage, though buying it is optional. When the other driver's liability limits run out, UIM may pay the difference up to your own UIM limits. Under Section 38-77-250, the at-fault driver's insurer must disclose its limits within 30 days of a qualifying sworn request from the claimant's lawyer.

Impaired drivers add another layer. Officers reported 183 non-fatal DUI collisions in Berkeley in 2024, and NHTSA estimated 16 alcohol-impaired driving deaths in the county that year. If alcohol, or drugs not taken as lawfully prescribed, substantially impaired the defendant's judgment, Section 15-32-530 lifts the ordinary ceiling on punitive damages. Those damages are never automatic, and a court decides whether the proof supports them.

Tractor-Trailer and Heavy Truck Collisions

Frost Law Group takes on truck and tractor-trailer claims from I-26, US 52 and US 176. A truck case may involve a carrier, a driver, a cargo owner and more than one insurer. Sorting out who controlled the truck and its load is part of building the claim. Federal rules let a carrier discard hours-of-service records after six months, which makes an early preservation letter worth sending.

Which Safety Rules Reach the Truck

Whether the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations apply depends on the truck's weight and where it travels. Those rules limit driving hours and set standards for inspections, maintenance and driver files. A truck outside their reach must still follow South Carolina's traffic code, including its speed and following-distance statutes.

Interstate Carriers at 10,001 Pounds

For a truck in interstate commerce, the federal rules generally start at a 10,001-pound weight rating under 49 CFR 390.5. A for-hire carrier hauling ordinary freight across state lines needs at least $750,000 in liability coverage under 49 CFR 387.9. That sum is a floor, not a cap.

In-State Haulers at 26,001 Pounds

South Carolina applies the federal rules to trucks that never leave the state at 26,001 pounds or more, under Section 56-5-4240. Lighter in-state trucks generally fall outside those rules, with exceptions for vehicles built for 16 or more passengers and for placarded hazardous cargo. The State Transport Police, part of the SC Department of Public Safety, enforces those rules in South Carolina.

Loose Loads on US 52 and US 176

Picture gravel blowing off an uncovered dump truck onto US 176. South Carolina's load statute, Section 56-5-4100, bars driving a vehicle loaded so its cargo can drop, sift, leak or escape. Since 2024, it also requires any load and any covering to be securely fastened. The statute says it does not bar a damages claim, though it exempts some cargo, such as forest products and livestock.

Motorcycle Crashes in Berkeley County

Officers recorded 114 motorcyclist victims in Berkeley crashes in 2024. Four died, and 14 suffered suspected serious injuries. Riders and passengers hurt on Berkeley roads can turn to Frost Law Group for motorcycle injury claims, which start with South Carolina's lane rules.

Section 56-5-3640 gives a motorcycle a full lane of its own and forbids other drivers from squeezing it out. The same section makes lane splitting illegal. Section 56-5-3660 requires a helmet only for riders younger than 21, so older riders have no statutory duty to wear one. In South Carolina's multi-vehicle motorcycle crashes in 2024, officers listed the other vehicle's driver as contributing 56.4% of the time. That figure does not decide any one claim, and a rider's own share of fault still counts.
