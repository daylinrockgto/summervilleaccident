Some people live for a while after a crash, for a few minutes at the roadside or for days in a hospital bed. What they went through in that time, and what their care cost, does not vanish when they die. Survival action lawyers in Summerville pursue those losses for the person's estate under a South Carolina statute that keeps the injured person's own claim alive. The family's grief belongs to a separate claim, and the law keeps the two apart.

Section 15-5-90, in Title 15, Chapter 5 of the South Carolina Code, keeps alive claims for injuries to the person and to personal property. The executor or administrator named by a probate court, called the personal representative, is the only one who can bring that claim. Any money it produces belongs to the estate. After the estate's debts, it passes under the will or the Probate Code. Until the estate opens, hold on to every bill and record that arrives, and leave any insurer's release unsigned.

What Survival Action Lawyers in Summerville Recover Under Section 15-5-90

Section 15-5-90 uses plain words. Causes of action for "any and all injuries to the person or to personal property" survive the death of the person who held them. Frost Law Group reads that sentence as two lists, one for harm to the body and one for harm to things the person owned. Each list becomes part of the estate's claim when the evidence supports it.

In a 2012 decision, Carson v. CSX Transportation, the Supreme Court of South Carolina described how the claim works. The personal representative "stands in the shoes" of the person who died. That representative may bring any claim the person could have brought in life. A jury setting survival damages looks at what the estate is entitled to, not at who will inherit. What any survival claim is worth depends on its proof, and no lawyer can promise a figure.

Pain, Distress and Hospital Bills the Estate Can Claim

South Carolina's Court of Appeals listed the damages a survival action can include in two opinions filed in 2000, Scott v. Porter and Welch v. Epstein. Both name medical, surgical and hospital bills. Both also name conscious pain, suffering and mental distress. A lawyer measures each item across the time between the injury and the death.

When a person lives through a severe wreck, those same losses form his or her own claim. Frost Law Group handles catastrophic injury claims for survivors, and an estate carries that claim forward if the person later dies.

The Wrecked Vehicle and Other Belongings

The statute's second list reaches personal property. Picture a pickup titled to the person who died, totaled on Orangeburg Road with a phone and a toolbox in the cab. The claim for that damage belongs to the estate, and the personal representative presents it with the injury claim. The personal representative checks the title and any loan papers first, since a co-owner or a lender may hold part of the interest.

Claims Against the Estate of an At-Fault Driver Who Died

Section 15-5-90 runs in both directions. Claims survive "both to and against" the representative of a person who has died. So when the driver who caused a crash also dies, the injured person's claim can proceed against that driver's estate. Frost Law Group looks up whether a probate court has opened that estate, and in which county, before any claim goes out.

The Probate Code's deadlines for presenting claims against an estate have an exception here. Section 62-3-803(d)(2) says those limits do not cut off a proceeding to establish the dead driver's liability, up to the limits of that driver's liability insurance. Other deadlines still matter, including the three years Section 15-3-530(5) allows for injury claims, so a lawyer checks each one against the dates in the file.

Is There a Survival Claim When Death Came at the Scene?

There can be, though what it covers changes. Pain counts only if the person felt it, so the estate needs evidence of awareness after the impact. An instant death may leave none. The estate can still claim the property damage under Section 15-5-90. Section 15-5-100 also lets it seek reasonable funeral expenses in the survival action, as long as the wrongful death case does not also claim them. Whether a death was truly instant is itself a question of evidence, and a lawyer tests it against the records and witness accounts.

Proving Conscious Pain and Suffering With a Summerville Survival Claim Attorney

In 2004, a train struck a van at a railroad crossing in Denmark, South Carolina, the crash behind Carson. The man riding in the back landed in brush beside the tracks and died there. His mother was driving the van. A driver who saw the collision heard him calling for her. Another witness heard moaning. A volunteer firefighter who responded said he could tell the man was in a lot of pain.

During deliberations, the jury asked whether finding the mother more than half at fault meant it need not award anything for conscious pain. In reply, the judge said the award turned on whether the man suffered conscious pain. The jury still awarded nothing on the survival claim, despite that testimony and the estate's funeral and burial receipts. The Supreme Court called the zero award legally incorrect and sent the survival action back for a new trial.

For survival action lawyers in Summerville, the same kinds of proof come together in the order events happened, from the roadside to the hospital. Tara L. Frost spent time on the bench as a Dorchester County Magistrate Judge. Her years there showed her how a courtroom weighs a witness's account, the preparation behind it and the witness's credibility. How much weight a jury gives any account varies, so a lawyer tests each one first.

Witnesses, Firefighters and Officers at the Roadside

People at the scene see the injured person before any doctor does. A driver who stops, a firefighter and an officer can each say whether the person spoke, moved or cried out. Frost Law Group can take their names from the collision report and ask what they saw, since recall of words and sounds fades with time. The Summerville Police Department's Traffic Safety Unit counts officers with training in technical collision investigation and reconstruction. Their work can help place each witness account on a timeline.

Hospital Charts That Record What the Patient Felt

Medical records can prove suffering when no witness heard a word. In Scott v. Porter, nurses' charts described a toddler as irritable, restless in sleep, vomiting and pulling at his monitors. A nurse also heard him say he did not feel good and ask his mother to hold him. The court treated that as competent evidence of suffering. It added that a jury may infer conscious pain from proof like it.

Ambulance Crew Notes on the Way to the Hospital

An ambulance crew's patient care report may hold the first medical description of the person after the crash. It can record whether the person answered questions, complained of pain or went quiet during the ride. The ambulance service keeps that report apart from the hospital chart, so Frost Law Group can request it separately.

Emergency Department and Transfer Hospital Charts

Every emergency department that treated the person keeps its own chart, and a transfer to a second hospital adds another. Each chart usually carries a time stamp on every entry. Those stamps let the estate line up the chart with the crash report and the ambulance notes. Two kinds of entries tend to speak to awareness.

Triage and Nursing Entries

Triage notes record how the patient looked and acted on arrival. Nursing entries then follow the patient through the shift. Some may note that the person was calling out, asking for family or pulling at lines. Read in time order against the crash report, those entries can show when the person was last aware.

Physician Orders for Pain Medicine

An order for pain medicine can show that a doctor believed the patient was in pain at that moment. A later note on how the patient responded to the dose can add to that record. Read together, the order and the nearest nursing note show what the staff saw.

A Physician's Account of What the Body Went Through

Scott also relied on a physician who described how the child's brain swelled until his breathing stopped. The court said that testimony let the jury infer pain that came before the coma. After a crash, a treating physician or other medical witness can walk through a similar sequence for a head, chest or internal injury. Whether that account supports conscious pain turns on the injury and the chart, which a lawyer reviews first.

What if the Person Never Regained Consciousness?

The survival claim can still exist, but its content narrows. In Welch v. Epstein, a patient fell into a coma he never came out of. The trial judge allowed survival damages only for the medical bills from the point his breathing stopped until his death. The jury's survival award matched those bills. Pain before a coma can still count, as Scott shows, so Frost Law Group looks closely at every stretch of time when the person was awake.

Medical Bills and Funeral Costs a Summerville Survival Claim Lawyer Documents

An itemized hospital statement lists every charge by date and service. A summary bill may show little more than a balance. The itemized version lets the estate show which care fell between the crash and the death. Frost Law Group can request one from each provider and match every line to a chart entry. Keeping those pairs together lets an insurer see the record behind each charge.

Picture a man hurt on Dorchester Road who goes by ambulance to Trident Medical Center and dies there after surgery. The ambulance service, the hospital, the surgeons and the radiologists may each send a separate bill. Medicaid or Medicare may have paid some of those charges already. Which charges a court or insurer accepts depends on the records, and no lawyer can predict that total in advance.

Bills Medicaid or Medicare Already Paid

Section 43-7-420 covers care Medicaid paid for. It treats the right to recover those payments from a liable party as assigned to the state Medicaid agency, the Department of Health and Human Services. When Medicare pays for crash care, it pays conditionally. Federal law then entitles the program to reimbursement from the settlement, judgment or award that follows. Frost Law Group identifies both programs' claims early, since either one can reduce what the estate keeps.

Funeral and Burial Receipts Under Section 15-5-100

Section 15-5-100 allows reasonable funeral expenses in either the survival action or the wrongful death action, but only in one of them. In Carson, the estate presented its funeral and burial receipts in the survival action. Frost Law Group collects the funeral home contract, the burial or cremation charges and the cemetery receipts, along with proof of who paid each one. A family paying for a headstone or a reception should ask before assuming those costs count.

The choice of claim changes who receives the money. Funeral costs placed in the survival action go to the estate. The same costs placed in the wrongful death action go to the statutory beneficiaries. The right choice depends on the estate's debts and on who inherits under the will, and a lawyer weighs both before filing.

How a Summerville Estate Claim Lawyer Handles Survival Damages Through Probate

In St. George, the county seat, the South Carolina Judicial Branch directory lists the Dorchester County Probate Court office at 5200 East Jim Bilton Boulevard. Estates of Dorchester County residents open in that probate court. No survival claim can move forward until a probate court appoints a personal representative, because Carson says only the representative may file it. Frost Law Group can work with the relative who will serve, so that the survival claim is ready once letters issue.

Title 62, Chapter 3 of the South Carolina Code sets out each estate step, from the application to the final distribution. The pace of any estate varies with its debts and its family. A lawyer plans the survival claim around whatever deadlines apply.

The Representative Steps Into the Person's Place in Court

Section 62-3-703(c) gives an appointed representative the same standing to sue on surviving claims that the person held just before death. Section 62-3-715(20) then allows the representative to prosecute claims that protect the estate. Under Section 62-3-703(a), the representative is also a fiduciary, meaning someone bound to act for the benefit of others. The relative who serves makes each decision with heirs, creditors and will beneficiaries in mind, since all of them hold an interest.

Court Approval Before the Estate Settles

Section 62-3-715(24) lets a personal representative compromise a claim for pain and suffering, or any claim that survives to the estate. It adds a condition, which is the approval of the probate court or the circuit court. Section 15-51-41 applies the same approval rule to both death claims, and it lists a federal district court as a third option. Frost Law Group can prepare the petition, and approval rests with the court alone.

Creditor Claims and the Order an Estate Pays Them

A survival recovery belongs to the estate, and Section 15-51-42(B) has the settlement petition list the appropriate creditors and the amounts they claim. Once appointed, a representative publishes a notice to creditors once a week for three successive weeks under Section 62-3-801. Creditors then have eight months from the first notice to present claims. Section 62-3-803 generally bars claims that arose before the death unless presented within one year after it, or sooner if the notice period ends first. How those rules touch a given recovery depends on the estate's debts, which a lawyer reviews line by line.

Funeral and Administration Costs in the First Class

Section 62-3-805 ranks claims when an estate cannot pay all of them in full. The first class holds the costs and expenses of administering the estate and reasonable funeral expenses. In that situation, an unpaid funeral home bill ranks ahead of taxes, medical debts and ordinary accounts. Frost Law Group checks who paid the funeral home before the estate accepts any settlement.

Last-Illness Medical Bills in the Third Class

The third class covers reasonable and necessary medical, hospital and personal care expenses of the person's last illness. It also reaches pay for people who attended the person before death. Care between a fatal crash and the death may fit that description. The same hospital charge can then appear twice, once as survival damages sought from the at-fault driver and once as a debt the estate owes. The fourth class names medical assistance paid under the state's Medicaid plan, among debts other state laws prefer.

Who Inherits Survival Money After the Debts Are Paid

Section 62-3-703(a) directs the representative to settle and distribute the estate under a probated will and the Probate Code. Survival money follows that route once the estate's claims are resolved. Picture a widower who left a will giving everything to his brother. The survival recovery would pass to that brother, while his children would stand first for the wrongful death recovery. The two recoveries can reach different people, so the split matters early.

With no will, the intestacy rules in the Probate Code take over. Section 62-2-102 gives a surviving spouse the entire estate if the person left no descendants, and half if descendants survive. Section 62-2-103 sends the rest, or everything when no spouse survives, to the descendants. If there are none, the parents come next. Frost Law Group works through that order with the family, since the survival money follows it after the estate's claims.

When a Summerville Survival Action Attorney Files Alongside a Wrongful Death Claim

Officers recorded 53 collisions in 2024 where Dorchester Road meets Old Trolley Road. The SCDPS Fact Book, which logs it by route number, ranks that intersection second in Dorchester County. Picture a driver hit there who dies at the hospital the following evening. One personal representative can then bring two claims against the at-fault driver. The survival claim carries what the driver suffered and what the care cost. The family's own losses belong to a separate claim under Section 15-51-10.

Carson shows how the two claims can proceed side by side. The personal representative filed the wrongful death action first and the survival action later, and the court consolidated them for trial. When the facts support each, survival action lawyers in Summerville generally file both. The same witnesses, crash report and insurance policies drive both claims. Whether one claim, the other or both fit a given death depends on the medical timeline, which a lawyer confirms before filing.

Car and Truck Crashes That Lead to Both Claims

A passenger car wreck and a tractor-trailer crash raise different questions about fault, yet the survival claim works the same way in each. Frost Law Group handles wrongful death cases from car wrecks and fatal tractor-trailer crash cases. In either one, the firm adds the survival claim when the person lived after the impact. The proof of that interval comes from the same places, including witnesses, ambulance notes and hospital charts.

Can an Estate Have a Survival Claim but No Wrongful Death Claim?

Yes. Section 15-51-10 requires that the wrongful act, neglect or default of another caused the death. Suppose a retiree hurt in a rear-end crash on North Main Street later dies of an unrelated illness. His own injury claim survives to his estate under Section 15-5-90. His death, though, may not support a wrongful death claim. The medical cause of death decides that question, and a lawyer reviews it with the treating physicians' records.

Do the Two Claims Share One Filing Deadline?

They can differ. Section 15-3-530(6) runs the three-year wrongful death period from the date of death. The survival claim belonged to the injured person first, and Section 15-3-530(5) gives injury claims three years. Section 62-3-109 pauses an unexpired claim that belonged to the person during the eight months that follow the death. Frost Law Group works from the earliest date that could apply, since the exact deadline turns on the crash date, the death date and the defendant.
