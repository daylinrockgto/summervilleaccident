Do You Have a Truck Accident Case?

A crash with a commercial truck is not a bigger car accident. Different rules, different records and a different insurer are involved.

1

You were hurt

Injuries, medical bills, missed work, or pain that showed up a few days later.

2

The driver or the carrier was at fault

Even partly. In South Carolina you can still recover if you were 50% or less at fault.4 A carrier that employs or assigns the driver can answer for the driver's conduct.8

3

There is a commercial policy to pay

A for-hire carrier moving general freight between states must carry at least $750,000 in liability coverage, far more than a typical auto policy.7

Records That Disappear If Nobody Asks

The carrier's own records usually decide a truck case, and federal rules let the carrier discard many of them within months.

1

Driver hours and duty-status logs

A carrier must keep each driver's records of duty status, and the documents that support them, for at least six months.6 Six months is a minimum, not a promise, so we ask the carrier in writing to keep them.

2

Inspection, maintenance and driver files

Pre-trip inspection reports, maintenance records, the driver's qualification file and drug and alcohol testing records each have their own retention clock.

3

The truck's own data

Electronic logging devices, engine control modules and dash cameras record speed, braking and hours. A preservation letter goes out early so none of it is overwritten.

What to Do After a Truck Crash in {{PLACE}}

Short on time? Do these, then call us. We handle the rest.

  1. Call 911 and get checked out. Go to the emergency room or urgent care, even if you feel okay.
  2. Photograph the truck. The cab, the trailer, the USDOT number on the door, the plates and the scene.
  3. Get the crash report. The investigating agency files it with the state. We can obtain it for you.
  4. Don't give a recorded statement to the carrier's insurance company. Their adjusters often call within hours.
  5. Keep every bill and receipt, and track the days of work you miss.
  6. Talk to a lawyer before you sign anything. A release ends your claim, and the carrier's records start disappearing in months.

What You May Recover

Every case is different. These are the damages South Carolina injury claims commonly include.

Medical bills

ER, surgery, therapy and the care you will need later.

Lost wages

Time missed from work, and lost earning ability if you can't go back.

Pain & suffering

Physical pain and the toll on your daily life.

Permanent injury & scarring

Lasting impairment or disfigurement.

Property damage

Repair or replacement of your vehicle, plus a rental.

Wrongful death

Damages for families who lost a loved one.
