Types of Cases Our Summerville Personal Injury Attorneys Represent

Most injury claims in South Carolina turn on two questions. Who was careless, and what did that carelessness cost you? Frost Law Group handles personal injury cases that begin with a highway wreck, a slip in a grocery aisle or a dog attack. The type of case decides which laws apply and which insurance policies may pay.

Fault does not have to be all or nothing. South Carolina uses a rule called modified comparative negligence, which compares your share of the blame with the other side's. If a jury finds you partly at fault, any award shrinks by your percentage, and you recover nothing once your share passes 50%. How the blame splits depends on the evidence. Tara and Jack, the Summerville personal injury attorneys at Frost Law Group, review that evidence before they give an opinion on fault.

Lawyers use the word damages for the money a claim seeks. Economic damages cover losses with a receipt, such as medical bills, lost pay and future care a physician documents. Noneconomic damages cover losses no receipt can show, such as pain and the activities you can no longer enjoy. Which damages a claim supports depends on the injuries, the proof and the coverage, and no lawyer can promise an amount.

Motor Vehicle Accident Injuries

South Carolina recorded 143,801 traffic collisions in 2024, and those wrecks killed 1,040 people. The figures come from the Department of Public Safety's yearly Traffic Collision Fact Book. Section 38-77-140 sets the minimum coverage for every South Carolina auto policy. In shorthand, that minimum reads 25/50/25. It means $25,000 of injury coverage for one person, $50,000 when a crash injures several people and $25,000 for property damage.

Every policy must also include uninsured motorist coverage, which steps in when the driver at fault carries no insurance. Insurers must offer underinsured motorist coverage as well. It is optional, and it can help when the other driver's coverage is too small for the losses. South Carolina requires no personal injury protection, the coverage that pays your own bills regardless of fault. That is why it is not a no-fault state.

After a wreck that injures or kills someone, Section 56-5-1260 requires the drivers to notify police right away. The officer's report then goes to SCDMV, which keeps the official copy. SCDMV describes that copy as more detailed than the paperwork drivers get at the scene, and you or a lawyer can order it.

A 2024 rewrite of Section 38-77-170 changed what can support a hit-and-run claim. Before that change, the statute accepted only physical contact or a witness who signed an affidavit. Now video showing that an unknown car caused the crash can support a claim on your own uninsured motorist coverage. In car and SUV collision claims, Frost Law Group can gather the fault evidence and look for every policy that might pay.

Coverage on an Uber driver's car rises in steps, and Section 58-23-1630 sets the minimum for each one. While the driver waits for a request with the app on, liability coverage must reach $50,000 per person and $100,000 per incident. Property damage coverage must reach $50,000.

Once the driver accepts a ride, the floor jumps to $1,000,000 for death, injury and property damage combined. That higher limit holds until the last rider steps out. Lyft Accident Injury Attorneys in Summerville, South Carolina

The same coverage steps apply to Lyft drivers, and two sources can show which step applied at the crash. Slip and Fall Lawyers

A store does not automatically owe you for every spill on its floor. The South Carolina Supreme Court restated the test in Wintersteen v. Food Lion in 2001. An injured shopper must show the store put the hazard there. If not, the shopper must show the store knew about the hazard, or should have found it, and failed to fix it.

When a store should have found a hazard, lawyers say it had constructive notice. A store's own inspection logs can help prove it. Your reason for being there matters too, since an owner owes a customer more care than a social guest.

Tenants can also rely on Section 27-40-440, which requires landlords to keep common areas reasonably safe. Frost Law Group takes slip, trip and fall claims from stores, restaurants and apartment complexes around Summerville. The South Carolina Tort Claims Act covers a fall in a public park or a government building, and it sets different deadlines. For a park, the claim also needs proof that the agency actually knew about the defect and left it unrepaired for an unreasonable time.

Dog Bite Attorneys in Summerville, South Carolina

South Carolina has no "one free bite" rule, so a dog does not need a history of biting before its owner can be liable. A dog's owner is liable under Section 47-3-110 when the dog bites or attacks, and so is anyone caring for it. The statute protects a person in a public place and a person legally on private land, including the owner's own yard.

The South Carolina Supreme Court says the statute imposes strict liability. That means a bite victim does not have to show the owner was careless or knew the dog was dangerous. The statute does not protect a trespasser, or someone who provoked or harassed the dog into attacking.

Frost Law Group represents adults and children in dog bite and dog attack claims. By the end of the next working day, a doctor who treats a bite must report it to the county health department. A claim can start with that report and the medical records.

Wrongful Death Accident Injury Attorneys in Summerville, South Carolina

Traffic crashes killed 1,040 people in South Carolina in 2024, including 37 in Dorchester County. When a careless or reckless driver causes a death, Section 15-51-10 gives the family a claim for its own losses. That claim exists only if the person who died would have had an injury claim of his or her own.

A personal representative brings the case. That is the executor or administrator the probate court appoints. Consider a head-on crash on Ashley River Road that kills a father of two. Section 15-51-20 puts his wife and children first in line to share any recovery.

A separate survival claim under Section 15-5-90 belongs to his estate. It covers his own losses before death, such as conscious pain and suffering. Frost Law Group represents surviving families in wrongful death claims after a fatal crash, including crashes involving a drunk driver. The personal representative generally has three years after the death to file the wrongful death suit.
