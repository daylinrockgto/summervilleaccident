A City or County Vehicle in the Crash

In a pocket neighborhood, the vehicle that hit you may belong to the city, the county or the state. Each counts as a governmental entity for purposes of the South Carolina Tort Claims Act, and that law runs on its own clock. The right defendant depends on which public body owns the vehicle or maintains the road. A city garbage truck, a sheriff's cruiser and a state work truck can each point to a different public body.

Deadlines for Suing a Public Body

The Act sets a shorter default window than an ordinary injury suit, with one way to extend it. That extension comes with a receipt deadline of its own, and missing it keeps the shorter window in force. Frost Law Group can calendar both dates once it identifies a public defendant.

Two Years Without a Verified Claim

Section 15-78-110 sets two years as the base limit for suing. The count starts on the date you discovered the loss or could reasonably have found it. That is the rule when nobody filed a claim with the agency first. Section 15-3-40, the extension for injured minors, also applies to claims under this Act.

Three Years With a Verified Claim

Filing a verified claim with the agency, and doing it before suing, opens a third year. The catch, found in Section 15-78-80(d), is timing. The agency must be holding the claim no later than twelve months after that same discovery date. File it late, and the two-year rule stays in place.

Road Defects and Notice Under the Act

Section 15-78-60(15) shields public bodies from claims over highway design. A third party's defect becomes the responsible body's problem only after that body knew of it, actually or constructively. Even then, it must have let an unreasonable stretch of time pass without a fix. A complaint log or a work order can help show when the public body learned of the defect.

Medical Payments Coverage Under Section 38-77-144

No South Carolina law requires personal injury protection, often called PIP. Med pay, short for medical payments coverage, is an optional add-on. Under Section 38-77-144, med pay cannot be assigned. It also carries no subrogation right, so your insurer cannot claim repayment of it from your injury recovery. Nor is it subject to setoff, which means no one may subtract it from what another insurer owes you. Section 38-77-260 adds a protection. An insurer paying med pay cannot get a release or repayment agreement from you unless it first delivers a disclosure statement and waits at least three days.

Health Plan Repayment Under Section 38-71-190

Section 38-71-190 lets a health policy written in South Carolina carry a subrogation clause. That clause reaches no further than what the plan already paid for injuries the at-fault driver caused. On the insured's petition, the Director of Insurance can refuse the repayment when it would be inequitable and unjust. Attorneys' fees and costs come out of whatever the insurer recovers, and the statute puts that cost on the insurer.

Does the Property Damage Deadline Match the Injury Deadline?
