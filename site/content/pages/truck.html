Your Own Share Under Modified Comparative Negligence

The South Carolina Supreme Court restated the state's comparative negligence rule in Davenport v. Cotton Hope Plantation in 1998. An injured person may recover as long as that person's own negligence does not exceed the defendant's. The jury assigns each party a share of fault and trims the award by the injured person's share. At exactly half, a reduced recovery remains possible, and past half, recovery ends.

One truck-specific wrinkle sits in Section 56-5-1930, the following-distance statute. It does not apply to a trailing truck, subject to the federal rules, in a line of trucks using cooperative adaptive cruise control. The same exception covers other automated driving technology. Whether a truck was running that way is a factual question to check before arguing distance.

Act No. 42 and the 50% Line Between Defendants

Act No. 42 of 2025 rewrote how South Carolina divides payment among defendants. The Act covers only claims whose cause of action arose or accrued after January 1, 2026. Joint and several liability lets a plaintiff collect an entire judgment from any one responsible defendant. The 50% line existed before the Act, which changed whose fault counts toward it and which exceptions remove its protection. Truck cases can involve several defendants at once, so the change can matter more in them than in a two-car crash.

Defendants Under 50% Pay Only Their Share

Since the 2005 version of Section 15-38-15, any defendant found less than 50% at fault has owed only that portion of the damages.
